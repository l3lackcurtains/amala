## Key rules and strategy from videos

### 1. Adding to a trade in drawdown — the exact DCA strategy (from "Adding To A Trade (When Your In Drawdown)")
- Working timeframe: the 5-minute chart on Nasdaq futures.
- THE SETUP: a bull flag forms. Long around the midpoint of the candle at ~31,358; stop at the Asia low ~31,311; target the Asia high 31,411 — about a one-to-one. Price pushes above VWAP (good sign), then rolls over, closes below everything, and makes a lower low.
- "This is the moment most people panic. Lucas adds."
- ADD POINT 1 — THE NEW LOW: if the broader trend is clearly up and there's an obvious high above you, the new low is where you add. In two contracts, add at least two more. Entry in the 50s plus an add in the 30s brings the average to the 40s — roughly 10 points better, "usually enough to save the trade."
- ADD POINT 2 — RETEST OF YOUR ORIGINAL ENTRY: price rolls over again but doesn't make a new low, just comes back to where you entered — add and get heavier.
- THE GOVERNING CONDITION: "as long as you don't get a candle close below the low you already have in place, the trade is still intact." That's what tells him he's safe to add and hold for the target. Lose that level and the premise is gone.
- CONFIRMATION: bottom wicks. Repeated clean bottom wicks "tell a story" and usually mean price is being driven higher — confluence on a trade already in.
- By the end of the example he's in FOUR TO SIX CONTRACTS, all added at retests, bottom wicks and the new low; a long hold through the London session that worked.
- WHAT MAKES IT WORK, NOT RECKLESS: "go in with a clear bias, a clear take profit and a clear stop. As long as neither of those two gets run, you're fine." VWAP and the EMAs work as guides for add-in spots too.

### 2. Pre-market routine for the New York open (from "My Pre-Market Routine For The New York Open")
- What he looks at in the five minutes before 9:30 and the thirty minutes after; the same process runs before Asia session.
- THE CHART: 9 EMA = purple, 21 EMA = green, VWAP = blue, plus the kill zones and pivots indicator marking prior-session highs and lows. "Those session levels are the ones you should always be hunting when you map your levels."
- MARKING THE HIGHS: London high at 31,095; then the obvious 5-minute wick highs below — 31,040, 31,052, the clear wick at 31,062, arguably 31,081.
- MARKING THE LOWS: pre-open wick lows, then the area of resistance below, then the deeper wicks at 30,748 and 30,667.
- THE PART MOST PEOPLE MISS: five minutes before the open price is pushing up. "It wants to trick you. It wants you believing it's running to those highs." But VWAP was above and there's a stack of deep lows underneath that can get retraced the moment the push runs out of fuel. "You hold both sides in your head before the bell, not one."
- THE 9:30 REACTION: price tapped an obvious wick perfectly and rejected — right where VWAP was. That's the A+ bear flag setup: pop, rejection, bear flag down, short taken as it loses the EMAs around 30,950.
- SCALING OUT AT THE MARKED LEVELS: three contracts — first off at 30,880, next at 30,744, last at 30,667. "Those aren't invented targets — they're the wicks you marked before the open."
- THE VWAP CHECK AT 9:25 IS THE TIEBREAKER: if price delivers above VWAP, you're looking at the London high and the stacked highs (longs); it didn't, so the short was on. First target alone was 100 points; the deep low was 300 points in roughly 45 minutes.
- PROCESS: "Mark both sides. Watch the reaction. Then go."

### 3. The whole playbook is flags (from "The 5 Trades That Paid Me $24,000 This Week")
- $24,000 in payouts that week across Lucid and Tradeify — roughly $8,000 Tradeify, $8,000 Lucid, $10,000 more requested.
- "Bull flags and bear flags, nothing else." Same structure every time: "bull flag or bear flag, enter at the midpoint or the EMA loss, target the nearest wick."
- TRADE 1 — Sunday night long: 15-minute bull flag, rejection off the 9 EMA (~29,920). Long in the 29,960s as soon as price hit the candle midpoint; target the nearest wick high at 29,990. ~30 points, almost no drawdown. "Above the EMAs and VWAP, you could have held it longer."
- TRADE 2 — Monday 9:30 long: rejection off the 21 AND the 9 EMA into a clean bull flag. Price closed above 30,244; with no highs nearby the target became the nearest psychological level — 30,500. 250 points, taken on both Tradeify and Lucid accounts; "it delivered well past it."
- TRADE 3 — Asia session short: 15-minute bear flag (push up, reject, fake out, roll over). Short as price dropped below VWAP ~31,040; target the nearest wick low 30,991. ~50 points in 30–45 minutes.
- TRADE 4 — bear flag short: rejection down, short ~30,785, target wick low 30,710, PM low and AM low at 30,641 below. "Kept delivering overnight — better safe than sorry on the exit."
- TRADE 5 — last night's long: bull flag, long once price closed above the 30,711 midpoint, targeting wick highs at 30,764 and the PM high at 30,791; live on Lucid and Tradeify.

### 4. Entry timing and sizing are everything (from "Being Right Doesn't Make You Money")
- You can be completely right about direction and still blow the account — entry timing and position sizing.
- THE RIGHT VERSION: daily levels marked days in advance; idea = short down into them, then long once swept. On the 5-minute there's far more noise, so the entry comes off the higher-timeframe level — a bull flag bouncing off 29,600. "That's an A+ setup, and one he'll ride all day."
- THE WRONG VERSION: Oct 14, a bear flag on the 15-minute into the 30,270 level — jump the gun and enter on the WICK instead of the CLOSE. That wick is 35–40 points. Five contracts with $2,500 of drawdown, and a retrace into the 9 EMA on the 15-minute blows the account. Same read, same direction, dead.
- Had he waited for the proper close on the bear flag: zero drawdown, and the trade printed beautifully to the downside anyway.
- THE CAGE (sizing rule): "It's like putting a dog in a cage that is way too small. If it's a big dog, he's gonna break the cage." The cage = your account and your sizing. Give the market room to fluctuate between drawdown and profit — "because anything is possible on the Nasdaq. NQ was down 1,000 points in 24 hours that week."
- WHAT LUCAS TRADES: bear flags and bull flags off the 9 EMA, with the 21 EMA and VWAP as confluence, plus session levels — AM high, PM high, Asia low, AM low.
- The deeper mistake: "thinking about today instead of this month, or the next three. Short-term thinking is where people get burned."

### 5. Process over P&L (from "Why Your P&L Is Lying To You")
- Trader A: +$5,000 on the day, didn't follow rules, poor risk management. Trader B: −$800, followed every rule, excellent decisions. "Repeat the process a hundred times and Trader B ends up further ahead. The daily number told you the opposite."
- "You can make $5,000 in a day trading like an idiot. You can make $1,000 executing your strategy perfectly."
- Two months of oversizing and luck can feel like being a god trader; "then things flatten out, the luck stops, and the market takes all of it back."
- "Losing money doesn't mean you traded badly." Nobody has a 100% win rate.
- OBSESS OVER THE PLAN, NOT THE NUMBER: Did the setup fit what you trade? Did you size correctly? Were you trading emotionally? "Money is a lagging indicator of your success — in trading and in business."
- Lucas's timeline: lost for over a year straight, then a couple of winning weeks, then a little money, then more; a year after that, his first $10,000 month. As of October 15, 2026: $50,000 taken in the last two and a half weeks of trading.
- "You shouldn't lose hope just because you're losing." And: "If you don't have money right now, get a job."

### 7. Asia session system (from RSS video 2qMuQZfB0co, 2026-11-12 — complete writeup in bdh-rss-latest.xml)
- Before the open: find the Globex open on the 5-minute (the green doji candle), mark prominent wick lows below and nearby highs. "This is how I find my levels… react to what's in front of me at the open."
- Trades the open right at 6:00; smaller-timeframe bull/bear flags taken to the marked levels.
- THE A+ SETUP: 5-minute bull flag — pullback, a 30-point bottom wick, new high in the candle, retracement back to VWAP with the 9 EMA and 21 EMA holding. Entry around the candle midpoint; target session highs in sequence (nearest wick high first, then next, then next — scaling out a contract at each); stop at the wick low of the entry. Even conservative targeting = a 2.5R trade.
- SECOND EXAMPLE: multiple bull flags, nothing closed below the EMAs, then a break — long the 960s, 9 EMA holds, ride to the PM high off the kill zones and pivots indicator.
- Both examples sat in drawdown first — 30 points on one, 150 on the other — before delivering.
- NO MAN'S LAND: the gap between the first ten minutes of Globex and the Asia open — he does NOT trade it. Volatility dies, spread widens, aggressive gaps. Wait for the Asia open.
- CORE: low-volatility sessions require sniper entries. "Bull flags and bear flags, that's the whole thing."

## Recurring Lucas mechanics (cross-video)
- Setup vocabulary: bull flags and bear flags ONLY. Enter at the candle midpoint or the EMA loss; target the nearest wick (session levels in sequence).
- Indicators: 9 EMA (purple), 21 EMA (green), VWAP (blue), kill zones and pivots indicator (prior-session highs/lows: AM high, PM high, Asia low, AM low, London high).
- Pre-open ritual: mark both sides (stacked highs AND deep lows), VWAP check at 9:25 as direction tiebreaker, watch the 9:30 reaction at a marked wick + VWAP.
- Scale-outs at pre-marked wick levels, never invented targets; stop at the structural wick low of the entry.
- DCA-in-drawdown adds at the new low and the original-entry retest — only while no candle CLOSES below the protected low; 4–6 contracts by design, not panic.
- Entry rule vs "being right": wait for the flag CANDLE CLOSE, never the wick (a 35–40 point wick entry with 5 contracts is account-killer sizing).
- Sizing = cage: size so the market can breathe between your stop and target ("NQ down 1,000 points in 24 hours").
- Asia session: sniper entries in low volatility; no-man's-land (post-Globex-open lull before Asia open) is untradeable; expect 30–150 points of drawdown before delivery on valid flags.
- Psychology: process over P&L; money is a lagging indicator; think in months, not days.
