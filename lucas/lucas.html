4. The Level-Mapping System (Core to Everything)

Before any trade, Lucas marks "the most obvious, prominent wick highs and wick lows on both sides of current price action" (my-pre-market-routine-for-the-new-york-open):

  • Upside targets: nearest prominent wick highs, then session highs (London High, PM High, Asia High).
  • Downside targets: nearest wick lows, support zones, session lows (PM Low, AM Low, Asia Low).
  • Psychological levels (e.g., 30,500) are the target when price is in "open air" with no nearby wicks: "When you are in open air like that, you target the nearest major psychological level." (the-5-trades-that-paid-me-24000-this-week)

These pre-marked wicks double as take-profit scaling zones once in a multi-contract trade.

5. Core Setups

Setup A — The Bull/Bear Flag (bread and butter)

Works on 5-min, 15-min, and 1-min charts in any session:

  1. Establish macro bias from trend and levels.
  2. Flag pulls back into the 9 EMA / 21 EMA with VWAP as added confluence.
  3. Trigger: the flag candle closes in trend direction / price breaks the flag structure and makes a new high (long) or new low (short). "Do not jump the gun. Wait for the actual candle close." (being-right-doesnt-make-you-money)
  4. Stop: below the wick low of the flag entry (long) or at/above the top wick of the flag structure (short).
  5. Targets: nearest wick high/low, then session highs/lows, then psychological levels.

Confluence requirements: "I trade bull and bear flags using the 9 EMA, 21 EMA, and VWAP as confluence. If price doesn't perfectly respect those indicators and my key daily levels (like AM High, PM High, or Asia Low), I do not take the trade." (being-right-doesnt-make-you-money)

Setup B — The A+ Asia Session Setup

From what-i-look-for-in-the-asia-session:

  1. At Globex open (6 PM): immediately mark any prominent highs/lows that print (e.g., a doji with a massive bottom wick → mark the wick low; mark nearby highs too).
  2. Wait for the flag: a clean 5-minute bull/bear flag (e.g., pullback with a ~30-point bottom wick making a new candle high).
  3. Retracement confluence: clean pullback to VWAP, holding above the 9 EMA and 21 EMA.
  4. Entry: "right around the midpoint of that pullback candle."
  5. Stop: "hard stop-loss directly below the wick low of the flag entry."
  6. Targets: nearest session highs; with multiple contracts scale out at nearest wick high → PM High → next major psychological level.
  7. Expectancy: "On a volatile day, holding this specific setup could net you a 350+ point ripper. On a normal day, targeting the standard levels still gives you a beautiful 2.5 Risk-to-Reward (RR) ratio trade."
  8. Pro tip: take profit slightly BEFORE the pivot level: "Sometimes the market will get incredibly close to your pivot target but not quite touch it. Do not be greedy!... Take profit slightly before the absolute level to ensure you get filled."

Setup C — NY Open Routine (9:25 → 9:30 → 45 min after)

From my-pre-market-routine-for-the-new-york-open:

  1. Indicators on: 9 EMA, 21 EMA, VWAP, Kill Zones & Pivots (e.g., London High at 31,095 auto-marked).
  2. 9:25 AM — mark both sides: obvious 5-min wick highs leading to the session high (31,040 / 31,052 / 31,062 / 31,081) and obvious wick lows below (pre-open wicks, 880s support, 30,748, 30,667).
  3. Avoid the 9:25 trap: a pre-market push above the EMAs "wants to trick you" — check VWAP position (ceiling overhead?) and untapped deep wick lows below.
  4. 9:30 decision rule: "If price delivers and holds above the VWAP at the open, we target the London Highs. If it rejects the VWAP, we look to short into those deep lows below."
  5. Execution example: price tapped a pre-market wick high + VWAP at the same spot, rejected, formed an A+ bear flag, broke below the 9/21 EMAs (~30,950) → short.
  6. Scale out on the pre-marked wicks: Contract 1 TP at 30,880 (~70 pts), Contract 2 TP at 30,744 (~200 pts), Contract 3 runner at 30,667. Result: a ~300-point flush within ~45 minutes.

Setup D — V-Shape Reversals with Liquidity Sweeps (100–200 point moves)

From how-i-catch-100-200-point-v-shapes-on-nq-bull-bear-flags (1-minute chart):

  1. Find the prominent high/low (liquidity). Example: price swept a prominent wick high left by the 8:30 AM news candle; a failed break of the Asia low earlier had flipped short-term momentum bullish/bearish accordingly.
  2. Wait for exhaustion at the sweep: "an indecisive, reversal-looking candle. The buyers were exhausted."
  3. Wait for the flag at the EMAs: after the first flush (~40 pts), price retraces to the EMAs — it "bounced perfectly off the 21 EMA but completely failed to close above the 9 EMA," forming a bear flag.
  4. Entry on the confirming candle close. Example short at 29,669.
  5. Confluences checked: swept major high + bear flag at EMAs + VWAP below + untapped lows at the bottom.
  6. Scale out on internal wick lows (5 contracts → targets 609 / 578 / 555), final TP at the untouched Asia session low (494/495). Result: 200-point drop in ~70 minutes.
  7. Mirror logic for longs: sweep a prominent low → bull flag at EMAs → target untapped highs.

Setup E — Consolidation Breakout

From the-setup-i-use-for-every-nq-breakout:

  1. In heavy chop, do not trade inside the range — wait for structure.
  2. Drop to the 1-minute chart; watch for a flag forming at the edge of the consolidation.
  3. Trigger = the break: "A flag forming isn't enough; you have to wait for the actual break... price finally broke out of that flag structure and created a brand new low."
  4. Targets = "draws on liquidity": previous session lows/highs are "the biggest magnets for price" (ultimate TP at the AM low ~29,138); internal scale-outs at obvious 15-minute wick lows (29,197).
  5. Trust the target over quick cash: with the position up $1,300 he held instead of closing — "if your technical analysis points to a lower level, you have to trust the setup."

Setup F — Trend Days ("The Money Glitch")

From passed-the-eval-and-took-3000-out-on-vest-live:

  • When NQ goes into a one-way expansion (his example: a historic 732-point flush), farm the 1-minute 9 EMA / 21 EMA retracements: "Every single time the 1-minute candle pulled back and tapped the 9 EMA or the 21 EMA, it bounced right off and created another 'kill candle' to the downside."
  • "Days like this do not happen often, but when they do, you have to press your advantage" — repeat entries payout after payout.
  • He also passed the Vest 10K Bespoke eval in 9 minutes on such a day (short from the 30,450s on long top wicks respecting EMAs, $1,000 target), got instantly funded, and pulled $3,000+ live.

6. Adding To A Trade In Drawdown — His Exact DCA Strategy

From adding-to-a-trade-when-your-in-drawdown-my-exact-strategy. Preconditions before ANY add:

  1. Clear macro bias from the previous trend (example: "a clear, undeniable uptrend... The bias is strictly bullish").
  2. An obvious target above (example: Asia High at 30,411).
  3. A hard stop-loss below (example: Asia Low at 30,311). Initial entry: long ~midpoint of a 5-min bull flag at 30,358 with 2 contracts — "a solid 1-to-1 risk-to-reward setup."

Add Spot #1 — the lower-low fake-out: price pushes above VWAP, then rolls over, closes below all EMAs, and makes a lower low. Newer traders panic here; he checks the macro trend and that the hard stop has NOT been hit, then: "When it rolls over and makes that new low, that is where I add to my size."

  • The math: "If I originally entered with 2 contracts in the 50s, I will add at least 2 more contracts down in the 30s. This pulls my average entry price right down into the 40s." Adding equal-or-greater size at the better price relieves pressure ("averaging down 10+ points").

Add Spot #2 — the retest: price pushes up slightly, rolls over again to the new entry zone.

  • Golden rule: "You can add more contracts here at your entry point as long as the candle does not close below the previous low you already established. As long as that floor holds, you are safe to hold for your original target."

Wick confirmation: "When I see multiple long bottom wicks printing in a row at a support zone, it tells me that buyers are stepping in and price is about to drive higher. It is a massive bullish confluence."

Result: holding 4–6 contracts from an averaged-down entry, confirmed by wicks holding support; trade may take hours (his ran 11 PM → London session) but hit the Asia High target.

Warning: "Dollar Cost Averaging is incredibly powerful, but you cannot just blindly buy dips when the market is crashing against you. You must have a clear bias based on the previous trend, an obvious target above you, and a hard stop-loss." (same article)

7. Scaling In / Out & Trade Management

  • Scale OUT, not up (profit side): with multiple contracts, take partials at each pre-marked level: first internal wick low/high → next major wick level → runner to the deepest session level. (NY open example: 3 contracts → 30,880 / 30,744 / 30,667.)
  • Move stop to break-even once the first target hits: "Once price drops and hits your first target level, immediately move your stop loss to break-even (or slightly into profit)." (my-a-bear-flag-checklist)
  • Lock profits early on extended moves: "It ended up flushing all night long, though we locked in our profits early — better safe than sorry." (the-5-trades)
  • TP slightly before major pivot levels to guarantee fills. (Asia session article)
  • Stops placement: below the flag's wick low (longs) / at the flag's top wick (shorts) / "at the nearest wick low or candle body, depending on the momentum" (1-min V-shape trades).
  • Zero-drawdown goal: a perfectly timed flag-break entry means "price never went against us" — his 140-point example had "absolutely zero drawdown the entire time."
  • Expect trades to need patience: Asia trades routinely take 30–90+ minutes to play out (his 200-point V-shape took ~70 minutes; the DCA trade ran from 11 PM into London).

8. Risk-to-Reward & Sizing Approach

  • Typical structure: stop at the flag wick, target the next session level → ~1:1 to 2.5:+ RR depending on setup (DCA trade was 1:1 by design; Asia setup ~2.5 RR on normal days).
  • Sizing rule of thumb: "You have to give the market enough space to fluctuate between drawdown and profit. If your size is so heavy that a standard 20-point wick blows your account, you are trading too big." (being-right-doesnt-make-you-money) — his math: 5 contracts on a bad early entry + a 40-point wick = "$2,500 and blown before the trade even goes your way."
  • Risk example he cites: entering a funded account "risking 15 points to lose $150." (the-brutal-reality-of-trading-when-youre-broke)
  • Scale laterally, not vertically: keep per-account risk small (e.g., 1 contract), then add more funded accounts on a copy trader rather than multiplying contract size. (what-getting-consistent-payouts-actually-taught-me)
  • His consistency math: "If you are disciplined and catch just 30 to 50 points a day" across 5 copy-traded accounts at 1 contract each ≈ $1,000/day ≈ $5,000/week, "even if you have one or two losing days and give back $10,000, you are still walking away with $15,000 a week... $60,000 a month in potential payouts, simply by trading one contract safely and consistently."

9. When NOT To Trade

From the-days-you-shouldnt-trade-nq-at-all-how-i-spot-them — "Knowing when to sit on your hands and protect your capital is the ultimate cheat code":

  1. After a huge overnight trend: "If we just had a massive, prominent trend overnight, the likelihood of the market giving us another massive, prominent move right at the AM open is significantly lower" (his example: 200-point London session). The market needs to digest.
  2. Indecisive opening candle: small 9:30 candle, wicks both sides, no volume/direction → expect chop.
  3. "Barcoding" on the EMAs: price ranging endlessly on top of the 9 EMA / 21 EMA / VWAP — "do not trade."
  4. No clear targets: tight range, no obvious liquidity draws above or below.
  5. Wicks everywhere: long wicks both directions = indecision.
  6. Fake flags: two red candles + tiny pullback baiting a bear flag; after 2–3 fakes in a range, skip the 4th attempt.
  7. No Man's Land (first ~50 minutes after the Globex open, before the true Asia open).
  8. Chop/consolidation on funded accounts generally: "If the market is ranging, chopping, or consolidating, I simply do not take a trade on my funded accounts. Some days I won't trade them at all." (what-getting-consistent-payouts-actually-taught-me)

His dramatic summary: on those days "you walk over to your computer, press the power button, unplug the power supply, and literally hide the cable from yourself."

3. Adding / Averaging Rules (DCA)

From adding-to-a-trade-when-your-in-drawdown-my-exact-strategy:

1Only average into a trend, never against it — clear prior trend (bias), an obvious target beyond, and a hard stop-loss are prerequisites. No blind dip-buying.

2Add when price makes a new lower low (fake-out) but the macro trend and hard stop are intact — add at least the original contract count (2 → +2) to pull the average 10+ points.

3Add again on retest ONLY if the candle does not close below the previous established low.

"As long as that floor holds, you are safe to hold for your original target."

4Bottom wicks in a row at support = bullish confluence that justifies holding the averaged position.

5The hard stop is sacred — if it runs, the DCA thesis is dead.

4. Drawdown / Stop Rules

1Hard stops at structure: below the flag wick low (long) / top wick of flag (short) / nearest wick low or candle body (scalps). Never arbitrary.

2Give the market room:

"You have to give the market enough space to fluctuate between drawdown and profit." (being-right-doesnt-make-you-money)

3Stop to break-even after the first target: "immediately move your stop loss to break-even (or slightly into profit)." (my-a-bear-flag-checklist)

4Cutting stops early is a symptom of over-leverage need:

"If you are cutting your stop early, that money means more to you than it should." (the-brutal-reality-of-trading-when-youre-broke)

5Moving your stop out of fear "works" sometimes — that's the trap: it's how "your P&L reinforces terrible habits and lies to you." (why-your-pl-is-lying-to-you)

5. Take-Profit / Payout Rules & Habits

1Targets are pre-marked wick lows/highs and session levels — never guessed. In open air, target the nearest major psychological level (e.g., 30,500).

2Scale out in tranches: contract 1 at the first internal wick level, contract 2 at the next major level, contract 3 held as a runner for the deepest level.

3Take profit slightly BEFORE pivot targets to guarantee fills:

5Trust the final target over quick profit: even flashing +$1,300 doesn't override the mapped target if TA points lower. (the-setup-i-use-for-every-nq-breakout)

2. Your P&L Is Lying To You

From why-your-pl-is-lying-to-you:

  • The core hard truth: "You can trade like an absolute idiot, break every single rule, and make $5,000 in a day. Conversely, you can trade perfectly, execute your strategy flawlessly, manage your risk, and only walk away with $1,000 — or even take a loss."

  • Bad habits get rewarded until they don't: "You can oversize, get lucky, and go on a winning streak for two months... But eventually, the luck runs out... and it swallows every single dollar right back into the black hole."
  • The reversal fake-out trap: moving your stop out of fear, holding a loser, and getting bailed out by a reversal "is exactly how your P&L reinforces terrible habits."
  • Trader A vs. Trader B: Trader A makes $5,000 breaking his rules; Trader B follows his process and loses. "If Trader B repeats their process continually and sticks to their proven strategy, their long-term outcome will be massively greener than Trader A."

5. Post-Payout Amnesia

From why-you-forget-how-to-trade-after-a-payout — a trap he's hit "three or four times":
