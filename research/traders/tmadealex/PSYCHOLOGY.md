## The Path — Debt to Payouts

- **Forex first, then futures props.** He came to futures from forex, and passed his first Topstep account in 2020. He traded props part-time for years and went full-time at the start of 2025.
- **"Market tuition" was expensive.** He says he spent about $27,000 before he started making profits. At his lowest he was around $50,000 in debt, part of it from trading and part from "stupid investments."
- **The turnaround.** His girlfriend gave him $25,000 and told him to "put it into trading. Buy more accounts. I believe in you." He says that within six months he paid her back, cleared the debt and passed $100,000 in profits. Later in 2026 he claims "200K in the last 11 months," including a "100K in 100 days" run trading three to five micros. These are his own claims, shown on stream.
- **Fear as fuel.** Being in debt is "one of the worst feelings." He streams for hours because he doesn't want to go back there: "use that fear as the energy."
- **From ICT to "home."** He learned ICT first ("I am a by heart ICT trader") and made a lot of money on initial-balance trading. When that edge faded, volume profile and order flow gave him the clarity ICT alone didn't. "I feel like I finally made it home when it comes to trading."
- **Live, every day, for free.** He trades New York, lunch and Asia on stream and won't put live trading behind a paywall. The TMADE community is "built on honesty and real performance, not hype."

## Patience Is the Edge

- **"Patience will change your life. It's that simple."** "You don't have to get into every trade. You don't have to wake up and trade… That's one of the biggest reasons why most of you are going to fail — because you have no patience."
- **"I don't chase it. I let it come."** It's a mantra he repeats on stream when a move leaves without him.
- **Missing a trade is fine.** "We could always find another trade."
- **Wait for the closure.** His most common self-review after a loss is that he entered before the candle confirmed: "If I just waited for structure…"
- **Asia gets office hours.** "The more you stay at the casino, the more you'll lose."

## Knowing His Own Weakness

- **He names his flaw:** "I will get aggressive into that level when I should not be doing that. I should be happy with the money I made." It shows up most after a green day.
- **He labels himself "an aggressive trader."** He reads the tape, scales in and out, and describes his style as "high cortisol, very entertaining, very aggressive." His co-host holds one or two micros for the whole move; Alex trades five to ten and gets in and out more.
- **Revenge trading happens, and he owns it.** On one stream he missed a target by a tick, re-entered big — "TP or nothing" — and blew a funded account. His lesson: "I became too fixated on hitting my target and I got completely smoked."
- **Rules exist for exactly those moments.** "It's taking everything I have not to pull the trigger" — then, after breaking his own rule and getting stopped: "That's why you don't break your rules."

## Losses, Ego and Being Green

- **"I don't care about being right. I care about being green."** Needing to be right is "all ego based." Choose the draw on liquidity and take what the market gives.
- **Take the paper cut.** He sizes small enough that a loss is "a paper cut," which keeps him able to re-enter calmly. "Because I'm in three micros, I can stomach this loss and then find a new position."
- **Own it out loud.** "I made the trade, I got to own it." He keeps losing days and blown accounts public: "People see me blow accounts, evals and funded, on stream sometimes."
- **Less anger over time.** Asked whether he's gotten less mad at losses: "Way less mad, bro… I'll take it. I'll own it."
- **Walk away from chop.** "The worst thing for me is chop." When nobody is getting rewarded, the right trade is no trade.
- **Protect the green.** Once he's up, he stops participating in a bad tape. That's "how you get past evals."

## Prop Firms — Fear, Greed and the Business

- **Why funded accounts die:** "It's fear and greed. You either have fear because you don't want to lose it, or you're too greedy and you just want to get a payout quickly."
- **Evals and funded are different games.** Evals are aggressive (risk most of the drawdown to pass fast). Funded accounts get small size and only A+ ideas, "how you keep the accounts alive."
- **Fewer accounts in bad conditions.** "I used to just go all in on 20 accounts and blow it." On rough days he lowers his risk by trading one or two accounts instead of ten or fifteen.
- **Pick rules that don't fight you.** He avoids trailing drawdown, daily-loss-limit add-ons and strict consistency, because rules that punish normal pullbacks wreck your psychology. He favours instant-payout, static-drawdown accounts for the "tangible feeling" of getting paid.
- **Big accounts can hurt your head.** "If I buy a 300K account, I'm going to want to trade it when I'm trading my 50Ks and it's going to mess my psychology up."
- **Take the money.** Small, frequent payouts rather than big buffers. Next step: move part of it into his own cash account.

## Learning and Belief

- **Belief comes from repetition.** "If you're not a believer of something, it ain't going to work… But if you can become a believer because you've seen it happen over and over again, that's when the confidence comes in."
- **Order flow is hard for about two months, then it clicks.** "The first two months of it I struggled… worth it in the end."
- **Always a student.** He spends a day a week on study and backtesting, building and testing his indicators with AI. "We're always students."
- **Build your own version.** "All these ideas and concepts work. They all make money… you're gonna have to put it together in a way that makes sense for you." "I don't want to turn you into me, but I want to give you all the tools that I use."
- **Adapt or get left behind.** The market shifts. Initial-balance edges faded and gap-fill statistics dropped, so he keeps changing what he trades and is "not afraid to admit it."
- **"It's hard. It's a lot of work, but you can get there. I believe in every single one of you guys."**
