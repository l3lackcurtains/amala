## 1. Method & concepts

**Overall framework**
- Alex describes his charts as "a mixture of my ICT liquidity stuff that I know very well and then all of the order flow volume profile stuff that I like" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [115:00]).
- He started with ICT: "I am a by heart ICT trader. That's what I first started trading with" (SQFUFmnif-Q [162:00]). He calls himself "an ICT background guy" (4kC8RG-Gckc [111:20]).
- His current core is volume profile and order flow: "my strategy is mostly volume profile… value area high, value area low, point of control mixed with absorption and exhaustion… and then I'm adding gamma levels into it" (SQFUFmnif-Q [128:40]).
- He compares his approach to "what Yush does… Andrea Chimi… Fabio Valentini… a lot of these very very big order flow traders" (SQFUFmnif-Q [129:00]).
- He used to be an all-in IB (initial balance) trader. Volume profile showed him why IB continuations started failing: the profile was saying the market was ranging (SQFUFmnif-Q [151:20]–[152:00]).
- He believes "volume profile and order flow is… the most goated." When a trade fails and he can't see why, the profile usually explains it (for example a rejection of value area high) (SQFUFmnif-Q [136:40]–[137:00]).
- On ICT: "one thing I will give mad credits ICT for is it teaches you entry model… But the volume profile and order flow… has really given me the clarity" (4kC8RG-Gckc [144:40]–[145:00]). He says ICT alone is "not enough context for" him (SQFUFmnif-Q [163:40]).
- He was once intimidated by order flow but now sees it as easier: "from a guy who knew everything about ICT, this [stuff] just looked hard… actually this is the easier thing to learn" (4kC8RG-Gckc [143:00]–[143:20]).
- He calls the change "the next evolution for me" and says "I feel like I'm home" in his trading (4kC8RG-Gckc [139:20], [141:40]–[142:00]).
- He keeps EMAs and VWAP "in the back of my head." He trades "level to level" and keeps a separate playbook for trending and ranging conditions (SQFUFmnif-Q [133:40]–[134:20]). Speaker attribution mixed between Alex and the co-host (unclear in captions).

**Time-based "boxes" (his own named concepts, taught in the coming TMade Academy)**

- **Breakfast box = "the New York AM trade."** It is the IB, the first hour of the NY session: "if you're an IB trader and you want to know where the IB is… that's why we have the breakfast box" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [339:20]; d8852XmmG5s [205:20]). IB low means "the first hour of the session" (SQFUFmnif-Q [66:40]).
- **Lunch box** is the midday range. His indicator plots lunch high, lunch mid, lunch VWAP and lunch low (B9RDdJSCVT4 [351:40]). No exact clock times are stated in this batch.
- **Bento box = 6:00–8:00 p.m. ET**, the two-hour range before Asia opens. "Sushi opens at 8" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [349:00]; SQFUFmnif-Q [135:00]; lTpwbRBxW_Y [29:00]).
- **Sushi box = the Asia session box** that opens at 8 p.m. ET (B9RDdJSCVT4 [349:00]). Its boundaries are sushi box high, sushi box low and sushi box VWAP (mwQ8r2uz9Mo [45:20], [80:40]).
- **Midnight snack = the 12:00 midnight open**, which he treats as "the extra" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [339:40]).
- He frames the boxes as accumulation, manipulation and distribution (AMD): "think of Bento as accumulation… sushi box is going to be your manipulation" (SQFUFmnif-Q [135:00]). In another stream: "You have accumulation, which is bento. You're going to have manipulation, which is going to be sushi… then… the trade that trades back towards… the middle" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [66:00]–[66:20]).
- He says the boxes work whatever the daily bias is. They are "a small piece of the bias… those things will move in a certain way where you can catch the scalps" (SQFUFmnif-Q [149:40]).
- He prefers range-friendly playbooks in choppy markets: "like sushi box would be good" for choppy markets (SQFUFmnif-Q [134:20]).
- "Lately lunchbox has been what's been paying me with like the least amount of stress" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [211:40]).
- How lunchbox trades in a range: price taps the lunchbox high, rejects, and moves back toward lunchbox VWAP. "This is a very big characteristic of how lunchbox trades when we are stuck in a range" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [357:20]).
- He reads a lunchbox "high to low formation" (the lunch range made its high first, then its low) as "not the best sign" and expects "we're probably going to take the lows" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [193:40]–[194:00]).

**Volume profile and auction market theory**
- He explains auction market theory with a flea-market analogy. Value is where "buyers and sellers are agreeing to transact." A breakout either finds acceptance in the previous value or "you're going to fail and you're going to run back into the market." Prices above value are "Whole Foods territory… Whole Paycheck" (SQFUFmnif-Q [138:20]–[139:40]).
- On ranges: "price… on volume profile the range is 70% of the time." On a balanced profile he will not take IB longs, IB shorts or continuation trades (SQFUFmnif-Q [180:00]–[180:40]).
- When the market repeatedly can't close below value area low, "what usually happens… is a full rotation to the other side of the range" (SQFUFmnif-Q [137:40]–[138:20]).
- Failed auction: price that can't get back into a value area, such as the overnight value area low or the previous weekly value, is a failed auction and he looks for the opposite rotation (SQFUFmnif-Q [154:40]–[155:00]; d8852XmmG5s [103:40]).
- On rotations: "rotation from value area to value area to point of control. That's the idea" (4kC8RG-Gckc [24:00]).
- On high and low volume nodes: "high volume nodes are… where prices go to and low volume node is where we run through." After running through a low volume node, price goes on to the next high volume node (B9RDdJSCVT4 [360:20]–[360:40]).
- High volume nodes act as support and resistance. He reads them mainly on the **weekly** profile: "it's areas where price has traded a lot… if we're finally above that, it should be support… below… resistance." Low volume nodes are gaps price runs through (_ifzXYkl3S0 [83:40]–[84:20]).
- He draws a weekly high volume node as a zone, for example a 40-point zone at 28,680–28,720. He then looks for extra reasons inside the zone, such as a call wall (B9RDdJSCVT4 [360:40]–[361:00]).
- On stacked value: levels that line up (for example current-session VAL, previous-session POC and previous-session VAL) form a key area (d8852XmmG5s [18:40]–[19:00]; SQFUFmnif-Q [172:40]–[173:20]).
- He uses overnight (ETH) session value areas: "I use those overnight session value areas cuz they're so goated" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [114:00]). He looks at the overnight profile once NY opens (SQFUFmnif-Q [168:00]).
- He uses weekly and previous-week profiles while trading on a 5-minute chart (SQFUFmnif-Q [144:40]–[145:00]).
- His ICT habit applied to the profile: "I 50% the weekly value area areas" (4kC8RG-Gckc [111:20]–[111:40]).
- Bias from POCs: "are there three point of controls… that continue to open lower… So I continue to have a bearish bias" (SQFUFmnif-Q [148:40]–[149:00]).
- Bias needs the other side to be trapped first: "I can't get short unless we fail to get above the higher value… I need the buyers to get squeezed. So if I'm looking for shorts, I want to go higher first" (SQFUFmnif-Q [149:00]–[149:20]).
- Naked POCs are targets: a naked previous-week POC that was never touched (4kC8RG-Gckc [137:20]). He says he has no data yet on how reliable weekly naked POCs are (4kC8RG-Gckc [137:20]).
- He has been testing older profiles. He uses a 6-month volume profile containing a VWAP for "no man's land" price (lTpwbRBxW_Y [10:40]–[11:20]).
- Profile shapes: his indicator labels P-shape and B-shape profiles and explains how to trade each (d8852XmmG5s [18:20]).
- Higher-timeframe context outranks lower-timeframe levels. On a weekly mean-reversion day a daily fair value gap "doesn't really matter" because price "can run through all that" (4kC8RG-Gckc [148:20]–[148:40]). The co-host added the HTF overpowering point (4kC8RG-Gckc [148:40]–[149:20]).

**ICT tools he still uses**

- Single prints (TPO) are similar to FVGs. "The good fair value gap… best fair value gaps have single prints," per a co-host. Alex plots single prints and says they matter more on ES than NQ (_ifzXYkl3S0 [85:00]; B9RDdJSCVT4 [354:00]–[354:40]).

- Liquidity he marks: Asia high/low, London high/low, previous day high/low, previous-day value areas, midnight open, 6 a.m. open, 10 a.m. open, hourly and 4-hour highs and lows. His higher-timeframe swing tool flags sweeps of these (B9RDdJSCVT4 [353:20]–[353:40]; d8852XmmG5s [22:00], [209:00]–[211:20]).

- Premium/discount and 50% of the range (d8852XmmG5s [65:20]–[65:40]). He also used CISD and order blocks once, putting his stop at the breakfast-box mid (d8852XmmG5s [82:40]).

- He used to always short 50% of big moves. "Shorting 50% of any move will work enough that you can keep spinning your wheels doing it, but it doesn't work enough without more context." **Devil's marks (from "Ocala" / Ocalan's 80/20 method)**

- His indicator shows 10-minute and 200-second devil's marks on any chart timeframe. It shows a mark only after the candle closes, and only if it sits within about 10–15 points of a 20 or 80 level. A mark disappears once touched (_ifzXYkl3S0 [38:00]–[38:20], [73:40]; B9RDdJSCVT4 [355:00]).
- He targets a devil's mark only when price is trading toward it or it formed inside the current range: "till then I don't put too much weight on them" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [355:40]; d8852XmmG5s [203:00]).
- A devil's mark that lines up with a 15-minute candle is a nearby draw (mwQ8r2uz9Mo [27:00]; lTpwbRBxW_Y [30:20]).

**Gamma / GEX**

**Order flow / tape**

- On why trapped traders matter: "Somebody has to feel pain for someone else to win… somebody has to be manipulated for me to want to get into the trade" (SQFUFmnif-Q [145:40]–[146:00]).

## 2. Setup & entry criteria

- **Bento/sushi box setup:** "wait for the bento box to form the high and the low. And as soon as the bento box closes… look for the sushi box to take out the bento box high or low and to retrace it back towards the bento box VWAP." The exception is when something on the chart blocks that target (lTpwbRBxW_Y [69:20]–[69:40]).
- The target after the sushi sweep is the middle of the range, "50% if possible… or one of the VWAPs at least." If liquidity sits beyond it, the target can be the other side of the range (SQFUFmnif-Q [135:20]–[136:00]).
- Example of a blocked trade: the trade back to bento VWAP failed because "a very important G1 gamma level" sat in the way (lTpwbRBxW_Y [66:40]).
- **Lunchbox re-entry long:** ES took the lows and NQ "recovered back into the lunchbox." He wants price back above lunchbox VWAP, targets lunchbox high, and puts the stop at the low. In the example he risked $500 to make $1,200 (B9RDdJSCVT4 [261:00]–[261:20]; [250:00]).
- **The 2 p.m. open:** "2 p.m. opens, manipulates lower… hopefully that sweep… is the manipulation." He takes a small paper cut first and then "get[s] more aggressive when I see the manipulation form" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [300:00]–[300:20]; [308:20]).
- **Volume-profile entry logic:** confirm the context with the profile, for example a failed auction back into value. Then use an ICT trigger such as a 5-, 3- or 2-minute IFVG to enter. "So volume profile confirms it. Now the trigger is the inversion fair value gap" (SQFUFmnif-Q [162:40]–[163:40]).
- **Continuation long:** sellers get absorbed, "they're selling back into the range, but they can't go lower and buyers take us back out of the range and then we go higher… just because I have volume profile I can't see the trade. I need to also wait to see who's getting absorbed" (SQFUFmnif-Q [145:40]).
- **ICT setup reframed with the profile:** reclaim value area low with a 5-minute change in state of delivery and an FVG. Sellers got stuck below VAL, then a displacement candle produced a V-shaped recovery back inside value (SQFUFmnif-Q [175:20]–[178:00]).
- **Confirmation depends on location in a HTF wick:** "If we hit 25% and not 50, but we get a 5-minute IFG, I might take it… But when we get to 50%, I'll take a 1-minute IFG… because 50% should just hold if you're a buyer" (SQFUFmnif-Q [120:00]–[121:00]).
- A longer timeframe is needed at a weaker location. A 1-minute trigger is enough at a strong one (SQFUFmnif-Q [120:20]–[120:40]).
- **Weekly HVN or POC support:** a weekly high volume node "should be support if we're above it." He added a full mini there because "if we don't hold this little box here, I don't want the trade" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [60:40]–[61:00]).
- **Stacked-confluence long:** "50% of the range, VWAP, low volume node… all sitting here. It's too perfect not to try it" (d8852XmmG5s [65:00]). He took it with 3 micros and a stop below the 15-minute FVG (d8852XmmG5s [62:20]–[62:40]).
- **Invalidation line:** "for me invalidation is the 15-minute gap" (d8852XmmG5s [63:40]).
- **HTF sweep then 1-minute inversion:** "High time frame liquidity sweep and then just quick one minute inversion up to the nearest imbalance. But we got to get the one minute inversion." He waits with "my finger on the trigger" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [57:00]–[57:20]). The phrasing may be from Zarb (unclear in captions).
- **His sweep alert:** his indicator notifies him when price sweeps an important higher-timeframe level such as a 4-hour high or Monday's high. He then wants an inversion or CISD right after the sweep. A sweep with "never… any sort of inversion gap" does not qualify (_ifzXYkl3S0 [166:40]–[167:40]).
- **Midnight-open bias:** "I'd like to get long below midnight… if we're going to be bullish" (d8852XmmG5s [30:20]; 4kC8RG-Gckc [29:40]). Speaker partly Zarb; Alex agreed.
- **Morning checklist he listed:** "We've swept liquidity. We're in a deep discount. We're above previous day's point of control. If we can just manipulate down below midnight, I'm just going to grab a long" (4kC8RG-Gckc [29:40]). Speaker probably Zarb (unclear in captions).
- **News-candle low:** before the open, the low of the 8:30 NFP data candle was swept and price then ripped. "We took out the data low" was part of his reason to hold a long (d8852XmmG5s [50:40]–[51:00]).
- **15-minute ORB:** "this is going to be the 15-minute ORB… once it closes… that'll be the high… see if it tries to break out and then fail" (d8852XmmG5s [43:40]–[44:00]).
- **Trade-the-trend days:** don't fade a strong open. "I definitely just don't want to be the guy looking for shorts right at the open" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [35:40]). On a parabolic day: "you don't want to step in front of the train" (4kC8RG-Gckc [148:00]).
- **When to skip:** "So ES has a SMT with the high and also rejected the G1… we have like one minute inversions for both directions. This is just where you don't do [anything]" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [30:20]).
- **Conflicting ES and NQ:** don't automatically short the weaker index just because "there's more room… There's a reason that one's not going down to previous days low yet" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [55:00]).

- **A+ setups only, especially on evals:** "right now, I'm not seeing any A+ right yet. Right now, I'm just seeing a bounce between the G1 and the [top]" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [37:40]).
- **Big-loss days:** he labels risky ideas "eval trade." "I would not do any of this in funded… this is like an eval trade" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [52:40]–[53:00]). "Don't advise this in funded. If you guys are trading funded accounts, I think you're gambling today" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [61:20]).
- **Discount and premium:** he admits shorting in the discount of a leg "is also not the smartest thing to do… I made the trade, I got to own it" (4kC8RG-Gckc [163:20]–[163:40]).
- **Reactions, not limits (on co-host Ike's 30-minute method):** don't place blind limit orders at the zone. Let price enter it, watch the reaction, then use a **buy stop** above the candle to enter "because the market will most likely do what it's already done in a choppy environment" (SQFUFmnif-Q [91:20]–[92:00]).
- He used a buy stop himself to get back into a range: "Closure back inside the range here… we're in the buys" (d8852XmmG5s [218:00]–[218:20]).
- **Ike's rule, borrowed:** "If price has been doing this for a while, it'll probably keep doing the same thing" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [369:00]).
- **His own backtest:** he tweaked the strategy he had traded "for pretty much the last year" for recent IB behaviour. It reached a 78% win rate over the last 45–50 days. "No matter what I did, the shorts never paid… only longs… +9R" (4kC8RG-Gckc [20:40]–[21:20]).

## 3. Stops, targets, management

- **Stop placement by order flow:** put the stop just beyond the aggressive buyers or sellers. "I have all these buyers that I can put my stop below for… a really tight stop… cuz we get below this, you don't want this trade" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [46:20]–[46:40]).
- In other trades: "Stops going to go below these sellers" (mwQ8r2uz9Mo [75:40]). "Going to put my stop above these orders here. We're going to risk 400" (mwQ8r2uz9Mo [68:00]).
- **Structure and gamma stops:** stop below the call wall or a G-level. In one trade he risked $146 to make $196, "a little more than one R" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [70:20]).
- He accepts negative R at times: "Risking 180 to make 132. A little bit of a negative R here" (mwQ8r2uz9Mo [76:00]). He also called a trade "some ICT negative R vibes" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [283:40]).
- **Targets:** session or box highs and lows, VWAPs, POC, VAH and VAL, devil's marks, high volume nodes, open FVGs, and the 50% of HTF wicks (B9RDdJSCVT4 [250:00]; _ifzXYkl3S0 [37:20], [40:40]; d8852XmmG5s [20:40]). He prefers "low hanging fruit": "I'm not going to be a mad man and try to take this high… I'm going to take the low hanging fruit" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [71:20]).
- **Scalping, not holding:** "I haven't been holding for the full move. I just take it in the direction and then I just take whatever profit I feel like I can get" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [32:00]). He regrets this: "I'm always on the right side of the trade and never holding it long enough" (lTpwbRBxW_Y [89:20]).
- **Breakeven quickly with bigger size:** "Because I trade bigger size I have to try to get those free trades on those pullbacks" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [49:40]).
- He moves to breakeven when sellers come in aggressively against the trade (d8852XmmG5s [44:20]).
- He moves to breakeven quickly in a range because "if I go break even, they're going to stop me out probably" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [97:40]).
- **Trailing:** "I'm going to just start trailing this in case it wants to reverse… secure 500… 525… 600" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [40:20]; speaker likely Zarb). His own trailing: "I'm going to trail this thing cuz I don't trust this market anymore" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [57:20]).
- **Tight stop after aggression without continuation:** "I'm going to have a tight stop here for $60 profit. So 60 bucks to try to make a thousand" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [45:20]).
- **Re-entry with more size after a stop:** "I'll take the stop and… enter back in with double the size. I'll do six micros instead of three at that point because I'll have more confirmation" (d8852XmmG5s [87:20]).
- "I can take a paper cut and then I can get aggressive… a three, $400 paper cut" (B9RDdJSCVT4 [300:00]).
- **Small size makes losses survivable:** "because I'm in three micros, I can stomach this loss and then find a new position" (d8852XmmG5s [63:40]). "I went small size so I can aggressively get back into it" (4kC8RG-Gckc [66:00]).
- **Adding:** he adds when price holds a level. "I'm adding to 10 here… if it holds" (_ifzXYkl3S0 [50:00]). On another trade he scaled a short from 6 to 8 contracts as price moved his way (d8852XmmG5s [94:20]–[94:40]).
- He admits averaging into losers. "I'm in 20 contracts now… this is my opportunity to get out" was a rescue trade, with breakeven as the goal (_ifzXYkl3S0 [111:40]–[112:20]).
