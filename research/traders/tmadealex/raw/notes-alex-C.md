## 1. Method & concepts

- He says volume profile beats any FVG: "Volume profile shits on any single Fair Value Gap or anything that I've ever learned from ICT... I feel like I finally made it home" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [90:00]–[90:40]).
- As an ICT trader he could "trade base hits into fair value gaps" but couldn't see where price would rotate to. Weekly volume profile shows him that (RdG1U26zMrg [88:20]–[88:40]).
- "Volume profile in my opinion is order flow, because that's volume and where money moved" (JC4NpAa4554 [55:20]).
- He describes his style as "a hybrid": a "volume profile, order flow, gamma level strategy" plus separate strategies for the Lunch, Breakfast, Bento and Sushi boxes (kH54smHa9II [257:00]). He says he has never seen anyone else combine the initial balance with breakfast-box trading (kH54smHa9II [255:40]).
- He reads both views of one setup. An ICT trader would see "an easy short: we took previous day high and have a 5-minute inversion gap." He sees "we retraced back to a 15-minute value gap and we're holding Sushi VWAP" (d4ICOyBiBdk [67:40]).
- Lately he avoids FVGs: "I've been wanting to just trade volume profile, low volume nodes, high volume nodes, and kind of stay away from the idea of a 15-minute FVG or an hourly FVG" (RdG1U26zMrg [84:40]).
- "Really the gap doesn't matter. It's the low volume node in the gap." With a giant 15m gap you can't tell whether to short its CE, its quarter or its top; the LVN inside it pinpoints the spot (RdG1U26zMrg [85:00]).
- He tells viewers to pick what suits them: "All these ideas and concepts work. They all make money... you're gonna have to put it together in a way that makes sense for you" (JC4NpAa4554 [150:40]); "everybody is different" (RdG1U26zMrg [104:00]).
- He has stopped trading the IB (initial balance): "the IB has been just a failure of a strategy lately" (3qTrUb7iWGo [170:40]). He still watches IB highs and lows as levels and says the IB "is calling it" for bias (JC4NpAa4554 [89:00]; kH54smHa9II [93:20]).

### Volume profile: definitions and rules
- **Weekly profile.** "It's just taking the weekly candle range... like a fib on the low to high. Instead we're putting a volume profile to that weekly range" (RdG1U26zMrg [89:00]).
- **Rotation rule.** "If price can't hold above value, it's going to rotate to the other side of value" to see whether the other side can take control (RdG1U26zMrg [90:40]). Examples: weekly VAH to weekly VAL (kH54smHa9II [55:40], [70:40]), and VAL long to POC to VAH (RdG1U26zMrg [171:00]).
- **Full-rotation trade.** "I'm looking for longs at value area low and TPs at point of control or value area high. You're looking for the full rotation" (RdG1U26zMrg [170:40]–[171:00]). He ignores LVNs inside value for this trade: "you'll never take the trade if you think those low volume nodes inside value are going to stop price" (RdG1U26zMrg [173:40]).
- **Acceptance back into value.** Sellers explore below value. If buyers "got us back inside of this value," the next TP is VWAP, then a rotation to POC. "This is the strategy that I'll trade literally every day in the New York session" (RdG1U26zMrg [83:20]–[83:40]).
- **Where to enter.** He won't buy below value area low. Longs go "around here on this first push" back into value, because that is where buyers show they will take back sellers' territory (RdG1U26zMrg [138:40]–[139:00]).
- **Best targets.** "P[OC], value area low, value area high is the best targets. Forget about the 4 hours, the hourlys... if I'm just trading off of the order flow stuff, I'm just going to trade to the previous day PC or current PC or weekly" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [86:40]–[87:00]).
- **Weekly levels beat daily levels.** "The weekly levels are more powerful" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [87:20]). Once price holds above weekly value it rotates to the weekly POC, and daily FVGs on the way "don't matter" when the weekly profile shows no volume there (8DwrB6Q56Ss [88:20]–[90:00]).
- **The "signature."** A 15m candle closed above the weekly VAL, which price had failed to close above for days. The next candle retraced into a bullish 15m FVG sitting on that VAL. That "was the signal that we're heading higher" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [89:00]–[89:40]).
- **POC.** "Where the most volume traded the previous day... Are sellers in control? Are buyers in control?" (JC4NpAa4554 [28:20]). For him, POC moving up after a big volume buy "shows me a lot of volume transacted" and kept him in a long (d4ICOyBiBdk [100:20]).
- **Golden rule (Zarb's, Alex endorses).** "If you're above previous day PC you have to have a bullish bias; below it, bearish." Zarb calls it "my golden rule" and says newer traders should "just don't look at shorts if you're above previous day's POC" (JC4NpAa4554 [28:40]–[29:00], Zarb speaking with Alex agreeing).
- **POC shifting.** "Three days in a row where the PC has shifted lower. Very bearish." After two sessions of lower POCs, he looks for continuation on the third (RdG1U26zMrg [128:40], [148:40]). He dislikes it "when POC shifts to the highs when we're at the highs" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [71:40]).
- **Value-area shift.** "We traded down here so long that value area low shifted below price" (RdG1U26zMrg [193:40]).
- **HVN vs LVN.** LVNs are where "there's not enough volume... price can move quickly. That's how candles get long." HVNs are where more time was spent, so "it'll be harder to break through" (RdG1U26zMrg [93:40]; JC4NpAa4554 [160:00]–[160:40]).
- **Why LVNs can hold.** Aggressive buyers created the LVN by pushing fast. When sellers come back, those buyers may defend it. "If they don't, it gets ran through quickly" (RdG1U26zMrg [81:40]–[82:00]).
- **HVN to HVN.** "If you hold one high volume node you go to the next high volume node." He uses nodes to judge where price can travel more than as entries (RdG1U26zMrg [103:20]).
- **LVN outside value.** These matter more to him than LVNs inside the range. Example: price fails to get back above VAL, retests the LVN below value, and continues lower; the LVN then acts as S/R (RdG1U26zMrg [171:40]–[172:20], [183:00]).
- **The 70% stat.** "We trade inside of value about 70% of the time" (3qTrUb7iWGo [129:00]).
- **Value stacking.** Value-area highs and lows from different sessions that line up form a key box. He cares about stacked VAH/VAL more than stacked POCs, because each session's VAL tends to hold. His indicator marks the stacked boxes; "this box here is two stacked levels, this one three" (RdG1U26zMrg [123:00], [128:40]–[129:40]).
- **Value stacking in practice.** "That's not a 15-minute fair value gap. That is a value stack," and price rejected and retested it repeatedly (kH54smHa9II [399:20]–[399:40]). He calls stacked levels "super OP" (RdG1U26zMrg [154:20]) and plans to teach them in "Teammate Academy" (kH54smHa9II [71:00]).
- **Session split.** His profile splits the overnight (ETH) session, in teal, from the New York (RTH) session, in purple (JC4NpAa4554 [158:20]).
- **RTH-only profile.** Some traders use only RTH. Example: price couldn't get above the previous New York VAH, so it should rotate to New York's POC: "it's really that simple" (RdG1U26zMrg [149:40]–[150:00]).
- **Adding confluence.** The simple concept needs confluence for a higher win rate: call or put walls, trapped buyers at the level, low volume above (RdG1U26zMrg [150:00]–[151:40]).
- **Timeframe doesn't matter for VP.** "The volume profile never changes" between the 1m, 5m, 15m or hourly. Timeframe matters for candle-closure confirmation (RdG1U26zMrg [101:00]).
- **Lookback.** He charts 10 days; on the 15m he loads about 3 months to see weekly value areas (3qTrUb7iWGo [167:20]–[167:40]).

### Order flow / delta / tape
- **Absorption.** Delta shows sellers are aggressive, yet price isn't going lower and is making wicks. "Sellers are getting absorbed by buyers... that's how you use Delta." A strong close above the level then squeezes the sellers (3qTrUb7iWGo [62:40]–[63:20]).
- **Aggression must be rewarded.** "If this low is going to get taken, those sellers have to get aggressive. They have to be rewarded for that aggression" (3qTrUb7iWGo [170:20]). Aggressive sellers with "a big ass wick on the 5-minute" means they're not being rewarded, so he goes long (3qTrUb7iWGo [126:40]).
- **Chop read.** When buyers and sellers are both aggressive at opposite ends and "nobody is getting rewarded," the market has no direction. "Trading this is a bit more gambling" (kH54smHa9II [247:40]–[248:20]).
- **Big buyer or seller as anchor.** He enters "with the big boys" and puts the stop beyond the big order. "Stop right under that huge buyer," previous-day POC as target (Druby describing Alex's trade, _09EJSMy208 [62:20]–[62:40]; kH54smHa9II [34:40], [293:40]).
- **Trapped participants.** "Seller got trapped... That's how I understood this was going to be a protected low. Buyers stepped in and absorbed price" (3qTrUb7iWGo [34:40]). Trapped buyers at a level are short confluence (RdG1U26zMrg [150:40]–[151:40]).
- **Squeeze mechanics.** If sellers lose an area where they were aggressive, they are "not underwater anymore" and can push lower. If price closes above their selling pressure, they get squeezed and buy back (3qTrUb7iWGo [39:40], [180:40]–[181:00]).
- **Buying a pump.** "Even on a pump, you can get in when you see those big buyers getting activated, as long as you have an area for price to go to" (3qTrUb7iWGo [57:00]).
- **Speed.** He says he reads tape well in real time and can scalp both directions from delta and structure (3qTrUb7iWGo [116:40]; kH54smHa9II [36:40]–[37:20]).
- **Inside the candles.** "I'm in the matrix of the candles. I'm seeing the orders." He gets in and out more than Zarb on the same idea (i_UTAJUHj_4 [113:20]).

### ICT / liquidity concepts he still uses
- **Premium/discount and 50%.** He wants entries in discount of the leg. "Taking a long up here is less probable... you're in way premium of this leg." The 15m gap at 50% of the leg plus the previous session VAH "was the place to get the longs" (nSwS9y-DpFo [65:00]–[66:40]).
- **Discount check.** Before calling a low protected, he checks whether the leg reached discount (3qTrUb7iWGo [176:20]–[177:00]).
- **Second sweep.** "Are we really going to get the first inversion off of a previous day high? It's very rare... a lot of times you get a second sweep" (d4ICOyBiBdk [68:40]–[69:00], [76:00]). He repeats this to Zarb on a failed eval short (JC4NpAa4554 [71:00]).
- **SMT.** "ES took the high, fell back into the range, causing an SMT at the high. This is where you get tricked and you get smoked" (d4ICOyBiBdk [84:00]).
- **SMT on a VWAP.** ES touched the lunchbox VWAP and NQ didn't: "very powerful SMT... when you're trading the VWAPs, you want to watch them both" (kH54smHa9II [402:40]–[403:00]).
- **Daily FVG + BPR.** A daily FVG together with a BPR (balanced price range) is a major draw; he planned to long it "with every account I have" (kH54smHa9II [45:20], [61:40]–[62:00], [82:20]).
- **10 AM PO3.** Accumulation, then 10 AM opens and runs into a 15m FVG (manipulation), then the distribution leg. "One of the most gangster PO3s there is because you're trading a brand new [hourly] candle" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [111:40]–[112:40]).
- **4h PO3.** He held a long because the 4h candle "opened and instantly went into that 15-minute gap below, inducing sellers, creating the manipulation leg" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [69:00]).
- **Change in state of delivery (CISD).** "We have two changes in state right there" as a short reason (RdG1U26zMrg [112:00]). He says you can take a CISD early "because you're already anticipating this entire move," or wait for the closure confirmation (RdG1U26zMrg [178:00]–[178:20]).
- **Equal highs and lows** are targets: "equal highs... that's what you need to target" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [25:00]–[25:20]; kH54smHa9II [235:20]).
- **Data wick.** The high or low of a news candle (CPI/PPI "data wick") is a liquidity target (3qTrUb7iWGo [29:20], [34:00]; kH54smHa9II [09:20]).
- **Hourly-candle play.** "This hourly candle swept the low of this candle. I go into premium of this hourly candle and I take the longs from there," and the idea is to break the Sushi Box high (nSwS9y-DpFo [90:00]–[90:20]).
- **Hourly wick fib.** He draws a fib from the hourly wick's body to its extreme and looks for price to hold 50% or the quarter. His "wick fib" indicator does this automatically on lower timeframes (JC4NpAa4554 [148:40]–[150:20]; 3qTrUb7iWGo [178:40]).

### Devil's marks
- **Definition.** A devil's mark is "an area where a candle has no upper wick" (kH54smHa9II [239:20]). He only looks at them on **200-second** and **10-minute** candles (kH54smHa9II [334:00]–[334:40]).
- **Use.** "It is a magnet for price. But you have to have a reason for price to go here. You can't just short into it" (kH54smHa9II [335:00]).
- **Price filter.** A valid mark must be "within 20 points of the 20s or the 80s" (e.g., x20/x80 handles). "If it's a devil's mark that's not at an 80s or 20s, I don't touch it." He credits this to "Ocala" (name unclear in captions) (kH54smHa9II [335:20]–[335:40]).
- A mark that is on both the 10-minute and the 200-second "at the same time" is a more extreme liquidity level; "very rare that we don't go tag" it (kH54smHa9II [323:40]–[324:00]).
- "These devil's marks are magnets for price. Back test it" (kH54smHa9II [160:20]; d4ICOyBiBdk [112:20]). They can also act as S/R after being tagged (kH54smHa9II [336:00]).
- His indicator removes a mark once it's touched "so that you never have to look at them again" (JC4NpAa4554 [148:20]).

## 2. Setup & entry criteria

- **Sequence (NY, volume profile).** (1) Mark weekly and previous-day value, POC and HVN/LVN. (2) See whether price is accepting or rejecting value. (3) Wait for a strong 5-minute closure through the value edge ("I want to see a nice strong push on a five-minute candle, nice closure inside"). (4) Read delta for absorption or squeeze. (5) Enter with the big orders. (6) Target VWAP, POC, then the opposite value edge (3qTrUb7iWGo [42:00]–[42:40], [57:20]; RdG1U26zMrg [83:20]–[83:40]).
- **Call-out logic.** "If we close strongly above value area low of the previous New York session... we would see a pop straight up into that [data] wick" (3qTrUb7iWGo [57:20]).
- **Weekly-level rule.** "Never short into a weekly value area low. Do not short into a weekly value area low. That is suicidal" (3qTrUb7iWGo [121:40]–[122:00]). "Never short weekly value area low or weekly value area high... You have to wait for confirmation through those levels. Those are higher time frame key areas" (3qTrUb7iWGo [160:40]–[161:00]).
- **"One rule."** "I do not long ever into value area high on a weekly and I do not short into a value area low when I'm close to it, because those are weekly pivot points where markets will rotate" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [98:40]–[100:40]).
- **Previous-day high rule.** "Can't really long once previous day high has been taken... breaks all my rules" (d4ICOyBiBdk [66:20]). With no liquidity left above, "I have no targets higher on NQ" (d4ICOyBiBdk [75:00]–[75:20]).
- **Needs a target.** "You can't just really long it or short it. You have to have a reason to... I had a target obviously PC" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [86:20]). He wouldn't short with "no real liquidity for me to target... no 5-minute gaps" above or below (3qTrUb7iWGo [87:40]).
- **No middle-of-range entries.** "I never trade from a middle of a range to where there's no imbalance" (8DwrB6Q56Ss [41:40]–[42:00], speaker likely Druby/Alex, unclear). Alex: "When the IB closes and price action is still around the middle of the range, it just makes it hard to identify which direction" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [80:00]).
- **Manipulation first.** "I just don't want to be the guy shorting right here. I want to be the guy that shorts off of a manipulation higher... squeeze out some longs" (kH54smHa9II [93:20]). "There's no manipulation yet. We have a range... no reason to long" (kH54smHa9II [103:40]).
- **Continuation trader.** "I'm a continuation trader. These buyers that are getting induced here, I need to see this actually start to move" (i_UTAJUHj_4 [89:40]).
- **Confirmation timeframe.** He uses the 1-minute for closures, "a lot of times I'll go to the three just to have confirmation" (RdG1U26zMrg [152:20]). Waiting for a 3m or 5m close can cost the entry and leave no logical stop (RdG1U26zMrg [169:20]–[169:40]).
- **Better entry after confirmation.** Draw a fib from the confirmation candle's low to high and wait for a pullback to 50% (RdG1U26zMrg [178:40]).
- **Grading.** "This is a C-grade trade... no liquidity above... and ES has failed to go higher." He would "never take this on a funded account ever." It was taken on an eval only (d4ICOyBiBdk [90:20]–[90:40]).
- **A+ example (RdG1).** A put wall at the reclaimed value low is "an A+ setup" (RdG1U26zMrg [84:00], co-host suggests and Alex agrees).
- **"No perfect setup."** "No such thing as a perfect setup. Like this is a good setup" (kH54smHa9II [110:40], co-host and Alex).
- **Skips (Asia).** He wants price below the 15m gap; "anything up here is just kind of stupid. You want to be in some kind of discount" unless anticipating shorts after a previous-day-high sweep. "I'm not going to anticipate that. I'll just read and react" (nSwS9y-DpFo [24:40]–[25:00]).
- **Follow the session trend.** "Since it's been buyers all session, I want to follow the trend of the session. I don't want to think I'm smarter than everyone else and try to short." He needs an inversion of the 15m gap before considering shorts (nSwS9y-DpFo [88:40]–[89:00]).
