Overview — Who Is Denny?

Denny ("Denny Dollas") is a BDH Trading floor trader and U.S. Army veteran who scalps Nasdaq futures (NQ) almost exclusively, trading prop-firm futures accounts (Apex, Tradeify, Lucid, My Funded Futures, IQ Capital, Blue Sky, Vest and more) — often 20+ funded 50K accounts at a time, cycled one at a time or multiplied with a trade copier. His system is deliberately small-ball: ranges marked on the 5-minute chart and executed on the 1-minute, strict 10-point "base hits" and 1:1 reversal targets, a stop loss on every trade, and his self-described "ultimate rule" of one trade per account per day — win or lose, the account is locked for 24 hours. Rather than hunting home runs, he compounds many small wins across many accounts, which he sums up in his own words as "small wins, lots of accounts, and compounding." In the July–September 2026 quarter he documented spending $4,992 on evaluations and taking $67,468 in payouts ($62,476 net) — all published transparently, including the blown accounts.

"One trade per account, per day. If the trade is a win? I am done with that account for the day. If the trade is a loss? I am done with that account for the day. No revenge trading. No looking for 'just one more setup' to make the money back. Once an account has taken a trade, it is locked for the next 24 hours."

5. Core Setups

A. "Two in the Chamber" (2ITC) — signature reversal setup (2-in-the-chamber)

The setup he says he "actually trades":

  1. Long: two consecutive 5-min candles each making a new session low (more = higher probability). The next candle sweeps yet another new session low but flips and closes green → enter long at the open of the next candle.
  2. Short: mirror image — consecutive new session highs, trigger candle makes a new high but closes red → short.
  3. Stop: exactly one tick below/above the trigger candle's wick. "Sometimes the next candle will wick down and miss your stop by a single tick before ripping upward."
  4. Target: strict 1:1 risk-to-reward — "on NQ, a 1:1 trade is often already a $300 to $400 profit. Going for more than that is just getting greedy."
  5. Difference vs a plain 3-bar reversal: the trigger candle MUST sweep the session extreme before reversing — "That sweep is the secret sauce."
  6. Context examples: risked $885 → +$3,000 on election night; +$770 (missed a $2,385 run by respecting the 1:1 — "I stuck to my rules"); +$800; used on Silver too.

B. Bull / Bear Flags (trend continuation) (learning-bear-flags-live, every-trade-i-took-on-a-bearish-nq-night, i-tried-to-trade-the-news)

  • Establish macro trend first ("the macro trend was clearly moving down").
  • Flag forms after an impulse; confirmation = price fails to take out the prior high/low, then breaks and closes outside the flag structure.
  • Enter on the break or on a pullback; ride 10–20 points ("base hits"); drop to 1-min for flags and use a VWAP break as confirmation.
  • Bigger consolidation inside the flag = bigger breakout expected.
  • In strong trends (price entirely below 9 EMA + 21 EMA + VWAP): "your job is simply to trade the flags and let the momentum work for you" (every-trade-i-took).

C. Chop Box / Range Scalping (dont-start-scalping-until-you-watch-this, how-i-trade-chop)

  1. Identify the range on the 5-min chart: multiple touches required (example: 3 distinct taps at the lows, 5 touches at the top) — "when price touches the top of a range five times and refuses to break out… it is going to keep consolidating."
  2. Execute on the 1-min chart: buy the bottom line, sell the top line.
  3. Strict 10-point target (~$200/contract) on every scalp; example run: 5 scalps = $1,000 in an hour.
  4. Stop: right outside the wick/boundary; if price breaks and closes outside the box, take the -10pt loss and stop — example: 5 wins + 1 loss still +$800 for the day.
  5. Repeat until the range fails. In chop he'd rather not trade at all: "The absolute easiest way to trade chop is to not touch a button" (how-i-trade-chop).

D. The Globex Strategy (first candle of the Globex session) (i-lost-accounts-for-the-first-time-in-months, i-broke-every-rule-i-have-today)

  • Watch the very first 5-minute candle of the Globex session: closes red → short, closes green → long; "That is the only confluence you need for this specific setup" (learned from a mentor).
  • Hard stop on the other side of that initial candle (examples: risk ~$350 targeting $700; risk ~$1,600).
  • Trailing rule: "trail your take-profit by two units of risk" — i.e., trail the stop in increments of 2R; in expansion markets this produced a potential $13,000 runner (he banked $1,000 early to secure a payout).
  • If held through the NY open during a trend day, risk $350 → "$10,000" (i-broke-every-rule).

E. EMA-Retest Trend Entries (i-trade-one-account-at-a-time, one-long-turned-my-worst-morning-green, every-trade-i-took)

  • In a trend, wait for the pullback to the 9 EMA and enter on the rejection with a target at the next liquidity level (Asia Low, London High, PDH/PWH, VWAP).
  • Bias flip rule: don't reverse on a single poke — "You want to see the candle close securely above the 21 EMA and the VWAP to confirm the trend is real"; entering between the EMAs is "trickery." A 5-min candle closing above both EMAs followed by the 9/21 cross = momentum flipped to bulls.
  • Targets set at mapped levels (29,563 low, 29,600 flat, London High, PDH/PWH "liquidity pool").

F. Level / Liquidity Trading (how-to-trade-kill-zones-like-a-pro)

  • Session highs/lows and previous day/week highs/lows act as "draws on liquidity" — used as take-profit magnets (long into NY AM High, London High, PDH/PWH) and stop hiders ("place my stop loss just below a previously tagged level… if price drops back below that level, the setup is dead").
  • A level that gets tagged is "dead" — stop watching it. Untagged levels keep extending until hit. He uses the indicator's hit-rate stats (e.g., "Asia Low has a 78% hit rate over the last 41 sessions").
  • Example sequence: short after Asia Low tag (sellers exhausted) → long to NY AM High → long (bull flag) to London High → long to PDH/PWH.

G. Three-Bar Reversal (news/panic bottoms) (why-i-shouldnt-have-traded-election-night)

  • At the absolute bottom of a violent dump, the three-bar reversal printed: "risking about $1,000 on your stop loss… if you held that long all the way to the London high… you could have made $20,000."

H. MOC (Market-on-Close) Trade (how-i-caught-these-3-futures-setups)

  • Unconventional: reads the 3:50 PM candle and mirrors the direction of the 9:30 AM opening candle into the close; example short at 29,123 → out 29,093 (+30 pts, $600).

6. Exits, Profit Taking & Trailing

  • Base hits: 10–20 points is the standard scalp/flag target ("locked in 10 points ($200)"; "rode this one up for 20 points ($415)").
  • 1:1 RR on reversal setups (2ITC); 2:1 on the Globex setup ($350 risk → $700 target).
  • Trail by two units of risk in trending/expansion markets; he had tightened trails during a choppy summer and switched back when expansion returned (i-lost-accounts).
  • "Take the money and walk away" when momentum stalls: "if you see momentum stalling and price starting to come back against you, take the money and walk away… Never let a solid green trade turn red out of greed" (why-most-traders-fail).
  • Manual cut-downs are acceptable risk management: cut a 20-point stop early to save capital (-$200 instead of -$400) (lost-900); bailed manually when a runner started pulling back instead of "letting hope take over" (i-made-2000-trading-the-asia-open).
  • Secure-payout exits: took $1,000 off a potential $13,000 runner because the IQ Capital payout mattered more that day (i-lost-accounts).

7. How He Handles Chop vs Trend Days

  • Chop/range: (1) best option — don't trade, wait for breakout; (2) if trading, play only the extreme boundaries for 10–20 pt base hits. Consolidation = "building energy for a breakout"; the eventual break direction is the signal: a downtrend that consolidates then breaks up = reversal, an uptrend that consolidates then breaks down = reversal (how-i-trade-chop). Wicks through a level that don't close beyond it = still valid support (wicked below support "but did not close below it").
  • Trend/expansion days: trade flags with the trend all session, use 2R trailing, let runners develop (every-trade-i-took-on-a-bearish-nq-night; i-lost-accounts).
  • His honest self-assessment: "I hate playing the chop! I know everybody says 'the range is your friend,' but the range is not my friend" (i-broke-my-own-rules-and-it-worked).

8. Scaling & The Multi-Account Business Model

  • Trade copier across 20+ funded accounts multiplies every setup: one 50-pt NY-open long produced ~$19,000 gross across firms (Apex: 2 contracts × 5 accounts = $10,000; Lucid, Legends, IQ, Tradeify additions) (one-long-turned-my-worst-morning-green).
  • One account at a time (his default discipline): "I trade one account at a time. I don't always blast the trade copier across all my accounts at once. I cycle through them to manage risk and keep my focus sharp" (i-trade-one-account-at-a-time). Copier used selectively (e.g., only for the highest-conviction opening short).
  • With many accounts and the one-trade-per-account rule, he aims for about five total trades a day, prioritizing accounts closest to a payout or eval pass (one-trade-per-account-per-day).
  • Known scale plan: pass evals (often in 1 day where rules allow), build buffer, request payouts, repeat — "Small wins, lots of accounts, and compounding" turned ~$4,992 of eval fees into $67,468 of payouts in one quarter (what-i-spent-on-prop-firms).
  • Risk isolation: when he wanted to gamble a news release, he bought a separate single account instead of copying it, so the main portfolio stayed green (i-tried-to-trade-the-news).

1. Discipline Is a Daily Fight, Not a Trait

  • "Honestly, discipline is something you have to actively fight for. If you aren't mentally prepared to go into a session and accept losing money, you aren't going to be disciplined. And if you aren't mentally prepared to sit in your chair for hours doing absolutely nothing while waiting for an A+ setup, you aren't going to make it."   • He is explicit that he is not perfect: "Am I 100% perfect at discipline? No. I have blown accounts, and I have even passed evaluations and blown the funded account on the exact same day. It happens to everybody."
  • His 3 Pillars of Trading Discipline:
    1. Dial in your risk-to-reward — a profile that fits your personality and account size; respect the stops.
    2. Master ONE strategy — "Stop strategy-hopping. Stick to a setup you have been practicing for months or years… only execute when your exact criteria show up."

Key rules and strategy from videos

1. The chop-box / range scalping system (his signature scalping strategy)

  • Everyone on YouTube posts scalping strategies; the one Denny actually runs on NQ: find a chop box on the 5-minute, buy the lows, sell the highs, take 10 points each time. Five of those is $1,000 off a single range.
  • He acknowledges the spectrum of scalping ("two points for $40, micro scalping, 10 or 20 point scalps") but his preference is: find a range, work it until it breaks.
  • IDENTIFYING THE RANGE: multiple taps at the high, multiple taps at the low. Nov 2 example: three taps at the lows, five at the top — "five touches at the top is the tell" that it isn't ready to break out and will keep consolidating. That's your window.
  • Mark the box from the top of the wick to the bottom of the wick, on the 5-minute chart.
  • TIMEFRAMES: marks ranges on the 5 minute and ONLY the 5 minute ("the best timeframe to find them and keep them clean"). Suggests doing both — mark the range on the 5 minute, then drop to the 1 minute to watch behavior at the touches and time entries.
  • EXECUTION (Nov 1 example, counted live on the 1-minute): Sell 83 → 73 = $200. Buy 59 → 69 = $400. Sell 81 → 71 = $600. Buy 60 → 70 = $800. Sell 81 → 71 = $1,000. The sixth one fails — price breaks out and closes outside the box. That's the signal to stop. Even eating the 10-point loss on the last scalp, the day finishes +$800.
  • THE END: every range breaks eventually. You can try to buy the bottom with a stop under the wick, but on a break like this you get taken out. "The edge isn't in catching the breakout — it's in working the range while it's working, and stopping on the first candle that closes outside the box."
  • CORE INSIGHT: "Scalping a range isn't one great trade. It's the same ordinary trade, taken five times, until the range tells you it's over."

2. The Globex strategy (learned from a mentor)

  • Entry rule: the FIRST five-minute candle of the session. Closes red → go short. Closes green → go long. "That's the only confluence it needs."
  • Stop at the top of the candle; risk a little over $350, target $700.
  • Result Oct 15: hit — $500 on one account, $700 on the one paying out. "If you'd held it through New York this morning, it never stopped going down."

3. One trade per account per day — walk away win or lose

  • After blowing two BluSky accounts in one night (started copy trading, taking multiple contracts, chasing what everyone else at the desk was doing): "One trade per account, per day. Win or lose, that account is done."
  • He trades one account at a time and waits for his setup. He has 20+ accounts — if running several: maybe five trades a day, one per account, and work whichever account is closest to a payout or a pass. Then close everything.
  • Before BDH, Denny traded one or two hours a day and that was it.
  • WHY: "Overtrading is gonna lead to losing the account, and losing the account is gonna lead to being in a bad mental state." A bad mental state the next day can cost the rest of the accounts.
  • CORE: "Up, walk away. Down, walk away. Come back tomorrow." "The market will still be here tomorrow."

4. Never trade without a stop loss (what losing two accounts taught him)

  • Lost his Lucid LIVE account (inside two days) and a personal $3,000 live account (his own payout money) about two weeks prior — "full tilt mode."
  • He was up $1,000 on Lucid live, said out loud he was done for the day, then took one more trade in a perfectly trending market — and it went straight down. "And you know what I didn't do, which is the number one rule? Put a stop loss. I did not put a stop loss, and it cost me a lot."
  • Cost: the Lucid account, the personal account, and two weeks unable to trade Lucid at all.
  • On $2,000-drawdown live accounts: the number is the same as a 50K eval, but "the mindset isn't." Take it slow — micros only, five micros max.
  • CORE INSIGHT: "Saying you're done is not the same as being done. The stop loss is what enforces it when you're not strong enough to."

5. Trading chop: the two options

  • Chop = range = consolidation, one condition. You're waiting for the breakout either way.
  • A wick through the level is NOT a break; a CLOSE through it is. (Asia/Globex example wicked below the level and never closed below it for three straight hours.)
  • Option one: don't trade. "There's no obligation to be in the market while it's ranging." Denny says it twice and means it.
  • Option two: buy low, sell high — every time price returns to the level and doesn't break it, ride it back. Go for 10 to 20 points, take the scalp, get out every time.
  • Works on short ranges too: a ~15-minute London consolidation pre-open where every 5-minute candle was a buy or a sell — the only way to lose was to buy the top and sell the bottom, which is exactly what happens without the box drawn.
  • He shows his own mistake: bought the top of the range on a "new low = continuation higher" read, chopped for almost an hour. "If you know how to play the range, you're fine. If you don't, just don't trade. Both are correct answers."

  • Perps, not CME futures: equities/indices/crypto/commodities/FX from one cross-margin account, up to 50x leverage; profits claimed to a Primary Account then withdrawn as USDC.
  • Evaluation tiers (static 6% max drawdown, non-trailing): Silver $5K — 3% daily = $150, DD $300, target 10% = $500. Gold $10K — daily $300, DD $600, target $1,000. Platinum $25K — daily $750, DD $1,500, target $2,500.
  • Bespoke 1-step: pick profit target (10%/20%), daily loss limit (3%/4%/none), split (80%/90%), tier ($5K/$10K/$25K). Denny's own setup: no daily loss limit, 10% target, 90% split — the only thing that can close the account is the $1,500 max drawdown.
  • Instant accounts: 95% flat split; payment = max drawdown exactly ($500→$10 DD 2%; $5K/$10K/$25K → $200/$400/$1,000 DD at 4%).
  • KEY MECHANIC: claims are removed from the account instantly and are irrevocable — once claimed, the money is no longer at risk even if the account later breaches (he lost one account after already taking payouts; payouts still went through). Unlimited claims per day — he took four.
