## The Path — From Hope to Structure

- **2019, the spark.** A software engineer at a top U.S. tech firm; a manager's advice sticks — "use your 20s to build wealth outside of work." Trading starts as a side project.
- **2020–2023, the expensive years.** Options on SPY/QQQ names first, then futures from around 2022. A 2020 bull run made pullback-buying look easy — "it was not really a strategy." He had big wins and gave them back, blew accounts "trading hope instead of structure", and calls the personal capital he lost **"market tuition. The goal is to pay as little as you can before you figure it out."**
- **ICT, then order flow.** He spent about a year and a half on ICT concepts without "a single dollar I could call mine" — a framework that explained every move in hindsight and got chopped up in real time. "You become a market historian instead of a market participant." The missing piece was confirmation in the moment, which order flow gave him.
- **What actually changed was rules, not setups.** The repeating pattern was a week or two of wins, then one red day where he kept trading and gave it all back. "I'm doing something wrong here. I have to create rules."
- **The reset.** He stopped forcing trades in illiquid markets and aligned the strategy with his personality. After a large drawdown in 2025 he stepped away for weeks, came back, and started journaling publicly on X for accountability.
- **Now: sim to live.** "Sim farming is dying. 2026 is about building foundations that transfer to live markets." The goal is several live funded accounts and a verified live track record.

## His Four Operating Principles

1. **Detachment — "P&L is a byproduct of execution."** Don't depend on trading income; judge the session by the process, not the number.
2. **Consistency — "Results only show up in hindsight."** Trust repetition and the data, not how one day felt.
3. **Defined Risk — "Set before the open. Never renegotiated."** Max daily loss and size are fixed before the session starts.
4. **Full Transparency — wins and losses, in public.** Both sides of the ledger get posted, not just the payouts.

## Eleven Lessons From Seven Years

1. Every day is a new day.
2. Consistency over big days.
3. Most traders fail from overconfidence and over-leverage.
4. Markets change. Your risk has to change with them.
5. You will lose. Cap the damage.
6. The market reflects your habits.
7. When you think it's time to scale up, you're probably wrong.
8. Respect the small wins.
9. Money won't fix your life. People will.
10. "I need to make $X today" is when you've lost.
11. If you can't accept losing, quit trading.

## Accepting the Loss Before It Happens

- **The mental side is about 80% of trading.** Any strategy works if the mental side is right; none does if it isn't.
- **Accept the worst case before the bell.** "My mind has already accepted… worst case that's what happens. I'm not coming into every day thinking I'm going to win."
- **Risk definition is the antidote to loss pain.** "If you take away one thing from the streams… it's what I do in the beginning of the day, where I define my risk."
- **Good losses vs bad losses.** A good loss is the one you planned; a bad loss is when you risk too much and lose more than you meant to.
- **Learning to lose is the core skill.** "Make losing your friend… As long as you know what your risk is per trade, per day, you'll survive." He will happily be red on win rate and green on the day — a 2R plan only needs half its trades to work.
- **Red days are pullbacks, not trends.** He reframes his equity curve as a stock chart: "That red day… is just a pullback. How do you make it just a pullback, not the start of a downtrend?"
- **Surviving counts as winning.** "As long as you don't blow your account, you survived to fight another day. You don't have to make money every single day to count as a win."
- **"My weapon is risk management."** Even trading badly, he stays in the game because the downside is capped.

## Expectations Kill More Traders Than Risk

- **"Most prop traders don't fail from risk, they fail from expectations."** Needing a number is the warning sign: "If I'm coming into the day, I'm like, I need to make this much to get a payout. It's like a bad sign. Every time I've done it in the past, that's when I've had a red day."
- **"The second you tell yourself 'I just need $500 in the next 5 days' that's when the spiral begins."**
- **No payout goals.** Payouts "just happen as a result of trading".
- **Don't look at the P&L.** He switches the display to ticks when teaching, because staring at dollars drives emotional decisions.
- **Success is what you keep.** "I don't really tie success to how much money you made. I tie success in trading to how much money you can keep."

## Tilt, Overconfidence and Patience

- **You can't prevent tilt — you can control it.** Limit trades and spending so a bad day can't spiral. "Tilt is one of the most dangerous things… if you don't cap your downside, that is unlimited."
- **"Let me try one more trade" is the account killer.** The urge to re-enter immediately after a loss is revenge trading — "this is why most people lose."
- **Overconfidence leads to oversizing.** "When you oversize that one time, you lose everything." After big wins he remembers past give-backs and scales slowly.
- **Patience is the #1 skill once you have a working strategy.** "People have a working strategy and they are not patient enough for it to develop."
- **"A lot of trading is just waiting."** "If the opportunity shows up, execute. If it doesn't, just wait. Just do anything other than press buy or sell."
- **Controlled FOMO.** "There will be another day. There will be another opportunity."
- **"If you look at chop for too long, it starts to look good."** He writes "this is chop — do not get involved" in his notes to stop himself.
- **Know when to stop and when to press.** "That's the mindset that all successful day traders have: they know when to stop trading. They know when to press." When reads are sharp and drawdown in trades is small, he presses; when anxious, he stops.

## Readiness, Review and Breaks

- **Head space is part of the risk.** Sleep, anxiety and profit targets are checked before the open. "If you don't have the right head space coming in the day, you essentially have so much risk. You don't realize it."
- **Mandatory end-of-day review**, win or lose — "what did I miss?" He journals in Notion, writing down what he thinks in the moment, and rewatches his own streams to compare his live read against hindsight. Hindsight doesn't count; only what you saw in the moment.
- **Breaks are underrated.** After a red week or a big loss he takes days to weeks completely away from the markets. "Your strategy didn't stop working — your confidence took a hit."
- **Screen time is not always good.** He trades the morning and leaves — trading is supposed to be freedom, not another 9-to-5.
- **Outside income buys patience.** Passive income from property means he doesn't need to trade every day, which makes waiting for the setup easy. His advice: get two to three years of consistency and outside income before going full-time, and "if you have debt, do not trade."

## Treat Props Like a Business

- **Think like an operator, not a gambler.** Revenue = payouts; expenses = evals and activation fees; inventory = funded accounts; working capital = a cash buffer for bad months; risk management = daily loss caps, drawdown buffers and sizing rules.
- **"A losing trade is not a personal failure. It's just a regular Tuesday at the office."** A winning day is the job working as designed.
- **Pay yourself a fixed amount.** The rest goes to taxes, eval reinvestment and savings — no lifestyle inflation off payouts.
- **Build a skill that transfers to live markets.** Props can change or disappear; a sim-only edge (Asia scalps at prop size, max-drawdown gambles) won't survive a real order book.

## Learning Order Flow

- **You can't backtest order flow.** It has to be watched live — market replay at most — until your eyes are trained. "It will feel like you understand it, but when you go apply it, you're like, what am I looking at?"
- **Expect months, not weeks.** He learned it within about six months; give any new method at least three months before judging it.
- **Simplify.** Too many indicators made him wait for too much confluence and miss trades; he dropped footprint, Bookmap and cumulative delta.
- **No signals, no copying.** "Signals are a short-term band-aid." Take pieces of someone's approach, test them, and build your own confirmation — "confirmation is built on confidence."
- **Learning to lose comes first, P&L later.** "Focus on learning a system and the money will come after."
- **Favorite books:** *Trading in the Zone* and *Best Loser Wins* — "what seems high can go higher, what seems low can go lower."
