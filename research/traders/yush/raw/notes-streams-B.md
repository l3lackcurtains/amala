## 1. Pre-market routine & level marking

**Purpose & format**
- A pre-market plan covers: higher-time-frame levels, the zones he marks on lower time frames, why each one matters, and if/then scenarios ("if this holds I want to be long; if we get below, sellers can take control") (pCmrsXoHFiM [01:40]).
- He normally does this plan in the Discord before trading live, or as a video or written post when he isn't streaming (pCmrsXoHFiM [02:20]).
- Levels also come from a Sunday "market review" stream in the Discord; on Monday he explains those boxes rather than redrawing them (7dTSRKTjlac [06:20], [38:20]).
- Pre-market streams start around 8:15–8:30 a.m. ET and finish just before 9:00 (j_70jtO38Uo [05:40], [57:00]; pCmrsXoHFiM [52:40]; 7dTSRKTjlac [14:40]).
- He uses one chart template for pre-market and builds on it; no separate template (j_70jtO38Uo [22:40]).
- Levels are drawn by hand, not auto-updated (7dTSRKTjlac [24:40]).

**Step 1: read the prior days' RTH ranges and their sequence**
- Starts with RTH-only data and measures recent day ranges. Example sequence: a 173-pt trend day (Fri), a 73-pt consolidation (Mon, "about half"), a 243-pt follow-through day (Tue), then a 128-pt inside day (Wed) (pCmrsXoHFiM [02:40]–[03:40]).
- Rule from that pattern: after an inside day, watch for follow-through out of the inside day's RTH high or low. Then turn overnight data on to check whether the overnight session already broke out; on that day it was "in the middle of yesterday" (pCmrsXoHFiM [04:00]–[04:40]).

**Step 2: identify the balance range and anchor profiles**
- Find the multi-day/multi-week consolidation (e.g. a ~2-week, ~120-pt ES range) and draw a volume profile over it. After a breakout, anchor a new profile from where the move started, "taking the edge of this RTH profile and dragging it all the way to this low", to see where new value forms (pCmrsXoHFiM [19:00]–[20:40]).
- Explaining his anchoring: "we were trading in this range, I drew the profile here and then we broke out. I anchored a new profile" (7dTSRKTjlac [08:40]).
- He also anchored one profile across the last week of May and first week of June (a consolidation range), and a new one after price gapped above last week's VAH (j_70jtO38Uo [28:40]–[29:40]).
- When price comes back inside an older range (e.g. the rollover week's value), that week's profile becomes relevant again: "we would want to put emphasis on that rollover week value area and volume profile… because we're back in that range" (9aNP10gBRb4 [44:40]–[46:20]).
- His intraday volume profile starts at RTH "cuz that is the only session I really trade" (pCmrsXoHFiM [06:00]). It is anchored to the 9:30 a.m. ET open (9aNP10gBRb4 [99:20]).
- His previous-day VPOC on the plan chart includes Globex (j_70jtO38Uo [06:00]).
- He uses default volume profile settings (VAH/VAL): "there's no special magic tool setting that I'm using that I'm gatekeeping" (j_70jtO38Uo [17:40]–[18:20]).

**Levels he marks (as listed in these plans)**

## 2. Setup criteria & entry triggers

**Environment first**
- Three environments (up, down, sideways/balance). He built his two models to cover them: trade edges in balance and pullbacks in trend, "because it keeps me on the higher probability side… whoever is dominating the market" (TWxuWaydoJY [58:40]–[59:00], [61:00]–[61:40]).
- "I have at least one opportunity each day… I'm a scalper… there will at least be one opportunity" (TWxuWaydoJY [61:40]–[62:00]).
- Strong trend means no counter-trend: "There's nothing that tells me short in this trend up… I'm looking for pullbacks to position myself long" (TWxuWaydoJY [07:40]); "We're not shorting all-time highs" (TWxuWaydoJY [34:20], [55:20]).
- "I never go long a momentum move" (pCmrsXoHFiM [43:40]). "I don't like to trade too many counter-trend… just trade the side that's winning" (9aNP10gBRb4 [75:20]–[77:40]).
- LBF/LAF are balance-only: "LBF and LAF on this type of day don't work because it's trending… the model is just based on price being in a range or in a balance" (9aNP10gBRb4 [57:20]–[57:40]).
- Higher time frame always wins. If a lower-time-frame entry conflicts with a higher-time-frame level, it is lower probability: skip it or size down. Example: he skipped an NQ long under higher-time-frame resistance and took the ES long at an LVN pocket instead (9aNP10gBRb4 [46:40]–[48:00]).
- ES/NQ divergence: "I like to be really cautious if both the indexes are diverging… likely stay on the sidelines… creates a chop fest and lack of follow-through" (j_70jtO38Uo [49:20]–[49:40]). If they diverge, go long the stronger index when strength confirms (7dTSRKTjlac [35:40]–[36:40]).

**LVN pullback (trend) model**
- What counts as an LVN: "you want to see a high volume node area and a breakout creating those low volume nodes, like significant amount of low volume pocket. I don't look at low volume pockets that are really small between two high volume areas" (7dTSRKTjlac [11:20]–[11:40]). The retest may not happen that day (7dTSRKTjlac [12:00]).
- Template: a volume cluster builds out, the breakout leaves LVNs, and the retest comes into those LVNs. Example: first-hour cluster, then breakout, then retest of an LVN that was also a GEX level (i5KLs6MsRtw [41:20]–[42:00]).
- Example from 9aNP10gBRb4 [14:40]–[15:00]: overnight high, quick sweep above it leaving an LVN, value forming higher; he entered long on a sweep under and reclaim, then trailed.
- Wants good R:R and won't chase: "If I enter here I feel like I'm chasing… if you get a nice decent pullback we get a good risk-reward, I think this area is much better" (TWxuWaydoJY [19:20]–[19:40]); "I think the RR is kind of skewed. I would like to see a little bit more of a pullback" (9aNP10gBRb4 [13:20]–[13:40]).
- Voided-idea rule: "if I had a planned trade and it goes to target without the entry, why would I still want to go back? Because now if it pulls back, things could change" (TWxuWaydoJY [39:00]–[40:00]).
- Example: he was 1.5–2 pts from his entry and didn't move the order up because he wanted price to come back first, calling it "ultimate discipline practice" (TWxuWaydoJY [49:00]).
- He holds the long only if the big trade at the LVN holds: "I want that to hold" (big trade at 523–524). The upside trigger is pushing through stacked negative delta plus a high-volume cluster at 540 (TWxuWaydoJY [72:20]–[72:40]).

**LBF / LAF (balance) model**
- LBF/LAF is "just a mean reversion setup at the end of the day". He created and named it from years of watching ES/NQ with volume profile, with no outside inspiration (9aNP10gBRb4 [97:40]–[98:40]).
- It doesn't need a clean straight-line rejection: "It doesn't always have to come straight down… we're looking for trapped participants" (9aNP10gBRb4 [57:20]).
- True breakout vs. failed one: after long compression a real breakout "should be really strong… shorts getting stopped out… we shouldn't even come back. We should be leaving low volume nodes on the breakout", or at most a small consolidation above VAH. A move up followed by a straight move back down is a failure sign. "This is me telling you from experience… not a set requirement" (9aNP10gBRb4 [58:00]–[59:00]).
- Clean LBF example: range forms, a breakdown attempt is confirmed by selling, then a quick buy-up traps the sellers and price goes to the other side of the range (7dTSRKTjlac [39:20]–[40:00]).
- Higher-time-frame LBF: value established above a level, sellers try to extend lower on thin Thursday-PM/Friday volume, then price gets bought back. If it holds above the level, it's a larger-time-frame LBF (7dTSRKTjlac [20:00]–[21:40]; j_70jtO38Uo [31:00]–[32:00]).
- LAF example: an opening drive above a level with lots of big trades, then price gets back under. He didn't short the first move back under aggressively; he "waited for a retest" and shorted that. Aggressive buyers and breakout traders were trapped (i5KLs6MsRtw [45:00]–[45:40]).

**Two entry styles at a key level (stated for both longs and shorts)**
- (a) Look-under / momentum flip: price looks under the level, sellers get trapped, momentum flips quickly and the level is reclaimed. This is the aggressive entry: size down and risk just below the level (j_70jtO38Uo [32:40]–[33:20]; pCmrsXoHFiM [23:40]; pCmrsXoHFiM [44:20]: "quick momentum flip… I'll be a little aggressive with the add, maybe size down").
- (b) Pullback and hold / retest: the more patient entry (pCmrsXoHFiM [23:40]).
- Which one he uses: "If there's momentum on a reclaim, I might have to be a little more aggressive cuz there might not be a retest… I keep in mind that I'm being aggressive, so I'll size accordingly… if I'm going super against the trend, I likely will wait for a retest" (j_70jtO38Uo [06:40]–[07:20]).
- For a long after a strong selloff, he wants selling momentum, consolidation, buying taking over, and then an entry on the retest (pCmrsXoHFiM [43:40]–[44:00]).
- Short side: "a deep extension, not a small one… and a retest and failure to get above", like a dead-cat bounce back to the level, or LVNs forming on the way down for confluence (j_70jtO38Uo [34:00]–[34:40]).
- First touch is not an automatic trade: "it doesn't necessarily mean the first time this area gets tested I'm looking for a long. It's all based on reaction… That is where order flow comes in" (pCmrsXoHFiM [24:00]).
- "It's not a blind long when it gets there. I want to see the reaction off it" (i5KLs6MsRtw [28:20]).
- Don't guess bottoms: if a key level gives way and price can't get back above, "even though it may look like there's some absorption happening here… you're just trying to guess a bottom… when price is clearly telling you that sellers are still in control" (pCmrsXoHFiM [26:00]–[26:40]).

**Overnight high/low traps**
- "A lot of traps happen near overnight highs and lows." Breakout traders position there and stops sit there. When the level trades, momentum picks up; aggressive opposite flow then flips it, traps the chasers and retraces at least partway, "to cause them pain for chasing" (j_70jtO38Uo [55:40]–[56:20]).
- He says this comes from "activity that I've noticed in the book where the big trade trigger", not a liquidity theory (j_70jtO38Uo [56:00]).
- Setup idea: an impulsive leg sweeps the overnight high, momentum stalls and traps buyers, then retracement back down (j_70jtO38Uo [55:20]).
