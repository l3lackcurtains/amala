## 2. Framework & level building

### The whole approach in one line
- He builds support/resistance zones from four tools, then executes with order flow: big trades, delta profile and "the speed that tape is moving" (hvyf6frvCcA [02:40]).

### Areas of interest (AOI)
- **The four AOI tools:** (1) market-generated levels, (2) volume profile, (3) big trades, (4) delta profile (hvyf6frvCcA [03:00], [08:40], [10:40]).
- **At least two must line up in an area** to make an AOI. All four is an A+ setup (hvyf6frvCcA [13:00]–[13:20], [39:20]–[39:40]).
- He calls two criteria "not an A+ setup". It can become A+ as more evidence stacks, for example repeated rejections with big trades and value shifting (hvyf6frvCcA [65:20]–[65:40], [72:20]).
- "If price never comes back, guess what? I don't trade." Patience while waiting for the area to be tested is a core skill (hvyf6frvCcA [13:20]–[13:40]).
- The same patterns can form new zones above or below the ones already marked (hvyf6frvCcA [13:40]).
- People think he has a "magic formula" for points of interest. He says he just uses the four criteria every time (hvyf6frvCcA [65:40]–[66:00]).
- **Absorption and trapped traders create the S/R levels** (hvyf6frvCcA [57:40]–[58:00]).
- Support can turn into resistance once big trades print there on the retest (hvyf6frvCcA [60:00], [60:40]).
- "Highs and lows are where a lot of stops are triggered." Overnight highs and lows are frequent reversal and trap points (hvyf6frvCcA [47:40]–[48:00], [69:20]).

### Tool 1: market-generated levels
- Previous day high and low, RTH only, because he only trades the New York session (hvyf6frvCcA [03:20]–[03:40]).
- **Overnight high/low: 9:00 p.m. to 9:29 a.m. EST** (hvyf6frvCcA [03:40]).
- **ORB high/low, using a 30-second ORB.** The idea comes from the pit days, and he got it from another ex-pit trader, "Pax Trader" (name unclear in captions). Price respects these levels on retests and shows a lot of order-flow activity there (hvyf6frvCcA [03:40]–[04:20]).
- Previous day close also counts. One ES short triggered "right at previous day close" (hvyf6frvCcA [51:00]).
- He uses RTH as 9:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. EST, matching TradingView's RTH/EXT filter (hvyf6frvCcA [04:40]–[05:00]).
- "These levels… without [a reaction] don't mean anything. I have to see a reaction" (hvyf6frvCcA [04:20]).

### Tool 2: volume profile
- The developing **RTH** volume profile for the day. He does not use a profile built from the Globex open (hvyf6frvCcA [05:20], [22:40]–[23:00]).
- The exception is overnight news, which he will pay attention to (hvyf6frvCcA [23:00]).
- **Value area = where 70% of volume transacted.** Inside value, buyers and sellers agree, nobody is aggressive, and it is fair value (hvyf6frvCcA [06:00]–[06:20]; 29HfHgBQQmA [06:20]).
- **LVNs** form when one side gets aggressive and leaves volume pockets between value areas. Trend days show many LVNs (hvyf6frvCcA [06:40]–[07:20]).
- **Day-type read:** if price trades inside value, he plays the edges (VAH/VAL), not the chop in the middle. If the day is printing LVNs, he looks for pullbacks into the value edge or the LVN (hvyf6frvCcA [07:20]–[07:40]).
- **Value migration is "a big part of my edge"**: "I can see where value is forming and position myself according to that." If value forms higher than earlier, price is accepting higher prices and buyers are stronger (hvyf6frvCcA [23:40]–[24:00], [71:00]–[71:20]).
- If price trades enough inside a range, value will shift up or down. A stagnant value area in the middle of yesterday's range means a choppy day where he waits for the edges (hvyf6frvCcA [71:20]–[71:40]).
- A **selling or buying tail** (no volume at the profile extreme) marks trapped traders. Profiles "like to trap at highs and lows" (hvyf6frvCcA [48:40]–[49:00], [66:40]).
- **Pre-market planning uses larger profiles.** He checks the weekly profile, the RTH profile (for example an RTH gap with no volume traded), the overnight profile's LVNs, and the previous day's VAH (hvyf6frvCcA [78:20]–[79:00], [80:40]).
- "Don't anticipate… Just react to what's taking place and position yourself according to that" (hvyf6frvCcA [08:20]).

### Tool 3: big trades
- He needs a platform with **tick data**. He says TradingView doesn't really offer it, so its big-trade readings aren't accurate (hvyf6frvCcA [08:40]–[09:00]).
- **Standard filters: NQ 75 lots, ES 200 lots.** He shows a bubble only when a print exceeds the filter (hvyf6frvCcA [09:40]–[10:00], [10:40]).
- When the book is thin (for example the April 2025 tariff period) he lowers them to **NQ about 50 and ES about 100** (hvyf6frvCcA [10:00]–[10:40]).
- Big trades use **aggregated orders, 100 ms**, which only shows in live markets, not in market replay (hvyf6frvCcA [35:00]).
- Big prints are not just 75s. He calls out 250, 300, 349/350 and 744-lot prints, and 1,000+ lot prints happen too (hvyf6frvCcA [40:40]–[41:00], [37:00], [69:40], [48:00]–[48:20]).
- He doesn't know the intent of a big print (new buyer, or a seller exiting). What matters is that if price goes back below it, that buyer feels pressure and gets liquidated (hvyf6frvCcA [49:20]–[49:40]).

### Tool 4: delta profile
- Delta = trades on the ask minus trades on the bid. The delta profile sums it by price level (hvyf6frvCcA [11:00]–[11:40]).
- Aggressive buyers hit the ask and aggressive sellers hit the bid (hvyf6frvCcA [11:40]).
- He looks for **absorption** and **trapped buyers/sellers** (hvyf6frvCcA [11:40]–[12:00]).
- **The delta profile resets every 30 minutes**, which shows what happened in each 30-minute window (hvyf6frvCcA [45:40]).
- **On the DOM he uses full-session delta** (hvyf6frvCcA [45:40]–[46:00]).
- He used to use Bookmap and footprint and doesn't anymore. Delta summarizes aggressive orders going through time and sales. "I don't care about a passive buyer or seller", because price reacting or pausing shows them (hvyf6frvCcA [64:20]–[64:40]).

### Timeframes
- **NQ on a 2-minute chart, ES on a 3-minute chart.** He doesn't wait for candle closes. Candles are only for structure (higher highs/lows, lower highs) and for trailing stops (hvyf6frvCcA [20:20]–[20:40], [63:00]–[63:20]).
- He used the 1-minute for both and found it noise (hvyf6frvCcA [62:40]–[63:00]).
- **Inconsistency:** in another interview he says his A+ setup checks structure on the **5-minute** (29HfHgBQQmA [33:20]).

### Sessions and times

## 3. Models & entry triggers

### Model 1: range or balance ("chop")
- Use it when price is stuck in a balanced range. It keeps him from trading the middle, where traders "get chopped up… bleed your account" because the risk parameters are skewed (hvyf6frvCcA [15:40]–[16:20]).
- Mark VAH and VAL of the volume cluster. "I'm not interested in the middle" (hvyf6frvCcA [16:20]–[17:00]).
- **Trigger at the value edge:** absorption, or aggressive buyers coming in at VAH "but there's not much follow-through" (hvyf6frvCcA [17:00]–[17:20]).
- **Look above/below and fail (LAF/LBF):**
  - It is a false breakout. Price looks above the range, buying comes in, then absorption follows "and then it quickly gets slapped down… momentum written one candle straight down back into value."
  - He has to react fast. "If you wait too long, you analyze too much, you end up missing that."
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [19:40]–[20:20], [43:20])
- **Short version:** sweep the overnight high, see large positive delta or a big bid with no progress, and price comes back inside the range. "That's how you trap buyers" (hvyf6frvCcA [47:40]–[48:20], [50:00]–[50:20]).
- **Long version:**
  - Price looks below the range, there is a strong push up, it comes back and holds support, then a higher low.
  - The sequence: "Low, higher low, look below, reclaim, other side of balance."
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [89:00]–[89:20])
- **Targets:** first the midpoint of the range or value, then the opposite edge of value (hvyf6frvCcA [18:20]–[18:40], [51:20], [73:00]).
- **Re-entry:** if a first Model 1 entry is stopped out and price retests the edge again, he will re-enter, "it meets the criteria". What matters is how it reacts there (hvyf6frvCcA [19:20]–[19:40]).
- On a day that stays balanced he keeps shorting each return to VAH "until I get stopped out" (hvyf6frvCcA [72:40]–[73:40]).
- Example of a two-criteria zone (opening-range low plus a big trade that got trapped): a quick look-up and then "within a second, straight down" told him the buyers were trapped (hvyf6frvCcA [65:00]–[65:20]).
- If he could keep only one setup, it would be "look above and fail… a false breakout or false breakdown" (3SoMtHNUeNU [43:20]–[43:40]).

### Model 2: trend
- A trend profile shows value established, a breakout leaving a small cluster or LVN, then a bigger cluster building higher. "Buyers clearly won" and price is accepting higher (hvyf6frvCcA [22:40]–[23:40]).
- While price consolidates above, he treats it like a bull flag (hvyf6frvCcA [24:00]–[24:20]).
- **Entry:** price pulls back into the LVN or the edge of the new value. He wants buyers to get aggressive there: big trades, sellers trapped, lots of orders hitting the bid "and then price not going down. All of a sudden you see a spike" (hvyf6frvCcA [24:40]–[25:00]).
- A look-below-and-fail into the LVNs that comes back into the range and holds is his confirmation entry (hvyf6frvCcA [25:00]–[25:20]).
- On a breakout it's "we let it break out, it creates a low volume pocket, and we try to enter in those low volume pockets" (hvyf6frvCcA [47:20]).
- Value doesn't always shift on pullbacks. After hours of consolidation there's a big cluster, and a very aggressive side may not give a deep pullback. That strength comes from new buyers plus squeezed shorts, "double the fuel" (hvyf6frvCcA [25:40]–[26:20]).
- **Liquidation into an LVN, example:**
  - The 9:30 open drops hard through the overnight low (new selling plus triggered stops), leaving an LVN. A 300-lot big trade prints with negative delta.
  - That is two confirmations. On the retest it becomes four (level, delta, big trades, LVN), "A+".
  - Short around the retest with the stop just above the overnight low. TP1 is the VAL or structural low.
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [36:00]–[40:20])
- When one side is very aggressive he may enter in a way that looks like chasing, using smaller size or a tight stop. Otherwise "you either have to wait hours… the day's done" (hvyf6frvCcA [37:40]–[38:40]).
- **Bearish trend:** the same rules mirrored, a break of balance then a retest-rejection for continuation (hvyf6frvCcA [31:00]; [85:20]–[85:40]).

### Absorption
- A support example: aggressive sellers hit the bid with lots of negative delta at a price, but price doesn't move. A passive buyer is consuming the orders, which confirms the level (hvyf6frvCcA [12:00]–[12:40]).
- At highs: large positive DOM session delta (800, 500, 700, 1,829…) near highs with price stuck means a passive seller is absorbing (hvyf6frvCcA [46:00]–[46:40]).
- An absorption pattern can also be called an **iceberg** (hvyf6frvCcA [55:40]).
- **Two ways to play it:**
  - An aggressive sell stop below, because absorption can break and liquidate.
  - Or wait for a lack of follow-through and a reclaim of the zone, which is a Model 2 defense.
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [55:40]–[56:20])
- "More selling came in, but price is not going down… some new buying came in." He enters there with the stop just below the wick low (hvyf6frvCcA [56:40]–[57:00]).
- **Failed absorption:** once buyers are trapped above, "those low volume nodes don't matter now". The level that held flips to resistance (hvyf6frvCcA [58:20]–[58:40]).
- Absorption on NQ example: 100–200 lots hitting the offer and price is stuck, so a passive seller is there. "Market moves based on who's aggressive" (3SoMtHNUeNU [29:20]–[29:40]).

### Trapped traders
- Trapped traders create the move. "If there's trapped buying or selling… the other side is going to cause max pain and stop them out" (hvyf6frvCcA [67:20]–[67:40]).
- NQ "loves to trap" buyers and sellers. That's why he doesn't trade breakouts. Trading NQ breakouts is "how I lost the most money" when starting (hvyf6frvCcA [67:40], [69:00]).

### Aggressive vs confirmation entries
- **Aggressive entry:** enter at the edge on absorption, sized much smaller with a tight stop, "because there's a higher chance I'm going to be wrong here… I'm going to know if I'm wrong quickly" (hvyf6frvCcA [17:20]–[17:40]).
- **Confirmation entry:** after the look-above-and-fail rejects back into the range. He either adds there or uses it as the first entry (hvyf6frvCcA [17:40]–[18:20], [25:20]).
- If the area is very high-probability ("tons of big trades… a lot of absorption") he will enter full size at the aggressive spot (hvyf6frvCcA [25:40]).
- **Squeeze-play entry (live):**
  - A buy-stop above a level that was rejected repeatedly, used when price holds support, forms a higher low and big trades are present.
  - He sized 3 contracts "cuz this is a little bit risky".
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [86:40]–[88:00], [88:40])
- **A+ setup definition:**
  - The level aligns with a higher-timeframe bias or S/R.
  - The order-flow reaction is right in real time, with big trades in the area and the right structure.
  - He starts with a smaller position, then adds on a lower-timeframe higher low.
  - (29HfHgBQQmA [33:00]–[33:40])
- An A+ setup "doesn't happen every day" (3SoMtHNUeNU [03:40]).
- His entry criteria are simple, and decisions take seconds: "within a second or I'd say 5-second window… or you're going to miss the optimal entry" (29HfHgBQQmA [08:00]–[08:20]; 3SoMtHNUeNU [10:00]).

### What invalidates or keeps him out
- **Never short into upside momentum, and never long into downside momentum.** He uses the DOM to watch tape speed and waits for it to slow: "I don't want to get in front of a moving train" (hvyf6frvCcA [45:00]–[45:40], [66:00], [70:20]–[70:40]).
- "When they squeezed up like that, there was no criteria… Why am I going to enter?" Entering without confirmation "just causes a tilt" (hvyf6frvCcA [70:20]–[70:40]).
- **No breakout trading**: "I hate trading breakouts or breakdowns because… horrible risk reward. I want to sell into these type of moves" (hvyf6frvCcA [18:40]–[19:00]; [79:40]).
- No trading in the middle of the range or value (hvyf6frvCcA [07:40], [16:20], [51:40]).
- **Trend exit signal:** a lower high on the 2-minute in an uptrend, especially one that forms on big trades, means "I'm out full" (hvyf6frvCcA [28:40]).
- Slow grinding action is "not a big fan". If the pre-market pivots aren't tested, "I'm okay not trading" (hvyf6frvCcA [83:40], [86:00]–[86:20]).
- If the zone isn't revisited he doesn't trade (hvyf6frvCcA [13:20]; 3SoMtHNUeNU [26:20]).
- **News rules:**
  - He is never in a position before news, and closes any open position "no matter what".
  - With news at 9:45 or 10:00 ET he doesn't trade that 30 minutes and trades after. News windows are often choppy and balance in a range.
  - (29HfHgBQQmA [52:20]–[53:00])
- Reading the book before news is "gambling". About 30 seconds before release the book thins out and visible liquidity 20–30 points away means nothing (29HfHgBQQmA [53:20]–[53:40]).
- He uses no fundamentals (29HfHgBQQmA [52:20]).
- **Chop awareness:** he writes "this is chop… do not get involved in this range" in his notes, because "if you look at chop for too long, it starts to look good" (3SoMtHNUeNU [25:40]–[26:20]).
- Reversal or counter-trend trading means a lower win rate and is hard with limited prop drawdown. Trade the edges in balance and pullbacks in trend instead (3SoMtHNUeNU [30:00]–[31:00]).

## 4. Stops, targets, trade management

### Stops
- **Stops are 15–20 NQ points, with targets of 40–50+**: "usually I'm looking for bigger moves, like at least 40–50 points… risking 15 to 20 points" (ps5In64-2So [87:20]–[87:40], speaker inferred).
- Live day: "Target at least 40–50 points risking 20. That's what I've been risking right now in this market" (hvyf6frvCcA [80:20]).
- Examples:
  - Risking 15 points for 60+ (hvyf6frvCcA [41:20]).
  - 40 points for 15 risked (hvyf6frvCcA [57:40]).
  - ES short risking 13 points (hvyf6frvCcA [82:20]).
- With a big buffer late in a day he still risks a static 15 points. "If I'm wrong, I'm done for the day" (hvyf6frvCcA [74:20]–[74:40]).
- **Stop placement:**
  - Just above the overnight low (the level broken) on Model 2 shorts (hvyf6frvCcA [39:40]).
  - Just above the previous-day close or the sweep candle on Model 1 shorts (hvyf6frvCcA [51:00]).
  - Just below the absorption wick low on longs (hvyf6frvCcA [57:00]).
  - Above the VAH on a wide range, trading micros because the stop is wider (hvyf6frvCcA [67:00]).
- **Wide ranges mean smaller size.** "I have a wider stop here because the range is really wide. So I'm trading micros in this case" (hvyf6frvCcA [67:00]).
- An NQ example: 2 minis risking about $1,000, about 25 NQ points (inferred from 2 × $20/pt; not stated). The trade made 1.7R in 30 seconds with a 20K buffer (29HfHgBQQmA [51:00]–[51:20]).

### Targets and R:R
- He usually targets at least 2R. His average is 2R. Some trades become 1.5R when he cuts early, and "a lot of my trades are about 3–4R" (29HfHgBQQmA [12:00]–[12:20]).
- **Aggressive entries** with tight stops usually come out around 3–4R. **Confirmation entries** usually target 2R (hvyf6frvCcA [27:20]–[27:40]).
- Model 1 targets: TP1 at the range midpoint or mid-value, TP2 at the opposite value edge, or another false break (hvyf6frvCcA [18:20]–[18:40], [51:20]).
- In a trend he takes profit into new highs or lows, selling into breakout traders and stops. "I can put a limit order. I'm going to get filled" (hvyf6frvCcA [27:00]–[27:20]).
- He exits with limit orders into the aggression (hvyf6frvCcA [83:00]).

### Break-even, trailing, partials
- **Once TP1 hits, the stop goes to break-even**, because "in chop, it can come back and tap you out" (hvyf6frvCcA [19:00]).
- **Core rule: never let a winning trade go red.** "If it stops me out… there'll be more opportunities, more days" (hvyf6frvCcA [19:00]–[19:20]).
- When adding on confirmation he may move the stop to break-even on the first entry (hvyf6frvCcA [18:20]).
- **Trend-day partials:** trim only a little at the high. With 5 contracts he trims 1–2, not half, then trails on 2-minute structure and watches where value is building (hvyf6frvCcA [28:00]–[28:40]).
- He always trims at the high, "because that's where a lot of fake-outs happen" (hvyf6frvCcA [28:00]).
- He trails stops using candle structure (lower highs and higher lows) on the 2- or 3-minute (hvyf6frvCcA [63:20]).
- On a big NFP short he got trailed out and still made about 1.7R after taking TP1 at value (hvyf6frvCcA [59:40]–[60:00]).
- **He knows he leaves money on the table.** "I leave a lot of profit on the table." In one example he caught 100 points of a 300-point move. Fixing exits is the area he's working on (hvyf6frvCcA [29:20]–[30:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [12:40]–[13:00]).
- He is building "a trailing model where I can hold on trades longer" for a live personal futures account. On props he prefers base hits because drawdown is limited (hvyf6frvCcA [30:20]–[30:40]).
- On consistency-rule firms he sometimes deliberately locks in a smaller win so he doesn't create a day that's too large (hvyf6frvCcA [43:20]–[43:40]).

### Adding to winners
- **Average up, never down:**
  - "A lot of traders average down. You should be averaging up, adding to a winning position when you get that confirmation."
  - "That's how you catch the big moves, and you press on your winners… where you make the big money."
  - (hvyf6frvCcA [17:40]–[18:00])
- He doesn't start aggressive. He adds as the trade works, for example half first and then the full position on a higher low (ps5In64-2So [137:20]–[137:40], speaker inferred).
- He learned to add using **micros**. On a higher low with big trades coming in, he adds and moves the stop to break-even. Low risk builds confidence with the technique (ps5In64-2So [139:00]–[139:40], speaker inferred).
- Live example: short 5 ES, with a plan to add 5 more if price rejects at the next level (hvyf6frvCcA [82:00]–[82:20]).

### Trades per day and holding time
- He averages 2–3 trades a day, and is "done within the first few hours" (3SoMtHNUeNU [04:20]–[04:40]; 29HfHgBQQmA [06:00]).
- "I'm only trading two or three times because I can just find these setups… This is all just chop" (hvyf6frvCcA [51:40]).
- **Scalper mentality:** he capitalizes as much as he can in the shortest time. He's usually in and out within 5–10 minutes at most, sometimes seconds (hvyf6frvCcA [28:40]–[29:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [50:40]–[51:00]).
- He used to trade much more and got emotional and attached to P&L. That is why he made the 2–3 trade rule (ps5In64-2So [87:40]).
- The trade limit is a patience tool. On a good day with buffer and a clear range he may take more trades. "But if you have a losing trade, that's when you stop" (29HfHgBQQmA [65:20]–[65:40]).

## 5. Risk & prop-firm business

### Daily loss and stopping rules
- **The rule that saved him the most money is the daily loss limit (DLL)** (3SoMtHNUeNU [43:20]).
- **He stops after 2–3 losing trades.** His trade sizing is set so 2–3 losers equal his personal DLL (3SoMtHNUeNU [05:00]–[05:20]; 29HfHgBQQmA [14:20]).
- **Daily risk comes first, and size follows from it.** He decides the worst case before the session (for example $500), then picks size (fewer than 5 micros). "First you set… the max loss… and then you base your sizing off that" (3SoMtHNUeNU [16:00]–[16:40]).
- **Worked example:** risk $2K today on this prop with a plan of 2 trades, so about $1K per trade (29HfHgBQQmA [64:20]–[64:40]).
- A personal DLL matters even when a prop doesn't require it. On a personal futures account, no DLL can mean a margin call in a day (3SoMtHNUeNU [38:40]).
- On props he uses a "soft breach" style DLL, not a hard breach. It forces him out in black-swan moves, like the recent 10% silver drop where "you just freeze up" (ps5In64-2So [35:00]–[35:20], speaker inferred).
- "If I have a red day with a particular prop, I don't trade that prop for a week" (3SoMtHNUeNU [01:20], [21:20]).
- **Conflicting cool-down rule:** in another interview the rule is "if I'm red, I'm not going to touch that account for 2 days" (29HfHgBQQmA [40:00]).
- The reasoning: "I'm more likely to get emotional with these accounts because I had a red day." He trades other funded accounts that aren't in drawdown, then later brings the drawdown accounts back up slowly with smaller size (3SoMtHNUeNU [21:20]–[21:40]; 29HfHgBQQmA [40:00]–[40:20]).

### Sizing and scaling an account
- **Start new accounts on micros.** Once over the trailing threshold with a buffer, he **adds one contract per $1,000 of buffer** (3SoMtHNUeNU [03:20]).
- On an A+ setup with buffer built he will size up, risking "like 20%" (unclear; probably 20% of buffer) (3SoMtHNUeNU [03:20]–[03:40]).
- **Leverage trap:**
  - A 50K account allows 5 minis or 50 micros on 2K of drawdown. Do that on a live account and on NQ "you'd get liquidated within minutes".
  - Trading minis on a 50K with a 2K drawdown is "gambling". It was a hot take on X.
  - (3SoMtHNUeNU [02:40]–[03:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [18:20]–[18:40])
- **Big days come from buffer.** He sizes in on high-volatility days (200–300 NQ-point ranges) only once a buffer is built (3SoMtHNUeNU [40:00]–[40:20]).
- **Confidence-based sizing:** he has rules to size down when confidence or performance is low and size up when it is high. A personal DLL anchors it (ps5In64-2So [05:00]–[05:20]).
- His tolerance depends on recent one-to-two-month performance. Up means he can risk a little more. Down for the month means "I'm not reading the markets correctly", so he sizes down (ps5In64-2So [130:00]–[130:40]).
- He is not a "go super heavy, try to get the yacht" trader. He's happy catching 100 points (ps5In64-2So [130:40]–[131:20]).
- "Overconfidence leads to oversizing more times than not. And when you oversize that one time, you lose everything" (29HfHgBQQmA [51:40]–[52:00]).

### Copy trading and number of accounts
- **No cross-firm copy trading.** He copies only across accounts at the **same** firm, never Apex with Tradeify, for example (3SoMtHNUeNU [20:20]–[21:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [39:20]–[40:00]; ps5In64-2So [45:40]–[46:00]).
- Why: if a cross-firm copy fails "you lose money on all those accounts". That brings tilt and the eval-spending spree (3SoMtHNUeNU [20:40]–[21:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [48:40]–[49:40]).
- He was funded at five prop firms at the time of the interview. He picks only one or two to trade on a given day, with risk parameters set by each one's buffer (29HfHgBQQmA [39:20]; 3SoMtHNUeNU [21:00]).
- With only 2–3 setups a day there's no need to trade every prop (ps5In64-2So [46:00]).
- For copying across his own accounts he uses Trade Copier ("trade of a" in captions, unclear) (ps5In64-2So [46:00], speaker inferred).
- His recurring example is 5 accounts, for example 6K across five accounts in one day (hvyf6frvCcA [73:00]).

### Eval strategy
- He doesn't rush evals. Most take at least 2–3 days. He doesn't use full leverage and doesn't need to hit the target on day one (29HfHgBQQmA [05:20]–[05:40]).
- Rushing evals builds habits that carry into the funded account, where you rush to the payout (29HfHgBQQmA [04:40]–[05:00]; 3SoMtHNUeNU [19:00]).
- **Roundtable contradiction:** "for evals I kind of size a little more aggressively… because I've made over 2 million… it's just a write-off." He sizes up as the buffer builds, and tries to pass "maybe five or 10" on news if the firm allows it (ps5In64-2So [67:20]–[68:00], speaker inferred).

### Consistency-rule vs no-consistency firms

## 6. Psychology & habits

### Mindset
- "Mental side of trading is… 80% of trading." You can trade any strategy if the mental side is right (3SoMtHNUeNU [08:00]–[08:20]).
- **Most prop traders fail from expectations, not risk.** "One reason most funded traders fail? False expectations" (3SoMtHNUeNU [01:40]–[02:00], [43:40]).

- "Never look at the P&L" (3SoMtHNUeNU [10:20]).
- **The fix: accept you can't win every trade, and accept the day's risk before the session.** "My mind has already accepted… worst case that's what happens. I'm not coming into every day thinking I'm going to win" (3SoMtHNUeNU [10:40]–[11:00], [16:00]).
- He never thinks about how much he'll make in a day, week or month. Needing a number for a payout is "a bad sign. Every time I've done it… I've had a red day" (29HfHgBQQmA [13:40]–[14:20]).
- He isn't scared to lose: "If I lose, okay. That's cool. I can wait for the next setup or call it and come back tomorrow" (hvyf6frvCcA [73:40]).
- On prop accounts he has "zero emotions" on losses. He jokes on stream and says "I suck". With props a loss is a business expense (29HfHgBQQmA [61:20]–[62:00]).
- **Good trade vs good outcome:** some red days he "loved the way I executed. It just wasn't my day". He also had a fill where he believes the market maker filled his stop at the tick extreme (29HfHgBQQmA [55:00]–[56:00]).
- "You can't prevent tilt… You can control it": control spending and trade count so tilt doesn't spiral (29HfHgBQQmA [45:40]–[46:00]).
- He stays grounded on hot streaks by remembering past give-backs (29HfHgBQQmA [51:20]–[52:00]).
- He struggles more with losses than wins. As a scalper, being wrong 2–3 times means a tilt mindset will take over, hence the trade rule (ps5In64-2So [107:00]–[107:20], speaker inferred).
- Losses are pullbacks: "If I don't have my losses, I'm not learning anything… a loss is just a pullback for new highs" (ps5In64-2So [122:40]–[123:00]).

- **Trading is boring when it works.** "I trade the same exact way for years. It's so boring, but there's no reason to change it cuz it works." Get excitement outside trading (29HfHgBQQmA [42:40], [43:00]–[43:20]).
- He doesn't compare himself to anyone: "I'm on my own journey… I'm fighting my mental space every day" (29HfHgBQQmA [37:40]–[38:00]).
- He frames market change as "a new puzzle that I'm solving" and calls it exciting (hvyf6frvCcA [21:40]).
- You need a model for every environment. Chop can last 3–4 months, and volatility keeps changing (hvyf6frvCcA [21:00]–[21:40]).
- A good trader is not very emotional about losses, sizes down when confidence drops, sizes up as it returns, and doesn't scale too quickly or dig too deep a drawdown (29HfHgBQQmA [63:20]–[64:00]).

### Advice to beginners
- Have rules. "If you don't have rules… you might get payouts, but you might give all that money… right back" (3SoMtHNUeNU [33:00]).
- **Minimum rules:** (1) a fixed daily risk accepted beforehand, (2) a trade-count limit (29HfHgBQQmA [64:00]–[65:20]).
- **Prop starter plan:**
  - One account with end-of-day or static drawdown, on micros only. Minis give a euphoria that becomes addictive.
  - Get it to payout, then reinvest only about 10–20% of the payout into 2–3 more accounts.
  - (29HfHgBQQmA [61:40]–[63:00])
- Start small and scale slowly. Learn the model with one or two accounts and don't copy trade (ps5In64-2So [145:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [68:00]).
- Don't compare yourself to others on X, YouTube or Instagram (ps5In64-2So [145:00]; 29HfHgBQQmA [68:00]).
- Survive: "The way to survive is not risk everything. If you survive long enough, I think you'll figure it out" (29HfHgBQQmA [48:40]).
- "If you have debt, do not trade" (29HfHgBQQmA [56:20]).
- Don't copy someone's strategy. Take pieces, backtest, and build your own entry confirmation (29HfHgBQQmA [42:00]–[42:40]; hvyf6frvCcA [52:00]).
- **Worst advice he's seen:** "risk your whole drawdown on your first trade". He says that's no different from a casino (29HfHgBQQmA [67:00]–[67:20]).
- **Best advice**, from Tom Hougaard's *Best Loser Wins*: what seems high can go higher and what seems low can go lower, so don't short a trend because it "looks expensive" (29HfHgBQQmA [66:00]–[67:00]).
- Favorite books are *Trading in the Zone* and *Best Loser Wins* (3SoMtHNUeNU [43:40]–[44:00]).
- **For a trader who lost everything:** take 1–2 weeks off, restart small with 1–2 accounts to rebuild confidence, get a payout, and use part of it for more evals (3SoMtHNUeNU [44:40]–[45:20]).
- Anyone can be a trader, but you must "rewire your brain" for the mental side (29HfHgBQQmA [56:00]).
