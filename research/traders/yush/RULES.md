## Level & Setup Rules

1. **An area of interest needs at least two of four tools.** Market-generated level, volume profile, big trades, delta profile. Two is tradable; three or four is A+.
   > "I need at least two to create what I call an area of interest. Like a support resistance zone."
2. **No return to the level, no trade.** Patience is part of the strategy; the setup doesn't get moved to where price is.
   > "If price never comes back, guess what? I don't trade."
3. **Never trade the middle of the range.** Only the edges of value (VAH/VAL) or the extremes of the larger range.
   > "You don't want to trade anywhere in the middle. You want to wait for the edges."
4. **React, don't anticipate.** Every trade needs confirmation at the level — the first touch is not an entry.
   > "Don't guess what's happening in the markets. Just react to what's taking place and position yourself according to that."
5. **Look for only two things at the level: absorption or trapped traders.** Aggression that gets no result, or aggression that gets reversed through.
6. **LBF/LAF is a balance-only model.** Don't fade edges on trend days.
   > "LBF and LAF on this type of day don't work because it's trending."
7. **In a trend, buy (sell) the pullback into the LVN — never the breakout.** Let it break out, let it leave a low-volume pocket, enter on the retest.
   > "I hate trading breakouts or breakdowns because I just have horrible risk reward."
8. **Never enter into momentum.** Wait for the slowdown, the reaction, then confirmation.
   > "I don't want to get in front of that. It's like I'm getting in front of a moving train."
9. **A reclaim is the trigger, not the sweep.** For an LBF long, enter on the close back above VAL — not while big prints are still hitting the bid, and not by chasing into the POC.
10. **If the planned trade runs to target without filling you, it's void.** Don't chase back in.
    > "If I had a planned trade and it goes to target without the entry, why would I still want to go back?"
11. **Don't short all-time highs.** Low probability; shallow pullbacks; grind risk.
    > "Don't short all-time high. That's the lesson… I faded my system."
12. **Higher timeframe always wins.** A lower-timeframe entry against a higher-timeframe level is skipped or sized down.
13. **Stay out or trade smaller when ES and NQ diverge.** Divergence = chop and no follow-through; when trading it, long the stronger index, short the weaker.
14. **Don't trade a value area too narrow to pay 2R.** Compression gets you wicked out.
15. **Two stops in the same area = done with that area.** Wait for a breakout or a break back inside before engaging again.
    > "Once you're wrong a few times in a range, just don't trade that range until you get out of it."

## Entry & Sizing Rules

16. **Aggressive entries get smaller size and a tight stop.** You find out quickly if you're wrong.
    > "With the aggressive entry, I'm always sized in much smaller, because there's a higher chance I'm going to be wrong here."
17. **Counter-trend ideas wait for the retest.** Only momentum reclaims with the trend justify an aggressive entry.
18. **Average up, never down.** Add only to a winner on a higher low / lower high or a reclaim.
    > "A lot of traders average down. You should be averaging up, adding to a winning position when you get that confirmation."
19. **Plan stop and target before every entry.** The biggest losses came from trades entered "in the moment".
    > "Every trade I take, I plan beforehand… That's when I take the biggest losses, because I just entered the moment without planning my stop loss and TPs."
20. **Micros on prop accounts until there is buffer.** Five micros max to start on a 50K.
    > "If you're trading minis on a 50K with a 2K drawdown, you're gambling."
21. **Scale one contract per $1,000 of buffer.** Size grows only from cushion, never from confidence alone.
22. **Wide stop means smaller size.** If an idea needs a stop wider than normal, switch to micros.
23. **Volatility changes the stop, not the risk.** VIX above ~20: widen stops and cut contracts so dollar risk stays fixed.
    > "You can't always have the same stop loss in all market conditions."
24. **Size down when confidence is down.** After a red day, start on micros and compound back; after a losing month, risk less.

## Stop, Target & Management Rules

25. **The stop sits where the idea is wrong.** Below the reclaimed edge, beyond the sweep low, above the high of the failed breakout — never an arbitrary number.
    > "When I have a stop, I usually have something overhead that needs to hold. If it doesn't hold, I deserve to get stopped out."
26. **Typical NQ risk is 15–20 points for 40–50+; ES usually 5 points or less.** Tighter (≈10) on clean absorption scalps.
27. **Plan for at least 2R.** At 2R, a 50% win rate is profitable.
    > "If you look for two R, you're profitable with a 50% win rate essentially."
28. **Model 1 targets: midpoint first, then the opposite edge of value.** Move the stop to breakeven when TP1 hits.
29. **Never let a winning trade go red.** Breakeven (a couple of ticks green) as soon as it moves — a "risk-free shot".
    > "I never let a trade that's working go red."
30. **On trend days, trim only 1–2 of 5 contracts at the high** and trail the rest on 2-minute structure.
31. **A lower high made on big trades in an uptrend = out in full** (mirror for downtrends).
32. **Cut early when it stalls.** If the level doesn't hold on the retest or the push is slow, scratch it.
33. **Re-entry is allowed only if the criteria are met again.** A stopped Model 1 trade can be retaken on a fresh retest of the edge — but not a third time in chop.

## Daily Risk Rules

34. **Define the day's maximum loss before the open, and never renegotiate it.** Size per trade comes from it.
    > "You have to define what you're willing to lose before the day starts."
35. **Daily risk is split into 2–3 losing trades. The last losing trade ends the day.** Emotion takes over after two or three losses, so that is the stopping point.
    > "When does my emotion start to get really factored in my trading? It's after two or three losses. So that's why I have that stopping point."
36. **2–3 trades a day.** Capping the number of attempts is the cure for overtrading.
    > "The only way to overcome overtrading is cap how many times you're going to trade in a day."
37. **Enforce the DLL yourself.** He dislikes relying on platform lockouts — the discipline has to be yours.
38. **No daily profit target.** Cap the downside, not the upside.
    > "Why are you capping your upside? That is the wrong mindset."
39. **Protect a green day.** A late, riskier trade is sized so that a loss still leaves the day green.

## Timing & News Rules

40. **Trade the NY morning only.** Roughly 9:30–12:00 ET; no Asia, no London.
    > "I don't think you really have an edge if you're trading Asia with ES or NQ."
41. **First 30 minutes: only the pre-marked higher-timeframe areas, and the plan doesn't change.**
    > "In the first 30 minutes I am only looking for price to trade around these areas. If it doesn't come, I'll just be sitting it out."
42. **Never hold a position into news.** Flat before scheduled releases and Fed speakers; trade the reaction afterwards, with no bias.
    > "I won't ever be in a position before news. If I'm in a position, I'll close it out no matter what."
43. **Out before the 3:50 PM MOC imbalance.** Don't hold into the close.
44. **If the plan's levels aren't tested by late morning, stop.** No trade is a valid outcome.
    > "No trade is a thing."

## Prop-Firm Rules

45. **No cross-firm copy trading.** Copy only across accounts at the same firm.
    > "I'm super against copy trading, cross-firm copy trading. It's too much risk at once."
46. **Trade only one or two firms per day** out of all funded accounts — there are only 2–3 setups anyway.
47. **A red day with a firm benches that firm.** Rest it (days to a week) and trade accounts not in drawdown; bring the drawdown account back later with smaller size.
    > "If I have a red day with a particular prop, I don't trade that prop for a week."
48. **Survive the first days of a funded account.** Smallest size, build buffer; most funded accounts die in the first two days.
49. **Trade end-of-day and trailing-drawdown accounts differently.** On trailing drawdown, take profits faster and only trade high-volatility days until clear of the trailing threshold.
50. **Don't take the maximum first payout.** Leave buffer — the real money comes on payouts two and three.
    > "The real money is made… the second to third payout."
51. **Cap monthly eval spend** (his is roughly $5–6K) and report payouts net of fees.
52. **Use consistency-rule firms for base hits, no-consistency firms for big-range days.**
53. **Wait six months before trusting a new prop firm.** Check payout complaints and support quality first; stick with established firms meanwhile.
54. **Beginners: one end-of-day or static-drawdown account, micros only,** reinvest only a slice of the first payout into more accounts.
