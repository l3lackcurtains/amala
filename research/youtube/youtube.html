Don't Start Scalping Until You Watch This!

2026-11-04 · Video · views n/a · recovered from blog embed · blog: onlypropfirms.com/bdh/dont-start-scalping-until-you-watch-this

  • The chop-box system (his signature): find a range on the 5-minute, buy the lows, sell the highs, take 10 points each time. Five of those is $1,000 off a single range. He knows the spectrum ("two points for $40, micro scalping, 10 or 20 point scalps") but his preference: find a range, work it until it breaks.
  • Identifying the range: multiple taps at the high, multiple taps at the low — mark the box from the top of the wick to the bottom of the wick. Nov 2 example: three taps at the lows, five at the top — "five touches at the top is the tell" that it isn't ready to break out and will keep consolidating. That's your window.
  • Timeframes: he marks ranges on the 5-minute and ONLY the 5-minute ("the best timeframe to find them and keep them clean"), then drops to the 1-minute to watch behavior at the touches and time entries.
  • Execution (Nov 1, counted live on the 1-minute): Sell 83 → 73 = $200 · Buy 59 → 69 = $400 · Sell 81 → 71 = $600 · Buy 60 → 70 = $800 · Sell 81 → 71 = $1,000. The sixth one fails — price breaks out and closes outside the box. That's the signal to stop. Even eating the 10-point loss on the last scalp, the day finishes +$800.
  • The end: every range breaks eventually. You can try buying the bottom with a stop under the wick, but on a break like that you get taken out. Stop on the first candle that CLOSES outside the box. "The edge isn't in catching the breakout — it's in working the range while it's working."

"Scalping a range isn't one great trade. It's the same ordinary trade, taken five times, until the range tells you it's over."

How I Trade Chop (And When I Just Don't)

2026-10-20 · Video · views n/a · recovered from blog embed · blog: onlypropfirms.com/bdh/how-i-trade-chop-and-when-i-just-dont

  • Chop = range = consolidation, one condition. You're waiting for the breakout either way.
  • A wick through the level is NOT a break — a CLOSE through it is. Asia/Globex example: price wicked below the level and never closed below it for three straight hours.
  • Option one — don't trade. "There's no obligation to be in the market while it's ranging." He says it twice and means it.
  • Option two — buy low, sell high: every time price returns to the level and doesn't break it, ride it back. Go for 10 to 20 points, take the scalp, get out every time.
  • Works on short ranges too: a ~15-minute London consolidation pre-open (~8:00) where every 5-minute candle was a buy or a sell — the only way to lose was to buy the top and sell the bottom, which is exactly what happens without the box drawn.
  • He shows his own mistake: bought the top of the range on a "new low = continuation higher" read and chopped for almost an hour. Reversal note: "If price has been dumping all day and then breaks to the upside, that's not just a move — that can be a reversal."

"If you know how to play the range, you're fine. If you don't, just don't trade. Both are correct answers."
