## Key rules and strategy from videos

### 1. EMA system — 14 / 50 / 200 (from "Everyone Says EMAs Don't Work")
- His EMA settings are 14, 50 and 200.
- THE RULE THAT MAKES THEM WORK: "You are not trading EMAs alone. You can't. They're confluence, not a signal. You stack them with everything else to tip the odds toward your direction."
- Example day: 15-minute opening range high 30,045 / low 29,853.75. Consolidation, back-and-forth, then a solid rejection and a break lower. After the rejection off the 14, "it melted."
- THE PATTERN: drop → retracement to the EMA → drop → retracement → drop. Each retracement is an entry. Printed in pre-market and again at the market open — both times price got right back below the 14 EMA.
- BEARISH EMA CROSS formed around 9:20. Then look at the SPACING: "well-separated EMAs mean strong pressure in that direction. When they're spread and pointing down, you're looking for shorts, not longs."
- HIGHER-TIMEFRAME CONFLUENCE: on the 1-hour, price was below the 14, 50 AND 200 — all three. On the 4-hour, another bearish cross. "That's a lot of selling pressure, and it tells you which side to take."
- EMAs ARE ALSO TAKE-PROFIT LEVELS: "On stream you'll hear D-Money say his TP is at the 14 EMA on the 5-minute, the 15-minute, the 30-minute — depending on what's in front of him."
- HONEST CAVEAT: "on a trend day the EMAs are very well respected. In a choppy market they don't perform as well. That's the real answer to 'EMAs don't work.'"

### 2. The 14 EMA trend day (from "NQ Dropped 780 Points. Here's How I Traded It" — Oct 14 CPI day)
- The setup before the print: EQUAL HIGHS. The 8:25 candle topped at 30,852.75 and the news candle topped at 30,852.75 — exactly the same. Then the 8:30 bearish candle covered 245 points in a single bar.
- WHY THE OPEN WAS A TRAP: the 5-minute opening range candle was a doji — 44-point top wick, ~50-point bottom wick, almost no body. "Bulls and bears fighting, not a direction." His rule: after heavy pre-market news movement, the 9:30 open usually gives you indecision before price settles. He took a long for continuation and got chopped on the first trade — stated in full transparency. Then a breakout above the ORB high, capitalized; then a rejection straight back down; the market sat in a mapped range for about an hour from 10:00.
- THE ALL-DAY SETUP, repeated over and over once the consolidation range broke down: Drop → retracement to the 14 EMA → rejection → continuation lower. Rejection off the 14 for an entry, again, again — "it printed all day. Multiple 45-point winning trades came out of it."
- STRUCTURE RULE: in a downtrend you get lower highs and lower lows. "Price went over two hours without reclaiming the 14 EMA on the 5-minute."
- THE ONE CANDLE THAT FLIPPED IT: one candle came in aggressive and CLOSED back above the 14 EMA. He told chat in real time he was taking a long. The move ran ~60 points and he caught roughly 50 — a little after 3:00, ending the stream.
- DAILY CONTEXT: yesterday's daily candle high 30,909 / low 30,591.25. "Coming in below yesterday's daily low often gives you expansion for more downside" — and every mapped level got hit: 30,350 tagged, bottom of the box at 30,120 hit. Almost 780 points to the lows.
- EMA SPREAD = TREND STRENGTH: "Look at how far apart the EMAs are. Wide separation means a strong trend. Then you take the rejections off them and ride it."
- EVAL ACCOUNT RULE (the tip nobody teaches): on a CPI day his goal isn't a dollar figure — it's passing evaluations, because news days give you momentum in either direction. He got several passed. And: the four accounts with a zero next to them were already passed, so he LOCKED them — "clicked the wrong button, traded an account that was already green, lost it" before. Lock a passed eval so you can't touch it by accident.

### 3. Trend trading: buy the retracement, not the breakout (from "The Trend Is Your Friend")
- Definition: uptrend = higher highs and higher lows. The part people skip: "if you can anticipate the next higher low, you get a better entry than the person chasing the move."
- Session structure walked live: significant drop off the opening range (Trump speaking); then a low, higher low, another higher low (consolidation); breakout up; high put in at 10:30; retracement back inside the mapped range; another breakout and a higher high. "Consolidation → breakout → retracement → breakout. That sequence is the setup."
- THE TRADE FRAME: instead of buying the breakout, buy the retracement toward the last higher low. Long at 31,036.75, stop below the last higher low at 31,025 — an 11-point stop. Target the previous higher high, roughly 55 points away. "Risking 11 to make 55. Not exciting, just asymmetric."
- WHY BREAKOUT BUYING KEPT FAILING: every single breakout in this session got retraced before going higher. Long at 31,123 dropped back to 31,097; the 31,144 breakout played then came right back — each was a stop-out for a breakout buyer and an entry for someone waiting on the higher low.
- LIVE EXECUTION: back at 2:00, looked left, saw higher lows being put in, got long; rode toward the 31,124 wick and got stopped out IN PROFIT; retracement, long again to a higher high; back down, long again — "three trades, essentially no drawdown."
- When every push up gets retraced: "spotting the trend doesn't mean trading it as a breakout. It means playing the range: buy cheaper, ride it back toward the top."
- Note: Yuri and Lucas covered the midday range before the 2 PM block — "the structure was mapped out long before the trade."

### 4. Double bottoms and the ORB (from "The Double Bottom Was The Low All Day" — Wed Oct 7)

### 5. Don't pick tops on expansion moves (from "Why You Can't Pick The Top")
- The rule: "trade what's in front of you, not what you think is going to happen."
- Session detail: 5-minute opening range mapped 30,763–30,868. Then breakout, continuation, retracement, continuation, retracement, consolidation, then pumping again. The 9 AM one-hour candle alone was 194 points. The 10 o'clock hour: small bodies, long wicks top and bottom — classic indecision; once price took off at noon, "off to the races."
- Mapped 31,040 live on stream as daily resistance; price hit it in the 2–4 PM session. "Will it reject from there? Nobody knows. You let Globex open tonight and you find out."
- Context: yesterday's reversal was over 500 points to the upside and "every single time it looked like it was going to drop, the market said no." On the 4-hour: exactly ONE bearish candle since the 18th.
- THE MATH: "You only have so much money in the account, and you're going to run out of steam before the Nasdaq decides to reverse." A news headline could flip it tomorrow — real — "but you don't position for that, you position for the trend that's actually printing."
- EXECUTION: higher high → retracement → higher high → retracement. "Your job is to buy the retracements and ride the direction. Take your A+ setups over and over, disassociate from the money, and let your win rate do the work."

### 6. Bottom wicks = reactionary levels ("Embrace the wicks" — from "What A 90 Point Bottom Wick Tells You")
- The day was a grind: consolidation since 7:00 AM, FOMC minutes in the afternoon, Trump at 1:00 PM.
- THE SIGNAL: the 9:30 candle, closing at 10:00 on the 30-minute, finished with a long bottom wick — roughly 90 points from the low back to the body. "Ninety points of wick means buyers were willing to step in and lift it all the way back." Looking left, no other candle inside the range had dropped that low. Price pushed up, back down, back up, closed green.
- THE TRADE: the next candle — also a bottom wick, about 47 points, digging back into the previous wick down to ~31,192 — was the entry, called live to chat. A 50-point winner; it eventually broke out above the opening range at 31,275.
- HONEST PART ABOUT ENTRIES: "you don't know how deep the retracement goes until it reaches into that previous bottom wick. That's the thing worth practising — not the read, the entry."
- SAME SETUP, HIGHER TIMEFRAME = MORE WEIGHT: the 1-hour 9:00–10:00 candle closure digging into a long bottom wick, pushing up, closing green — sets up the frame for the 11:00 candle.
- "REACTIONARY LEVEL" DEFINED: map where price bounced the last time it was down there. Support printed on the 30th at 7:00 AM and got retested on the 31st at 11:00 AM. Marking them makes framing far easier.
- BEARISH VERSION: rejection wicks. A rejection on October 2nd at 9:00 AM — map it, draw it across — becomes resistance; price came back to it on the 4th and rejected again.
- "Always look left. Look at how price reacted the last time it was at a level, and you'll see how often you get that reaction."

### 7. Liquidity logic (from RSS short "A 70 Point Candle In One Minute", dvAMJUom3Hw)
- "It hunted one side, didn't get it, and turned for the other." When a move fails to take the obvious liquidity (London low), the other side (London high) usually becomes the target. 15 → 20 points, through 28,300; 25 points, took it; +$535.

### 9. Power Hour NQ plan (from RSS video a5FafEPZCEw, 2026-11-14)
- Power Hour = 3–4 PM ("A lot of the time you get volatility heading into that window — but it isn't guaranteed… Two to three is the setup for it.") Streams Power Hour 2–4 every day.
- THE LEVELS: two ranges marked — 27,863 above, 27,637 below — "then the one that matters — the 50 EMA on the one hour at 27,704. Price was pushing right into it." Plan: see if the level holds or "do we get some kind of rejection off that 50 EMA for a rollover back to the downside."
- THE METHOD: "You let price basically come to you. You let the one hour candle open up and you're trying to join the momentum in the direction of the move." Not predicting, not positioning early.
- 5-MIN READ OF THE DAY: the whole day repeated — drop, retracement back to the EMAs, continuation lower; lower highs, lower lows, price rejected off the 14 EMA over and over: "Look how many times that's actually printed."
- POWER HOUR QUESTION: on the one hour — lower high or higher low? Then join the party in whichever direction they take it.
- THE 3:50 CANDLE: "At 3:50 you get MOC — market on close. A lot of the time that's a momentum candle on its own." Chopping into Power Hour? You may get follow-through at 3:50. "Every day is a little bit different." CPI day context: "You have to come into this thing with an open mind."
- CORE: "Power Hour isn't a setup. It's a window where volatility shows up more often than not — and the prep is knowing your levels before it opens so you're reacting, not deciding."
- Schedule: "We rock out Sundays from 5:45 for Globex open, all week long, and Power Hour two to four."

### 10. Bank-holiday / low-volume playbook (from RSS video WcHol3sU1EE)
- THE TELL: prior 1-hour candles ~400 points; the current one ~95 points. "It almost feels like a one minute chart… That's just what happens when there's low volume."
- APPROACH: "Can you really go into this thing with high expectations? I don't think so." Low expectations. SIZE DOWN. Don't go in willy-nilly on a session where you don't know what the market's going to bring.
- LEVELS: 28,465 possible resistance (1H + 4H); 28,695 the cleaner reversal area; daily 14 EMA ~28,500, price reached 28,406. Question: was the November 5 crash candle the drop, and is this the retracement before continuation lower?
- HOLIDAY SESSIONS: "Sometimes on the holidays it is just a grind. It's a grind in one direction." Bank holiday often a 1:00 PM close. "Your job as a trader is to join the party in either direction."
- 15-MIN: above the 14, 50 and 200 EMA; pattern: higher high, retracement, repeat.
- CORE: "Compare today's candle size to last week's before you decide what size to trade. The chart tells you what kind of day it is before the open does."

## Recurring D-Money mechanics (cross-video)
- EMA stack: 14 / 50 / 200 on all timeframes (5m/15m/30m/1H/4H/daily); confluence only, never a standalone signal; EMA spacing = trend strength; EMAs double as TP levels.
- Core entry pattern on trend days: drop → retracement to the 14 EMA → rejection → continuation; reversal tell = a candle CLOSING back above the 14 EMA after hours of holding below it.
- 5-minute ORB read at 9:30; doji OR candle = wait for indecision to settle; ORB low/high breaks traded for momentum.
- Equal highs/lows as liquidity setups; London high/low hunts; "if it fails to take one side, the other side is the target."
- Wicks: bottom wicks = buyers lifting; entry = when retracement digs into the previous wick; rejection wicks = resistance when retested. "Always look left."
- Trend definition into a frame: buy the retracement toward the last higher low, stop just below it, target the previous higher high (e.g., 11-point risk for ~55 points).
- Never pick tops on expansion; trade what's in front of you; buy retracements repeatedly and let the win rate work.
- Range/double-bottom days: ORB breakdown shorts, double bottoms called live, 30-minute bullish wick-candle entries.
- Power Hour 3–4 (setup 2–3): let the 1-hour candle open and join the momentum; 50 EMA on the 1H as the decision level; 3:50 MOC momentum candle.
- Low-volume days: compare candle size to last week, size down, expect a one-direction grind.
- Eval discipline on news days: goal = pass evals, not dollar targets; LOCK passed accounts.
