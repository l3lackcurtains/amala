## 1. The "10 Years In" Lessons (his four biggest)

From `10-years-in-what-id-do-differently` (10 Nov 2026). Context: 10 years across stocks, options, forex, futures; accounts blown along the way; **two full years of losses before his first payout**.

1. **Slow down to speed up.** Sizing up to force payouts doesn't shorten the curve: "you are trying to go a million miles an hour... you think that simply **sizing up and going full port** is going to speed up your process... that is not the case." Protect capital in chop: "be smart, buy the lows, short the highs of the range."
2. **Stop comparing yourself to others.** Seeing others' $300K payouts when yours is $2,000 creates "this sudden urge to speed up, size aggressively, and force trades. **Do not do this.** ... Aggressive sizing might work in a perfectly trending market... but in a choppy market, you will get absolutely chopped to pieces. Focus on your own race."
3. **Brush off yesterday's baggage — blank slate daily.** "You are going to experience a million emotions: self-doubt, frustration, and self-criticism... you have to learn how to **brush that off and start every new trading day with a blank slate.**" Also: **take breaks** — he used to think trading London/US/Asia sessions meant more payouts: "We all know that is a lie. Sometimes you catch one or two good trades at the market open, you lock your accounts, and you walk away."
4. **Incorporate the higher time frames — learned by losing.** "How did I finally figure out that I needed to watch the 1-hour, 4-hour, and daily charts? Full transparency: by losing accounts, losing money, and getting incredibly frustrated." Staring only at the 5-minute gets you trapped shorting "right on a major 1-hour support level" before a 400-point rip.

Closing message of that article: "it took me two full years of taking losses and walking through the fire to get my very first payout. If you are struggling right now, feeling defeated, and dealing with outside noise telling you to quit—you have to keep going. Your self-confidence has to come from within."

## 2. Handling Chop Weeks (the frustration antidote)

From `my-first-trade-today-was-a-chop-trade-keeping-it-real`:

- He admits his own failures publicly: "I take chop trades too. My very first trade today was a chop trade right at the 9:30 AM open... and it immediately failed." Transparency normalizes losses.
- "You might have had a rough day... a rough week... even be feeling like quitting... you aren't alone." **Don't beat yourself up.**
- Key realization: "**the market is not trending 100% of the time.** In fact, the market spends the vast majority of its time consolidating and ranging."
- Adjustment playbook: (1) take profits early and stack base hits; (2) size down; (3) "There is nothing wrong with taking a break, closing the laptop, and stepping away to clear your head."

## 3. The Flat-Day Win

From `trading-psychology-why-i-stayed-flat-on-a-difficult-day` ("nothing burger" session):

- After a 300-point rip and a 350-point dump in the morning, the afternoon market is "completely gassed" — even range-trading the box "was sketchy."
- His conclusion: "the smartest thing you can do as a trader is **scale your size down and call it what it is.** Do not force trades when the volume isn't there. Do not let boredom trick you into taking a terrible setup. Walking away flat on a day designed to chop you up is a win for your psychology." And: "Monday is a new day, and the setup will be there."

## 4. Killing the Need to Be Right (chopping trades early)

From `dont-let-greed-kill-your-trade-cut-early`:

- "One of the hardest psychological barriers in trading is the need to 'be right.' You might have correctly predicted that the market is going to sell off, but if your entry is off by 30 points, the natural retracements in the market will shake you out."
- Reframe: cutting early "is a tool, not a failure" — "It is always easier to exit a trade for a tiny, controlled loss and re-enter when the price gives you your desired behavior."
- Being right directionally with a wrong entry "can still cost you a blown account."

## 5. No Bias, No Ego

From `stop-trading-your-bias-how-i-survived-this-choppy-open`:

- "I used to log on and say, 'The macro timeframe is bearish, so I am only looking for shorts today.' But the reality is that if you are a momentum day trader, the market makers will often push the price massively higher to hunt liquidity *before* they roll it over and go lower. If you stick stubbornly to a bias, you will get crushed."
- Same lesson in `my-full-am-trading-routine-before-i-take-any-trade`: "you don't trade what you *think* should happen; you trade what is right in front of you."

## 6. Emotional Resilience in Live Trading

From the recaps:

- **Two stops then a 150-point win** (`the-reality-of-trading-2-stops-then-a-massive-trade`): "Trading isn't always a straightforward, one-directional move. Sometimes, you have to take a few controlled losses, read the market structure, and pivot to catch the real move of the day." The key enabler: losses kept to 10–15 points so capital and composure survived to catch the reversal.
- **Loss first, banger second** (`lost-my-first-trade-then-caught-the-biggest-move-of-the-day`): "When the setup fails, you don't hold and hope—you cut the trade. We took the loss and waited for the real move." Sign-off: "Cut your losers quickly, follow the momentum, and let the winners run!"
- **First chop trade of the day, then the best trade of the day** (`the-double-bottom-was-the-low-all-day`): "In a market like this, you have to scrap and take what you can get." He still ended green (+ passed an eval at $3,235 and +$1,830 elsewhere).
- **Eating chop and adapting** (`nq-dropped-780-points-heres-how-i-traded-it`): "our first long continuation attempt got chopped up during the open indecision. But in trading, you eat the chop, adapt, and move on!"
- **Resisting the short-the-top urge** (`why-you-cant-pick-the-top-on-nasdaq-futures`): "the market is going to do whatever it wants... Trade what is in front of you, not what you think should happen." He notes traders who shorted a 500+ point reversal "paid the price."

## 7. Process Tools for Staying Disciplined

- **Talk through the chart out loud** (`how-i-frame-quality-setups-on-nasdaq-futures`): "Verbalizing the checklist slows your brain down, keeps you from clicking buttons randomly, and helps you frame high-probability setups with real conviction." Sample self-talk: "Okay, the market is consolidating right now. What usually comes after consolidation? Momentum." / "Where is my invalidation? Right below the 30-minute wick low."
- **Slow down to spot reversals live** (`how-i-spot-equal-highs-and-equal-lows-for-reversals`): "you have to actively train yourself to **slow down**. Do not just blindly hit buy or sell based on emotion. Look closely at the exact price points of the wicks."
- **Accept the market's character** (`i-knew-it-would-chop-before-the-open`): "accept the market for what it was and take what you could get."
- **Patience as the edge** (`what-is-momentum-trading-my-full-breakdown`): "Momentum trading is about patience. Wait for the big bazooka candle, wait for the perfect EMA retracement, and let the institutions do the heavy lifting!"

## 8. Expectations & Realism

- "You are going to have ups and downs, rough stretches, and hot streaks where you feel like you can't miss. You can study, backtest, and memorize your A+ setup all day long, but none of it matters if you can't piece the puzzle together when the live market is actually moving" (`how-i-frame-quality-setups-on-nasdaq-futures`).
- "the market is rarely ever in a true one-directional trend—you are going to deal with consolidation far more often than clean expansions" (`the-market-is-ranging-more-than-you-think`).
- Green-is-green mentality: small positive days ($370, $585, $1,580) are celebrated as wins ("a green day is a green day!").
- Not every wick/pattern works: "you will get faked out sometimes. But when you combine wick analysis with EMA crossovers and pure momentum, you develop a serious edge" (`the-secret-to-finding-reversals-wick-analysis-101`).
