## Discipline Is a Daily Fight, Not a Trait

- *"Honestly, discipline is something you have to actively fight for. If you aren't mentally prepared to go into a session and accept losing money, you aren't going to be disciplined. And if you aren't mentally prepared to sit in your chair for hours doing absolutely nothing while waiting for an A+ setup, you aren't going to make it."* 
- He is explicit that he is not perfect: *"Am I 100% perfect at discipline? No. I have blown accounts, and I have even passed evaluations and blown the funded account on the exact same day. It happens to everybody."*
- **His 3 Pillars of Trading Discipline:**
 1. **Dial in your risk-to-reward** — a profile that fits your personality and account size; respect the stops.
 2. **Master ONE strategy** — "Stop strategy-hopping. Stick to a setup you have been practicing for months or years… only execute when your exact criteria show up."
 3. **Time management & waiting** — "Waiting is the hardest — and most profitable — part of the job. Rushing into a setup out of boredom is the fastest way to blow your account."

## The Army's "Hurry Up and Wait"

His self-described "unfair advantage": military training made sitting still easy. *"In the military, the daily motto is literally 'hurry up and wait'… Because I was trained to stand still for hours without losing my mind, sitting at a desk waiting for a clean setup on NQ is easy for me."*. Patience is his stated edge over most traders.

## Tilt: The Blowups He Owns Publicly

- **The live-account disaster:** blew his Lucid Live account within two days of getting it, plus a personal account holding his own prop-firm savings. *"I went into full tilt mode… I started overtrading, overtrading, and overtrading. I took a hit, and then I kept trying to force trades just to make the money back."* He was actually **up on the day**, told himself he was done, took a FOMO re-entry in a trending market without a stop loss, and lost everything.
- **Key insight — live money feels different:** *"the 50K evaluation accounts you trade only have a $2,000 drawdown, what's the difference? The difference is entirely in your mindset… the psychology changes when real cash is on the line."*
- **The comparison trap:** watching coworker "Lucas absolutely killing it" made him push multiple contracts and rush entries — blowing two Blue Sky accounts: *"what works for someone else at the BDH is not always going to work for me. Because I abandoned my strict discipline, I ended up blowing two Blue Sky accounts"*.
- **The rule-breaking day:** one session of "greed trap," "breakdown trap," and a news-candle long cost him two Apex accounts: *"I broke my rules, ignored my discipline, had terrible risk management, and I paid the price."* His closing lesson: **"If you don't follow your risk management plan, the market will make you pay."**
- **The revenge trade that worked (and why he still condemns it):** after a loss he sized up out of frustration and won — *"You should not trade like that. I shouldn't have sized up out of emotion just to get my money back. It played out this time… but you should always stick to your sizing rules!"*. His point: a working revenge trade is still a rule violation, and luck isn't a strategy.
- **Buying the top on FOMO:** twice chased a ripping market, twice eaten by big momentum candles: *"These are $1,000 mistakes every single time those 50-point candles drop."*

## Handling Loss Days

- **Full transparency as a policy:** *"I am making this daily recap because I want to be 100% transparent when things go wrong"*. He publishes losses, exact P&L, and blown accounts on stream.
- **Emotion control while losing:** after losing multiple accounts in a violent flush: *"you have to control your emotions on stream and accept that the market wasn't giving any re-entries. It just wanted to flush. … Take your losses on the chin, control your emotions"*.
- **The break-even heartbreak:** cut a consolidating trade at break-even, market instantly ripped without him — *"Are you kidding me?! I was furious."* Instead of tilting, he waited for the volume and took one clean NY-open long that flipped the whole morning green. Lesson he draws: manage risk on early losers, stay alive, and the A+ setup pays for everything.
- **The comeback pattern:** down in London chop → "I took a deep breath," waited for a high-conviction wick-rejection + tight consolidation, sized up deliberately, and finished the day green. Same story on Silver after two chopped shorts: disciplined 1:1 win → green night. His formula: *"You take the hits, stick to your rules, and wait for your setups to print."*
- **On losing accounts after months of wins:** *"This is the first time in a couple of months that I have actually lost accounts… I absolutely hate losing accounts"* — but he refuses to let it spiral.

## Patience & Boredom as the Core Skill

- "Stop expecting instant action… you cannot sit down at the open — or tune into a live stream — and expect an immediate entry right then and there." 
- Demonstrated repeatedly: sitting out chop for over an hour, waiting 15 minutes between setups, 25 minutes for a flag to reach the London High.
- He also names the failure mode: "If you keep FOMOing into candles or revenge trading after a loss, you might as well kiss your accounts goodbye."

## Protecting Profits & The Mind

- *"Never let a solid green trade turn red out of greed — another setup will always come around the corner"*. He applies it literally: locking a winner early because "I had some stuff to do" and watching the gap fill later without regret; refusing to re-enter after payout-worthy days.
- *"Stop rushing it. You do not need to rush to pass an evaluation, and you do not need to rush to get a payout today. The market is still going to be here tomorrow, next week, next month, and ten years from now."* 
- *"Protect your mind, protect your capital, and know when to shut it down."* — the mental-state bleed is his stated reason: a tilt night damages the next day's trading.

## The Economics Mindset That Keeps Him Sane

- **Small wins compound:** *"Small payouts. You do not need to hit massive 100-point home runs to make a lot of money in this industry… The strategy is simple: Small wins, lots of accounts, and compounding."* His own quarter on that model: modest eval fees in, multiples of it back out in payouts.
- **Base hits over home runs:** small scalps and 1:1 targets are treated as legitimate profits stacked across accounts; greed is flagged as the enemy even when a trade could have run much further — "I stuck to my rules."
- **Prop firm realism:** he teaches that a prop firm "does not fix a bad trading strategy" and that re-buying blown evals is "just gambling… feeding the firm's business model"; *"The account size is never the hard part. The discipline is."* 

## Accountability & Recovery

- Apologizes on the record: *"I apologize for that. You guys watch the streams to learn, not to watch us gamble and take stupid risks. I promise that tomorrow I am going back to being disciplined."* 
- Bounce-back framing after the live-account blow: *"I am just going to come back stronger… get in the trenches, absolutely kill it, and get moved back to live status."* Year goal unchanged: get to Live on all prop firms and build capital for next year.
- On his own mistakes in chop: *"even I mess this up sometimes… I bought at the very top of the range because we had just made a higher low"*. He treats each logged mistake as a lesson for the audience and himself.
- Chat/community as an external check: he credits chat for calling his bad entries ("chat… you were right, and I was wrong") and cites their warnings in recaps.

## Summary of His Psychology in His Own Words

1. "Discipline is something you have to actively fight for."
2. "Refusing to click buttons when my setup isn't there" is where he excels.
3. "Take the money and walk away" — both daily profit and stalled winners.
4. "If you don't follow your risk management plan, the market will make you pay."
5. "Small wins, lots of accounts, and compounding."
6. "Protect your mind, protect your capital, and know when to shut it down."

**Caveat:** These are lessons as he presents them in video-recap articles — practical and anecdotal rather than a formal psychological program. All content above is directly sourced from his articles; nothing invented.
