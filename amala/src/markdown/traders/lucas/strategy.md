## Who He Is & What He Trades

- Lucas is a 21-year-old full-time futures day trader and streamer at the BDH (started trading ~2023 in high school; consistent payouts followed within his first two years).
- **Primary instrument: NQ (Nasdaq futures).** He recommends beginners start on **ES** (S&P 500 futures — "moves a little bit slower, the price action is cleaner") and graduate to NQ after 6–12 months of consistency.
- **Accounts:** Runs a portfolio of funded prop accounts simultaneously via a **copy trader** — named firms include **Lucid Trading**, **Tradeify**, **Vest Markets**, and **WarBux**.
- **Style:** Scalping momentum via bull flags and bear flags. "I keep it simple: I trade 15-minute bull flags and bear flags around the 9 EMA, 21 EMA, and VWAP."

## Sessions He Trades

Lucas trades two windows:

### a) The Asia / Globex Session (overnight)

- Globex opens **6:00 PM EST**. The real Asia volatility begins ~50 minutes later.
- **"No Man's Land" rule:** the 50-minute window between the Globex open and the Asia market open is untouchable — "I do not touch the market during this time... the spread gets weird, orders don't get filled cleanly, and the market can aggressively gap up or down and blow past your stops."

### b) The New York Open (9:30 AM EST)

- Pre-market prep at ~9:25 AM (see routine below), execution from the 9:30 bell for roughly the next 30–45 minutes.

He also streams/trades through the London session when an Asia trade needs time to develop.

## The Level-Mapping System (Core to Everything)

Before any trade, Lucas marks "the most obvious, prominent wick highs and wick lows on both sides of current price action":

- **Upside targets:** nearest prominent wick highs, then session highs (London High, PM High, Asia High).
- **Downside targets:** nearest wick lows, support zones, session lows (PM Low, AM Low, Asia Low).
- **Psychological levels** are the target when price is in "open air" with no nearby wicks: "When you are in open air like that, you target the nearest major psychological level."

These pre-marked wicks double as **take-profit scaling zones** once in a multi-contract trade.

## Core Setups

### Setup A — The Bull/Bear Flag (bread and butter)

Works on 5-min, 15-min, and 1-min charts in any session:

1. Establish macro bias from trend and levels.
2. Flag pulls back into the **9 EMA / 21 EMA** with VWAP as added confluence.
3. **Trigger:** the flag candle closes in trend direction / price breaks the flag structure and makes a new high (long) or new low (short). "Do not jump the gun. Wait for the actual candle close."
4. **Stop:** below the wick low of the flag entry (long) or at/above the top wick of the flag structure (short).
5. **Targets:** nearest wick high/low, then session highs/lows, then psychological levels.

Confluence requirements: "I trade bull and bear flags using the 9 EMA, 21 EMA, and VWAP as confluence. If price doesn't perfectly respect those indicators and my key daily levels (like AM High, PM High, or Asia Low), I do not take the trade."

### Setup B — The A+ Asia Session Setup

1. **At Globex open (6 PM):** immediately mark any prominent highs/lows that print (wick extremes above and below price).
2. **Wait for the flag:** a clean 5-minute bull/bear flag forming after the Asia open.
3. **Retracement confluence:** clean pullback to **VWAP**, holding above the **9 EMA and 21 EMA** (mirror for shorts).
4. **Entry:** "right around the midpoint of that pullback candle."
5. **Stop:** "hard stop-loss directly below the wick low of the flag entry."
6. **Targets:** nearest session highs; with multiple contracts scale out at **nearest wick high → PM High → next major psychological level**.
7. Expectancy: "On a volatile day, holding this specific setup could net you a massive move. On a normal day, targeting the standard levels still gives you a beautiful 2.5 Risk-to-Reward (RR) ratio trade."
8. **Pro tip:** take profit slightly BEFORE the pivot level: "Sometimes the market will get incredibly close to your pivot target but not quite touch it. Do not be greedy!... Take profit slightly before the absolute level to ensure you get filled."

### Setup C — NY Open Routine (9:25 → 9:30 → 45 min after)

1. **Indicators on:** 9 EMA, 21 EMA, VWAP, Kill Zones & Pivots (session levels auto-marked).
2. **9:25 AM — mark both sides:** the obvious 5-min wick highs leading up to the session high, and the obvious wick lows below (pre-open wicks, support shelves).
3. **Avoid the 9:25 trap:** a pre-market push above the EMAs "wants to trick you" — check VWAP position (ceiling overhead?) and untapped deep wick lows below.
4. **9:30 decision rule:** "If price delivers and holds above the VWAP at the open, we target the London Highs. If it rejects the VWAP, we look to short into those deep lows below."
5. **Execution logic:** tap of a pre-marked wick high + VWAP rejection → A+ bear flag → break and close below the 9/21 EMAs → short.
6. **Scale out on the pre-marked wicks:** first contract at the nearest wick low, second at the next major one, the runner at the deepest pre-marked level.

### Setup D — V-Shape Reversals with Liquidity Sweeps

On the 1-minute chart:

1. **Find the prominent high/low (liquidity)** that was just swept — often a level left by the 8:30 AM news candle or an Asia extreme; the failed break flips short-term momentum.
2. **Wait for exhaustion** at the sweep: "an indecisive, reversal-looking candle. The buyers were exhausted."
3. **Wait for the flag at the EMAs:** after the first flush, price retraces to the EMAs — it "bounced perfectly off the 21 EMA but completely failed to close above the 9 EMA," forming a bear flag.
4. **Entry on the confirming candle close.**
5. **Confluences checked:** swept major high + bear flag at EMAs + VWAP below + untapped lows at the bottom.
6. **Scale out on internal wick lows,** final TP at the untouched session extreme (e.g., the Asia low).
7. Mirror logic for longs: sweep a prominent low → bull flag at EMAs → target untapped highs.

### Setup E — Consolidation Breakout

1. In heavy chop, **do not trade inside the range** — wait for structure.
2. Drop to the 1-minute chart; watch for a flag forming at the edge of the consolidation.
3. **Trigger = the break:** "A flag forming isn't enough; you have to wait for the actual break... price finally broke out of that flag structure and created a brand new low."
4. **Targets = "draws on liquidity":** previous session lows/highs are "the biggest magnets for price" — the untouched session extreme is the ultimate TP, with internal scale-outs at obvious 15-minute wick lows.
5. **Trust the target over quick cash:** even with the position deeply profitable he holds if TA points lower — "if your technical analysis points to a lower level, you have to trust the setup."

### Setup F — Trend Days ("The Money Glitch")

- When NQ goes into a one-way expansion, farm the 1-minute **9 EMA / 21 EMA retracements**: "Every single time the 1-minute candle pulled back and tapped the 9 EMA or the 21 EMA, it bounced right off and created another 'kill candle'" in the trend direction.
- "Days like this do not happen often, but when they do, you *have* to press your advantage" — repeat entries payout after payout.
- Trend days are also eval days: he has passed funded evaluations within minutes on such days by trading his normal setup — not by gambling.

## Adding To A Trade In Drawdown — His Exact DCA Strategy

Preconditions before ANY add:

1. **Clear macro bias** from the previous trend — "The bias is strictly" one direction, based on an undeniable prior trend.
2. **An obvious target above** (longs) / below (shorts) — a prominent session extreme.
3. **A hard stop-loss below** (longs) / above (shorts) the structure. Initial entry at the midpoint of a 5-min flag — "a solid 1-to-1 risk-to-reward setup."

**Add Spot #1 — the lower-low fake-out:** price pushes above VWAP, then rolls over, closes below all EMAs, and makes a lower low. Newer traders panic here; he checks the macro trend and that the hard stop has NOT been hit, then: "When it rolls over and makes that new low, **that is where I add to my size.**"

- **The math:** "If I originally entered with 2 contracts in the 50s, I will add at least 2 more contracts down in the 30s. This pulls my average entry price right down into the 40s." Adding equal-or-greater size at the better price relieves pressure ("averaging down 10+ points").

**Add Spot #2 — the retest:** price pushes up slightly, rolls over again to the new entry zone.

- **Golden rule:** "You can add more contracts here at your entry point **as long as the candle does not close below the previous low you already established.** As long as that floor holds, you are safe to hold for your original target."

**Wick confirmation:** "When I see multiple long bottom wicks printing in a row at a support zone, it tells me that buyers are stepping in and price is about to drive higher. It is a massive bullish confluence."

**Result:** the averaged-down position holds through wick-confirmed support and runs to the pre-set target — these trades can take hours.

**Warning:** "Dollar Cost Averaging is incredibly powerful, but you cannot just blindly buy dips when the market is crashing against you. You must have a clear bias based on the previous trend, an obvious target above you, and a hard stop-loss."

## Scaling In / Out & Trade Management

- **Scale OUT, not up (profit side):** with multiple contracts, take partials at each pre-marked level: first internal wick low/high → next major wick level → runner to the deepest session level.
- **Move stop to break-even** once the first target hits: "Once price drops and hits your first target level, immediately move your stop loss to break-even (or slightly into profit)."
- **Lock profits early on extended moves:** "It ended up flushing all night long, though we locked in our profits early — better safe than sorry."
- **TP slightly before major pivot levels** to guarantee fills.
- **Stops placement:** below the flag's wick low (longs) / at the flag's top wick (shorts) / "at the nearest wick low or candle body, depending on the momentum" (1-min V-shape trades).
- **Zero-drawdown goal:** a perfectly timed flag-break entry means "price never went against us" — "absolutely zero drawdown the entire time."
- **Expect trades to need patience:** Asia trades routinely take 30–90+ minutes to play out.

## Risk-to-Reward & Sizing Approach

- Typical structure: stop at the flag wick, target the next session level → ~1:1 to 2.5:1 RR depending on setup.
- **Sizing rule of thumb:** "You have to give the market enough space to fluctuate between drawdown and profit. If your size is so heavy that a standard 20-point wick blows your account, you are trading too big."
- **Scale laterally, not vertically:** keep per-account risk small (one contract), then add more funded accounts on a copy trader rather than multiplying contract size.
- **The consistency math:** modest daily points × one contract × several copy-traded accounts compounds into five-figure weeks — "even if you have one or two losing days... you are still walking away" green.

## When NOT To Trade

"Knowing when to sit on your hands and protect your capital is the ultimate cheat code":

1. **After a huge overnight trend:** "If we just had a massive, prominent trend overnight, the likelihood of the market giving us another massive, prominent move right at the AM open is significantly lower." The market needs to digest.
2. **Indecisive opening candle:** small 9:30 candle, wicks both sides, no volume/direction → expect chop.
3. **"Barcoding" on the EMAs:** price ranging endlessly on top of the 9 EMA / 21 EMA / VWAP — "do not trade."
4. **No clear targets:** tight range, no obvious liquidity draws above or below.
5. **Wicks everywhere:** long wicks both directions = indecision.
6. **Fake flags:** two red candles + tiny pullback baiting a bear flag; after 2–3 fakes in a range, skip the 4th attempt.
7. **No Man's Land** (first ~50 minutes after the Globex open, before the true Asia open).
8. **Chop/consolidation on funded accounts generally:** "If the market is ranging, chopping, or consolidating, I simply do not take a trade on my funded accounts. Some days I won't trade them at all."
