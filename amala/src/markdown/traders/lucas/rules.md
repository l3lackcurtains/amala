## Entry Rules

1. **Wait for the candle close.** Entering a bear flag before the candle closes gets wicked against you before the move ever goes your way.
 > "Do not jump the gun. Wait for the actual candle close."
2. **Strict checklist — no entry until every box is checked.**
 > "You must have a strict checklist. It doesn't matter if your checklist is different than mine; what matters is that you refuse to enter a trade until *every single box* is checked."
3. **His confluence checklist (V-shape/flag entries).** The full sequence he runs before any entry:
 - Sweep of a prominent low/high (liquidity taken out),
 - Stagnant/exhausted price action after the sweep,
 - V-shape recovery (or rollover) + clear bull/bear flag,
 - Flag candle closes above (or below) the EMAs,
 - Only then: entry.
4. **A+ bear-flag checklist.** Bias from failed retest of internal high → price staying below VWAP and no 5-min closes above EMAs → targets mapped (internal wick lows + session lows) → clear flag → entry on flag break to a new low → stop at the flag's top wick.
5. **No blind buying.** FOMO spikes are not setups.
 > "If the market spiked up, I'd just blindly hit the 'buy' button out of FOMO. That isn't a setup; that is just gambling."
6. **9:25 AM NY-open decision rule.** Hold above VWAP at the bell → target the London Highs; reject VWAP → short the deep pre-market lows.
7. **No trading in "No Man's Land"** — the ~50 minutes between the Globex open (6 PM EST) and the true Asia open. Liquidity is too thin, stops get blown through.

## Sizing Rules

1. **Size so a normal wick can't kill you.**
 > "If your size is so heavy that a standard 20-point wick blows your account, you are trading too big."
2. **Size down after losses.** After big losses he sized all the way down to rebuild.
 > "When you take a massive loss, you have zero conviction left in yourself. The only way to rebuild that confidence is by stacking up micro-wins."
3. **Recalibrate size after every payout.** After a large withdrawal he tried to full-port the smaller remaining balance — "Spoiler alert: it did not work out for me."
 > "When your drawdown buffer shrinks after a withdrawal, you *have* to recalibrate your contract sizing and risk parameters."
4. **Scale laterally, not vertically.** Keep one contract per account, add more funded accounts on a copy trader instead of bigger size. "Do not randomly start trading 10 contracts."
5. **Beginner sizing.** While testing a strategy on an eval:
 > "Your position sizing should be much smaller than you think it should be."

## Adding / Averaging Rules (DCA)

1. **Only average into a trend, never against it.** A clear prior trend (bias), an obvious target beyond, and a hard stop-loss are prerequisites. No blind dip-buying.
2. **Add when price makes a new lower low but the macro trend and hard stop are intact.** Add at least the original contract count to pull the average down meaningfully.
3. **Add again on retest ONLY if the candle does not close below the previous established low.**
 > "As long as that floor holds, you are safe to hold for your original target."
4. **Bottom wicks in a row at support = bullish confluence** that justifies holding the averaged position.
5. **The hard stop is sacred.** If it runs, the DCA thesis is dead.

## Drawdown / Stop Rules

1. **Hard stops at structure.** Below the flag wick low (long) / top wick of flag (short) / nearest wick low or candle body (scalps). Never arbitrary.
2. **Give the market room.**
 > "You have to give the market enough space to fluctuate between drawdown and profit."
3. **Stop to break-even after the first target.**
 > "Immediately move your stop loss to break-even (or slightly into profit)."
4. **Cutting stops early is a symptom of needing the money.**
 > "If you are cutting your stop early, that money means more to you than it should."
5. **Moving your stop out of fear "works" sometimes — that's the trap.** It's how "your P&L reinforces terrible habits and lies to you."

## Take-Profit / Payout Rules & Habits

1. **Targets are pre-marked wick lows/highs and session levels** — never guessed. In open air, target the nearest major psychological level.
2. **Scale out in tranches.** Contract 1 at the first internal wick level, contract 2 at the next major level, contract 3 held as a runner for the deepest level.
3. **Take profit slightly BEFORE pivot targets** to guarantee fills.
 > "Do not be greedy! What's a few missed points if you secure a massive win?"
4. **Lock in early on extended moves.**
 > "We locked in our profits early — better safe than sorry."
5. **Trust the final target over quick profit.** A big unrealized flash of green doesn't override the mapped target if TA points lower.
6. **Withdraw consistently and small.** He runs many modest payouts across accounts rather than letting balances pile up.
7. **After any payout: recalibrate size down, drop to A+ setups only, rebuild rhythm with small wins.**

## Eval / Account Rules

1. **Set a strict monthly budget for eval purchases.** Only scale up after definitively proving the process repeats.
 > "Set a strict monthly budget for how many evaluation accounts you are allowed to buy. Keep everything in moderation and track what you are spending."
2. **Never brute-force accounts.**
 > "When I was blowing accounts, I didn't set a limit. I just bought as many as possible and tried to brute-force my way into profitability. Do not do this!"
3. **Never fund evals with debt.**
 > "I opened credit cards and maxed them out buying trading accounts with no job and no way to pay the money back. *(Do not do this. It is the dumbest thing you can possibly do.)*"
4. **Skin in the game matters.** Move from sim to funded/micros once the strategy proves out — the cost of one evaluation account is real hours of your life, and "you will trade that account much more cautiously."
5. **Use real risk to pass evals the same way you trade funded.** He has passed evaluations in minutes on trend days by trading his normal setups — not by gambling.
6. **Prefer firms/rules that don't constrain his style.** He looks for no payout caps, no news restrictions, and no forced move to live — freedom rules matter to his multi-account approach.

## "When To Stop" Rules

1. **Don't trade the chop days at all.** Big overnight move + indecisive open candle + barcoding on EMAs + wicks everywhere + no clear targets = no trading that day ("hide the power cable").
2. **Some funded-account days get zero trades.**
 > "Some days I won't trade them at all. I wait for my specific setup."
3. **After 2–3 fake-outs in a range, stop taking the 4th.**
 > "Stop trying to force a setup in a dead market!"
4. **Don't force trades to buy things.** His largest single-day loss happened because he forced trades "to squeeze just a little bit more out of the market to pay for" a purchase he wanted.

## Daily Routine (framed as rules)

1. **Same pre-market ritual every day.** Indicators on (9 EMA / 21 EMA / VWAP / Kill Zones & Pivots), mark obvious wick highs and lows on BOTH sides of price ~9:25 AM, define the VWAP-based if/then plan before 9:30. "This is the exact routine I do every single day before the New York open and before hopping on stream for the Asia session."
2. **Asia routine.** At 6 PM Globex open, mark the initial prominent highs/lows immediately; wait out No Man's Land; trade only flags with VWAP + EMA confluence after the Asia open.
3. **Discipline routine that fixed his consistency.**
 > "I started waking up at 4:00 AM, doing push-ups on my balcony, tracking all my calories, going to the gym twice a day, and strictly limiting myself to just **one or two trades a day**."
4. **One setup, repeated.** After a payout or a win, "reverse engineer your success" — write down the exact setups, trade count, and mindset, "and do nothing but repeat it."

## Money / Lifestyle Rules

1. **Always keep a second income.** His own solution in college: a part-time job with guaranteed weekly pay that covered living costs, so trading carried no survival pressure.
 > "You should always have a separate, guaranteed income outside of trading."
2. **Budget lifestyle off your BASELINE month, not your best month.** He "started basing my entire lifestyle and spending habits off my absolute *best* trading months, instead of my baseline, consistent monthly average" — and calls it a core mistake.
3. **Don't convert P&L into price tags.** Stop mentally pricing purchases in trading "points."
4. **Start on ES, then NQ; one ticker only.** "Pick one ticker and master how it moves."

## The 10 Rules In Short (his operating system)

1. Mark obvious wick highs/lows + session levels before every session; both sides of price.
2. Trade only bull/bear flags at 9 EMA / 21 EMA / VWAP confluence — after the candle close.
3. Never trade No Man's Land, chop days, or after a big overnight trend.
4. Hard stops at flag structure; risk sized so a standard wick can't matter.
5. Scale out at pre-marked levels; BE stop after target 1; TP just before pivots.
6. DCA only with trend + target + hard stop; add on new lows, never if a candle closes below the prior low.
7. One contract per account; scale by adding accounts, not size.
8. Monthly eval budget; never debt; recalibrate size after every payout.
9. One or two trades a day; reverse-engineer winning days and repeat them exactly.
10. Keep a second income; live off your baseline month, never your best month.
