Rules as stated or clearly practiced across D-Money's Trading FAQ articles. Short quotes are D-Money's own words.

## Trend-Day Rules

1. **Trade what's in front of you — join momentum, don't predict.** Forecasting the turn is not the job; joining the move the market chose is.
 > "Your job is not to pick the top. Your job is to join the momentum in whatever direction the market decides to go"
2. **Never pick tops (or bottoms) on expansion moves.** Fighting a trend drains the account long before the reversal ever arrives.
 > "You only have so much money in your account to try and fight this thing with, and you are going to run out of steam way before NQ decides to actually reverse"
3. **Never fight heavy momentum.** Large solid-bodied candles = institutional control; stick with them.
4. **In an uptrend: buy the retracement near the last higher low; stop below that higher low; target the previous higher high**.
5. **Don't chase breakouts.** Wait for the pullback instead of buying the high, and never short the bottom of a red candle — wait for the failed retracement.
 > "Stop chasing the high. Look to **buy cheaper** inside the retracement near the previous higher low"
6. **Inside yesterday's daily range ⇒ expect chop; break of yesterday's high/low ⇒ expansion day.** Set your brain accordingly before the open.
7. **Trend shift rule:** after a massive move (500+ points), if lower-highs/lower-lows break and double/triple bottoms or higher highs print, the trend is over — stop pressing it.

## Chop / Range Rules

8. **Inside yesterday's range = base hits only.** Ego stays at the door on inside-range days.
 > "When the daily chart tells you it's an inside range day, leave your ego at the door and stack 15- to 25-point base hits"
9. **Range targets: "10, 15, or maybe 20 points tops."** Don't hold for home runs in a range.
10. **Range trading rule: buy the absolute bottom of the range, short the absolute top.** Stops go 10–15 points beyond the box edge; if the box breaks, the range trade is dead.
 > "Buy the absolute bottom of the range. Short the absolute top of the range"
11. **In chop, when the market gives you 50 points, take them.** You cannot be greedy in range conditions — bank it and wait for the next setup.
 > "In choppy market conditions, you cannot be greedy. When the market gives you 50 points, you take your profit off the table and wait for the next setup"
12. **If the day is a "nothing burger" (range too choppy even to ping-pong), scale size down, force nothing, and accept flat**.
 > "Walking away flat on a day designed to chop you up is a win for your psychology"
13. **No bias into the open.** Wait for the market to establish its range, read the clues (like long bottom wicks), and join the momentum whichever way it decides to go.
 > "You cannot bring a bias into the morning session... Wait for the market to establish its range, look for the clues (like long bottom wicks), and join the momentum, whichever way it decides to go"
 >
 > "Coming to the charts with a rigid bias is an appetite for destruction"

## Entry Rules

14. **Only enter on EMA/zone rejections with confirmation.** You need the push → retracement → **rejection wick (exhaustion wick)** at the 14/50 EMA before entering; never enter "just because the EMAs exist".
15. **Enter as close to the EMA as possible** so the EMA itself anchors the stop — often only 10 to 20 points away.
16. **Wait for EMA cross + location before directional trades.** Bearish cross and price below all three EMAs for shorts; bullish cross / hold above all three for longs; match entry-timeframe cross to the 1H/4H direction.
17. **Equal-low long rule:** while the Equal Low holds, look long; enter when a candle closes bullish above the 14 EMA; stop a few points under the equal low.
18. **Equal-high short rule:** enter short on rejection away from the Equal High; stop a few points above it. A break above kills the thesis for a tiny, controlled loss.
 > "If the bulls magically find volume and break above the Equal High, your thesis is invalidated, and you take a tiny, controlled loss"
19. **Reversal needs structural confirmation.** One bullish candle doesn't mean a reversal — require the shift to higher lows.
 > "One bullish candle doesn't mean a reversal"
20. **Swipe-and-go entry:** enter long after a liquidity swipe below the prior low rejects back up; stop strictly below the rejection wick.
21. **Do not enter right as a new 30-min/1H candle opens at an extreme** — HTF candles almost always push the other way first to create a wick.
 > "When a new 30-minute or 1-hour candle opens, it almost always pushes in the opposite direction first to create a wick before resuming the trend."
22. **Don't execute right before an hourly close** (final-10-minute wick hunts) — watch the clock.

## Stop-Loss Rules

23. **Stop goes on the opposite side of the level you traded:** opposite side of the EMA (10–20 pts); below the rejection/swipe wick; a few points beyond equal high/low; 10–15 pts beyond the range box edge.
24. **Structural invalidation stops:** below the last higher low (longs) / above the last lower high (shorts).
 > "My stop is going right below that previous 30-minute candle low. If it takes that out, I'm out"
25. **If price breaks fully through the EMA, you *want* to be stopped** — the trend is broken; the stop did its job.
 > "If it does break above, you want to be stopped out anyway because the trend is broken!"
26. **Keep controlled losses small:** initial losses "strictly controlled (10-15 points max)" so capital survives to catch the real move.

## Trade-Management Rules ("Chopping Trades")

27. **Cut early when the trade stops acting right.** There is zero shame in a 5-to-10 point scratch.
 > "If a trade does not immediately begin acting the way your thesis predicted, there is zero shame in closing it for a 5-to-10 point loss"
28. **Exhaustion wick against you = exit now.** Especially an opposing wick that sweeps a prior low/high — do not hold through it hoping the trend continues.
29. **Never sit and hope.** Price hesitated, momentum stalled — chop the trade and look for the next one; also "always cut it if the momentum dies".
 > "Never sit and hope—cut it if it doesn't go!"
30. **Protect open profits on trailing-drawdown accounts.** An open profit that pulls back to flat consumes the same amount of drawdown room.
31. **Lock accounts immediately once an eval is passed** — so you can't accidentally execute a trade on the wrong account and blow it up later.

## Position Sizing / Risk Rules

32. **Default = ONE contract.** You will very rarely see him trade two.
 > "I am almost exclusively trading **one contract**. You will very rarely see me trade two"
33. **The drawdown math:** $50K prop account ≈ $2,000 trailing drawdown; 1 NQ contract ($20/point) with a 20-point stop = **$400 risk ≈ 5 consecutive losses** of ammo. Two contracts halves your ammunition.
34. **Two contracts ONLY on an A+ entry with a ~10-point stop** (entered right on an EMA rejection wick), keeping total dollar risk at the same ~$400.
35. **Don't size up to force payouts.** Desperation sizing gets destroyed, especially in choppy, range-bound markets.
 > "If your position sizing is too large because you are desperate to get to payouts, you are going to get destroyed—especially in a choppy, range-bound market"
36. **Size down when clarity is gone**.
 > "If the directional clarity isn't there, reduce your contract size"
37. **NQ candle ranges justify small size:** a single 5-minute candle can outrun an entire day's drawdown plan — which is why size stays minimal.

## Pre-Open Routine (hard sequence, every day)

38. **Step 1 — Daily chart:** draw yesterday's candle absolute high and absolute low.
39. **Step 2 — ORB:** at 9:30, let the first 5-minute candle close; map its high/low (use the 15-min ORB if you missed the open).
40. **Step 3 — Pre-market context:** overnight trend? tight box against the EMAs? scheduled 10:00 AM news?
41. **Step 4 — Wait:** let price break the ORB, then wait for the retracement to the EMAs and the rejection before entering; if the trade fails, pivot instantly.
 > "Accept the small loss, pivot, and look for long entries."
42. **Calendar check:** know CPI/FOMC/NFP/earnings/speaker times before trading; expect consolidation ahead of big catalysts.

## Multi-Timeframe Rules

43. **Analyze on 1H/4H/daily; execute on 5-min/15-min.** The higher timeframe finds direction and targets; the lower timeframe enters surgically with tight stops.
 > "The higher timeframe will always win. Use it to find your direction and targets, then use the lower timeframe to surgically enter with tight stops!"
44. **Respect the 1-hour rubber band.** Don't keep pressing a move far extended from the 1-hour EMAs — it snaps back violently.
 > "...it will snap back violently toward the EMAs"
45. **HTF wicks outrank LTF wicks** for reactionary levels.

## Behavioral Rules

46. **Cut losers fast, follow momentum, let winners run** — his stated sign-off formula.
47. **Take breaks — don't trade every session.** Catch one or two good trades at the open, lock the accounts, walk away.
 > "Sometimes you catch one or two good trades at the market open, you lock your accounts, and you walk away"
48. **Slow down to speed up.** Protect capital first; in ranges, buy the lows and short the highs.
49. **Verbalize your analysis out loud before executing.** Consolidation? Location vs ORB? Invalidation? Talking it through slows the brain and stops random clicking.
 > "Verbalizing the checklist slows your brain down, keeps you from clicking randomly"
50. **Take what the market gives.** Accept the market for what it is and take what you can get.
 > "you have to accept the market for what it was and take what you could get"
