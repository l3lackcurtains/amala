Gold's setups are few, famous, and each has a measured asterisk. Order: the ones with the most structure behind them first.

## London/session breakout — the signature trade

**Mechanics.** Mark the Asian range. On the London open's liquidity step, take a **15-minute close** beyond the boundary (not a wick poke) with a volume expansion signature; stop inside the range (the far boundary is invalidation, a full-range stop is for scalers only); targets at 1× and 2× the range height, plus the previous day's extremes.

**The filters that make it a system:**
- Overnight range already > half the 14-day average true range → the day spent itself; skip.
- Never straddle both sides of the range with breakout stops — the market's actual release-day pattern is sweep-one-side, reverse, *then* resolve the other. Straddling converts one fakeout into two losses; that sequence is the most repeated operational warning across the gold breakers' own pages.
- ECB and NFP-adjacent London opens go on the skip calendar — the burst statistics (volatility page) make the entry minute the worst-executed trade of the day on those mornings.

**Evidence class:** educational-structural. Vendor EA marketplaces attach in-sample profit factors around 1.4 and out-of-sample claims near 1.6, and marketing pages assert open-window win rates of "73 vs 51%" for other windows — tuned results, disclosed as such. The *shape* (compression → resolution) is the reliable part; the numbers are yours to verify on your own window.

## Break and retest — the anti-fakeout version

**Mechanics.** Gold alternates quiet consolidations with sharp expansions, and it has a *reputation* for false breaks that is backed by the sweep behavior below. The rulebook version: break with a body close (≥ a quarter of the ATR, so it's a real auction shift and not a wick), wait for the retest, enter on the rejection of the retested level, stop beyond the retest structure; a close back through the level invalidates. Wick-through-then-close-back is a grab; a strong body holding is intent — the two-bar reading every gold educator teaches because gold prints both daily.

## Liquidity sweeps / the fade-the-sweep swing — the highest-signal, most-overused pattern

**Mechanics.** The London open almost always *sweeps* one side of the Asian range first. The disciplined version of the reversal: sweep of the boundary, structure break back inside on a close, then fade toward the opposite pool; stop beyond the sweep wick, with **extra buffer** because double sweeps are common on gold specifically.

**Two documented traps inside the pattern:**
1. **Counter-trend sweeps lie.** A sweep-fade against a higher-timeframe trend is not a reversal trade — inside a real gold trend the sweep is often the continuation's launch candle. The fade needs the daily/4H tape to agree.
2. **The NQ sweep lesson transfers:** a measured study of sweep models found the sweep *moment* carries genuine information (it beat matched random controls) but the packaged stop/target geometry lost after costs — which on gold's news bursts is worse because the "wick" is partly slippage.

## Supply & demand zones — the measured asterisk

Zones on gold are real market behavior: $10–20 hourly impulses create them, round numbers and the Asian session mark them, and macro trends slice straight through multiple zones on impulse days. But the honest, rare thing in gold content is a published bare-rule test — one exists, with a corrections log: mechanical zone touches (limit at the proximal line, stop beyond the distal, 2R target) hit 2R on only **26% of first touches, below the ~33% breakeven** the payoff requires. Demand zones outperformed supply ones in the sample (a bull tape confound, disclosed). The takeaway generalizes across all "rectangle" strategies: **the zone is a location, not a signal** — trend alignment, structure break confirmation, and visible room-to-target are the trade.

## News-window trading — the "not-a-setup" setup

Every gold plan eventually trades the burst or the aftermath. The aftermath version, distilled from the event datasets: pre-position nothing, watch where price settles by minute ~10–15 (liquidity back, second move running), then join *that* in the direction of the 60-minute held-vs-reversed base rates — and on FOMC specifically, assume reversal odds before positioning for continuation at all. The burst version (entering the release minute) is where firms' ±2-minute rules exist to punish you *technically* while the spread punishes you *financially*; it's the one entry style this guide set rates as not-a-strategy, for prop accounts, on evidence.

## Choosing among them — the same cross-cutting rules as the indices

1. Structure first (daily/4H), session second (where in the day), trigger third (close beyond/reject) — in that order, on every one of the five menus above.
2. Confirmation is a *candle close*, not a level touch — the one pattern every measured setup shares.
3. Targets from the range (1–2× Asia range, prior day extremes, measured moves from the impulse), never round numbers for show — gold's fat tails hit levels that "should" be psychological mostly because liquidity sits there.
4. A skip list is a setup too: compressed afternoons, pre-data hours, the rollover spread window, half-days, the day-after-FOMC hangover — the same "no-trade list" the prop-rule pages keep arriving at, written for gold's clock.
