Each control below pairs a documented failure mode with its pre-committed fix. Nothing here is aspirational — the test for inclusion was that a firm, a dataset, a simulation, or a paid-trader corpus independently recommends it.

1. **Define the stop before the entry — structurally, in dollars.** The disposition research and the platform educators agree: stop placement decided under position is decided by pain, not by the chart. Set the level (beyond the sweep, past the EMA side, outside the IB extreme), write the dollar number, then click. If the dollar number surprises you, the size is wrong — fix the size, never the stop.
2. **Size so five identical losses are survivable.** The rule-verified simulators converge: risk 2–5% of the floor per trade, micro units at 50K. The evals don't die from bad days; they die from a plan where a bad day is already a third of the drawdown. The sizing math is the discipline — everything downstream is execution.
3. **Cap the day's losses at your number, not the firm's.** If the platform allows −$2,000, stop at −$800–1,200. The 300K-account dataset says the killer is the recovery trade, and recovery trades require *time in the seat past the first real loss*. A personal stop below the official one deletes the window where the reflex operates. Where the firm won't do it for you, use what's available: platform limits, the lockout button, the broker-side daily cap.
4. **Cap the trades.** Two to five planned attempts, then the terminal closes regardless of state. The most self-aware line in the trader corpus is a forum veteran's: "after 5 trades I go nuts and start making irrational moves" — so the cap is not about the market's schedule, it's about the known decay curve of your own judgment. Fee drag and the midday chop statistics make the same argument from the tape's side.
5. **Ban the recovery trade by name, in writing.** Not "avoid revenge trading" — the specific rule: after a loss, the *next* entry must be same-or-smaller size, and no position opened within N minutes of a stop-out. The documented death sequence (sweep → bigger re-entry → daily limits breached across accounts) is recoverable only by pre-commitment, because the urge it runs on feels like conviction in the moment.
6. **Make absence a decision.** The no-trade list (Tier-1 windows, compressed Mondays for aggressive plans, half-days, your post-tilt cooldown, the >½-ATR-overnight skip) converts "sit out" from willpower into procedure — and the sessions evidence backs it: sitting out is the only position with zero drawdown, and the base rates say most hours don't contain the trade you think they do.
7. **Grade the day on rules, log the state.** Two ledgers: rule-followed (did I execute the plan) and emotion-tagged journaling. The independent prop dataset's line is the mechanism — the pattern is visible in the log after the fact and invisible while it's happening — which means the only way to see it live is to have written it down for three weeks and met it coming. Money is a lagging indicator; rule-compliance is the leading one.
8. **Pre-commit the payout calendar.** Amount, cadence, day. This is the countermeasure to both funded-stage traps named in the psychology evidence: house-money sizing after a withdrawal and fear-of-denial hoarding that leaves profits in the account as the next drawdown's collateral. The withdrawal-discipline simulation (claiming roughly halves modeled ruin vs reinvesting) says the calendar is a risk control, not a cash-flow preference.
9. **Scale the cushion, not the size.** After each payout, size resets to the plan, not to the account's new balance; more funded accounts at the proven unit come before bigger contracts in one. The lateral-versus-vertical finding is in every income source this library holds — and the recovery-trade corpus explains why: extra size is extra ammunition for the exact trades that end runs.
10. **Step away on schedule, not on feeling.** A fifteen-minute break after the second loss; a planned rest day after a breach day; sleep before strategy. The clinical writeups name over-trading, revenge, and screen-staring as one syndrome, and the firms' own lockout design is institutionalized version of the same walk. The cheapest discipline tool in existence is the door.
11. **Run the pre-mortem weekly.** One page: what killed my week, was it market, floor, clock, or the last trade's feelings? The whole behavioral stack in this guide — sizing, caps, absence, calendar — exists to make that answer boring. Traders whose weekly post-mortems keep finding the same cause either changed the rule or were rationalizing; the log is what tells the difference.
12. **Budget the timeline like the studies, not like the screenshots.** Expect 9 months to two years to first consistency, 2–4 dead funded accounts on the way, and zero learning-by-osmosis (the persistence research). Then build what the evidence says actually shortens it: a journaled edge in one instrument, the twelve controls above, and patience priced into the subscription line. The people who get paid are not calmer — they simply removed the decisions that require calm.
