## What you are actually buying

A prop evaluation is not trading capital. It is a **cheap call option on your own edge**: pay $35–150 (frequently less on promo cycles), get the right to trade a virtual account whose *real* size is its drawdown, and collect 90%-ish splits if you steer it through the stage gates. The account label is marketing — a "50K account" with a $2,000 max loss is a $2,000 risk budget that prints $20-per-point P&L. The independent datasets price the option honestly: average lifetime fee spend is around $800 (≈3 attempts), and roughly one in seven challenge buyers ever sees a payout. Every tactic in this guide exists to raise the value of that option and to collect it before the floor collects you.

## The account types, and who each one is for

| Type | Structure | Buy it when | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Two-step eval** | Target 1 → target 2 → funded | you want the cheapest option ($30–60 promos, monthly discounts) | two funnels of failure before a dollar |
| **One-step eval** | single target → funded | your edge is consistent but slow | same floor physics, less fee discount |
| **Instant funding** | pay once, trade funded immediately | you refuse to spend weeks passing | a barrier-model simulation found every instant tier it studied loses money on average — tight buffers and consistency gates tax the speed you paid for (contested; vendors market the opposite) |
| **Buffer model** | eval → build your buffer → funded *at* your buffer → payouts above it | you want daily payouts eventually without a firm audit at every claim | your funded "starting balance" is your own accumulated profit — effectively paying twice |
| **Live capital** | real brokerage, real margin | you've survived the sim game long enough | the funnel's end: one firm's published figure is 0.71% of funded starters reach it |

## The stage clocks, side by side

Every firm hides a different calendar inside the same promise. Verified rule tables (re-check before purchase — these change without notice):

| Firm / plan | Min days to pass | Funded payout gate | The fine print that bites |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Topstep Combine** | 2 days, no deadline, 55% consistency | 5 winning days ≥ $150 (Standard); 3 days + 40% (Consistency) | a payout **resets your loss limit to zero** — you rebuild the cushion before money is withdrawable again |
| **Apex** | can pass in 1–2 days on promo clocks | min 5 qualifying days ≥ $250, balance floor ~$52.6K, 30% windfall consistency | safety-net buffer required before the first three payouts; intraday-trail version follows *unrealized* peaks |
| **Tradeify Growth** | **1 day**, no eval consistency | ≥5 profitable days, 35% consistency | the profitable-day count **resets after every payout** — each claim restarts the clock |
| **MyFundedFutures** | Builder 1 day; Rapid EOD 4 days + 30% | daily above buffer ($2,100 on 50K) | live-account triggers ($10K single day) can promote you mid-farm; auto-flat skipped on half-sessions |
| **Lucid** | 1-day pass possible | Flex: 5 profitable days, no buffer; Pro: buffer + ≤40% | plan family is a decision tree — read the payout doc before, not after |
| **Take Profit Trader** | 5 days in the Test | PRO: withdraw **day one** above buffer, no cap | 80/20 split — the trade for speed is 10 points of split |
| **FTMO (forex/CFD)** | 4 days per phase | first reward ~14 calendar days, then biweekly | daily limit resets at **CET midnight** — a timezone trap for Americas traders |

Two patterns repeat across every table: the **pass is cheap and the payout is gated**, and **the gates have clocks** (day counts that reset, floors that must be rebuilt, windows that open weekly). The documented play is not "pass fast" — it's knowing your firm's calendar well enough that a payout request never finds you mid-gate.

## Contract specs and caps

Micro conversion is uniform: 10 micros ≡ 1 mini, and firms count it that way against your cap. Typical 50K caps run 3–6 minis (30–60 micros), sometimes product-specific (metals carry separate caps that appeared mid-2026). Household rules vary — one major futures firm allows up to 20 funded accounts, another caps 5. Positions must be same-direction across accounts (see the traps page for why reverse-copy is an account-closing event). These caps are not annoyances; they are the reason the scaling model in the rules page is *lateral* — you cannot vertically size into a $2,000 floor even if you wanted to.

## The fee model is the tax on slow

Monthly-subscription evals (the standard on futures props) mean time costs money: a zero-edge simulation shows why the "small and patient" trader still must eventually *earn* — 14% pass odds and a 189-day median at $100/day swings is a fee graveyard. The corrected reading: size small until the edge is proven, then size to the planned-risk band and pass in weeks, because the eval budget compounds elsewhere. The fee model rewards exactly one behavior: converting disciplined practice into attempts once — not many — the journal proves expectancy.
